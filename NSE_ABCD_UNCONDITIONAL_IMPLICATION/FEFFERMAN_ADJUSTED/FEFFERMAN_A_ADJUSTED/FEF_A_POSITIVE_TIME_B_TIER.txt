THEOREM
There is a universal ε*>0 with the following property. Fix ν>0.
Let b be a classical divergence-free Schwartz field on ℝ³ with
‖b‖_{L³}<ε*ν. Let w=Sν(·)b be its unforced evolution. For τ>0 put
u₀=w(τ). Whenever u₀ belongs to the original v0.25.0 admissible class,
there exists a whole-space zero-force solution in the exact v0.25.0 sense
with viscosity ν and initial datum u₀. The same conclusion holds if u₀
is specified by a classical finite-energy history from b on [0,τ].

CONSTRUCTION
1. At viscosity one, Wang, Gao and Xue, Theorem 1.1(ii), give a global
mild velocity for sufficiently small L³ data and joint analyticity on
t>0. For viscosity ν apply that theorem to b/ν and set
w(x,s)=ν v(x,νs), p(x,s)=ν²q(x,νs). This gives the threshold ε*ν.
Local H¹ theory for Schwartz data supplies smoothness up to elapsed
time zero. On each compact interval the mild trajectory is a strong
solution and obeys the unforced energy identity, so
    ∫|w(x,s)|²dx ≤ ‖b‖₂²,    s≥0.
For this energy assertion one may first identify the small L³ trajectory
with the H¹ solution on its local interval by mild uniqueness. A putative
finite H¹ endpoint is excluded by smoothing bounds on compact positive
time intervals and the H¹ energy estimate. In detail the small-data
theorem bounds ‖w‖∞ away from zero on each such interval; testing against
−Δw gives d‖∇w‖₂²/ds ≤ Cν⁻¹‖w‖∞²‖∇w‖₂². The H¹ norm then stays bounded.
The elementary integrations by parts are valid on the strong solution's
lifespan; the resulting estimates extend to each finite elapsed time.

2. At positive elapsed times F=νΔw−∂sw−(w·∇)w is jointly real analytic
and is a spatial gradient by the NSE. The normalization
    p(x,s)=∫₀¹ x·F(λx,s) dλ
has ∇p=F. It is jointly analytic locally: along each compact integration
segment the analytic integrand has uniform local factorial bounds,
by a finite cover, which survive integration. At s=0 the smooth pressure
from Schwartz local theory can be given the same normalization. For
the original ambient time derivative at zero one may extend velocity
to s<0 by b+s∂s⁺w(·,0); no equation is required there.

3. For every t≥0 put
    u(x,t)=w(x,t+τ),       π(x,t)=p(x,t+τ).
The time derivative at t equals the ancestor's derivative at t+τ.
All spatial derivatives are unchanged at the corresponding point.
Thus u solves the same unforced NSE at viscosity ν, is divergence-free
and has trace u₀. The shift maps the closed half-space inside s>0.
Consequently u and π are jointly real analytic within t≥0. Every
velocity slice is square integrable and satisfies the single strict bound
    ∫|u(x,t)|²dx < 1+‖b‖₂².
These are precisely clauses 01, 02, 03, 06 and 07. Admissibility of the
snapshot, including its rapid spatial decay, is retained as an input.

4. If an earlier segment [0,τ] is specified instead of w itself, any
smooth finite-energy unforced history there with datum b has the same
velocity. Tao, Corollary 11.1, upgrades it to L∞H¹ ∩ L²H², and Lemma
4.1(i) identifies the pressure gradient. Subtracting the strong solution
gives, with d the difference,
    (1/2) d‖d‖₂²/dt + ν‖∇d‖₂² ≤ ‖∇w‖∞‖d‖₂².
Both traces are b and the coefficient is integrable on [0,τ]. Grönwall
gives equality, including at τ. Steps 1–4 supply SeedExtension ε*
mathematically. This construction uses published analytic theorems.

SCOPE
Strict τ>0 is essential for this argument. The ancestor b need only be
classically smooth, whereas u₀ and (u,π) use the original analytic-order
predicates. The restriction acts on initial states. It does not assert
the unrestricted Fefferman A proposition. The zero field supplies a
member for all ν and τ; no assertion about rapid decay of all snapshots
is used.

For t in [0,T], either t=0 or t>0. In the first case the supplied equalities
give u(0)=v(0). In the second case LE090 gives u(t)=v(t). Therefore
  u(t)=v(t) for every t in [0,T].

A strong right trace does not determine a separately assigned value of a
total-real function at zero. The two zero-value assumptions are therefore
retained in the exact theorem; no endpoint value is silently inferred.

[LE092] eq_on_nonnegative_of_ae_relative_energy

The complete inputs now are

  G1: g is continuous on (0,infinity);
  G2: g(t)>=0 for every t>0;
  G3: for every T>0, g is genuinely interval integrable from 0 to T;
  G4: for every T>0, g*e is genuinely interval integrable from 0 to T;
  G5: for every T>0, Good holds a.e. on (0,T);
  G6: u and v have the common strong right trace a0;
  G7: u(0)=a0 and v(0)=a0;
  G8: for every s>0 with Good(s), and every t>=s,
        e(t)<=e(s)+integral_s^t g(r)*e(r)dr.

For an arbitrary fixed t>=0, choose T=t+1>0. Restrict G1-G2 to (0,T],
use G3-G5 at this T, retain G6-G7, and restrict G8 to starts s in (0,T)
and upper endpoints in [s,T]. These are all LE091 premises. Since t is
in [0,T], its conclusion gives u(t)=v(t). Thus
  for every real t>=0, u(t)=v(t).

No global-in-time L1 coefficient bound or limit T->infinity is needed.
Every integrability and a.e. hypothesis remains a genuine finite-horizon
condition. G8 retains every upper endpoint after a good positive start.

[LE093] restricted_class_eq_singleton_of_eq_on_nonnegative

Let V now be any type, C a set of total-real functions R->V, and v:R->V.
Assume exactly

  S1: v in C;
  S2: for every u in C and every real t>=0, u(t)=v(t).

Let Rplus={t:R | 0<=t}, represented by the subtype {t:R // 0<=t}, and
let restriction(u)(t)=u(t.val). The exact conclusion is

  restriction '' C = {restriction(v)}.

If f belongs to the left side, choose u in C with f=restriction(u).
For every t in Rplus, its nonnegativity property and S2 give
u(t.val)=v(t.val). Function extensionality yields f=restriction(v), so f
belongs to the right side. Conversely, S1 supplies the actual reference
v in C, whose restriction belongs to the left side. This proves both
inclusions and the equality of sets.

S1 supplies nonemptiness; an at-most-one conclusion alone cannot yield the
singleton. No claim C={v} for total-real functions follows, since negative
time values remain auxiliary. In a physical application, C must be the
actual admissible class and S1 must be independently constructed. For each
u in C, the actual G1-G8 data must discharge LE092 to supply S2. LE093 is
not an unconditional theorem of Leray-Hopf uniqueness.

[01] OBJECTS, NORMS, AND SEMANTICS

Domain:
  Omega = R^3, with Lebesgue measure dx.

Parameters:
  nu > 0, constant in time; f = 0.

Spaces:
  H := L^2_sigma(R^3)
     = {a in L^2(R^3;R^3) : div a = 0 in distributions}.
  V := H^1(R^3;R^3) intersect H.

Norms:
  ||v||_2^2       := sum_i int_R^3 |v_i(x)|^2 dx.
  ||grad v||_2^2  := sum_{i,j} int_R^3 |partial_j v_i(x)|^2 dx.
  ||Delta v||_2^2 := sum_i int_R^3 |Delta v_i(x)|^2 dx.
  ||v||_infty     := ess sup_x |v(x)|, with Euclidean vector norm.

Fourier convention:
  vhat(xi) := (2*pi)^(-3/2) int_R^3 exp(-i*x.xi) v(x) dx.
  ||v||_{dot H^s}^2 := int_R^3 |xi|^(2*s) |vhat(xi)|^2 dxi.

Equation NSE_nu(u,p):
  partial_t u + (u.grad)u + grad p = nu*Delta u,
  div u = 0.

LH_nu(a) denotes VELOCITY trajectories, with equality in L^2 at each time.
It does not denote arbitrary velocity-pressure pairs.

Definition LH_nu(a):
  u in C_w([0,infty);H)
       intersect L^infty(0,infty;L^2)
       intersect L^2(0,infty;dot H^1),
  u(0)=a,
  lim_{t downarrow 0} ||u(t)-a||_2 = 0,
  distributional incompressible NSE with f=0,
  and the standard strong energy inequality:

  ||u(t)||_2^2 + 2*nu*int_s^t ||grad u(r)||_2^2 dr <= ||u(s)||_2^2

  for s=0, for almost every s>0, and for every t>=s.

The distributional velocity equation is equivalently:
  int_0^infty int_R^3
    [u.partial_t phi + (u tensor u):grad phi - nu*grad u:grad phi] dx dt
  + int_R^3 a.phi(0) dx = 0
  for every smooth compactly supported solenoidal test phi on [0,infty)xR^3.

If an imported convention initially supplies only almost every upper endpoint
t, weak L^2 continuity and weak lower semicontinuity extend the energy
inequality to every upper endpoint. The representative above is fixed.

For every t>=0, set E(t):=||u(t)||_2^2.
At the almost-everywhere times with u(t) in V, set
  D(t) := ||grad u(t)||_2^2.
Assign D=0 on the remaining null set; this does not change any integral.

For initial datum a, set
  M := ||a||_2,
  E_0 := M^2,
  K_0 := M^2/2.
Thus E is the squared L^2 norm and K=E/2 is kinetic energy.

Smoothness of an L^2-valued trajectory means existence of a smooth spacetime
representative agreeing with that trajectory in L^2 at every relevant time.
It does not quantify over arbitrary pointwise modifications on null sets.

[02] EXACT FROZEN ROOT THEOREM

  T_QFA(a,nu) := M^4/(16*pi^2*nu^5)
               = K_0^2/(4*pi^2*nu^5).

THEOREM QUASI_FEFFERMAN_A.

  For every constant nu>0 and every a in H:

  (Q0) LH_nu(a) is nonempty.

  (Q1) For every u in LH_nu(a):
         lim_{t downarrow 0} ||u(t)-a||_2 = 0.

  (Q2) For every u in LH_nu(a) and every delta>T_QFA(a,nu),
       there exist smooth representatives u,p on [delta,infty)xR^3
       satisfying NSE_nu(u,p).

  (Q3) For every such u and delta:
         LH_nu(u(delta)) = {tau |-> u(delta+tau)}.

Equivalent combined quantifiers:

  forall nu>0, forall a in H,
    LH_nu(a) != empty
    and forall u in LH_nu(a),
      [lim_{t downarrow 0} ||u(t)-a||_2=0
       and forall delta>T_QFA(a,nu),
         (exists p, (u,p) have smooth representatives on [delta,infty)xR^3
          and NSE_nu(u,p) on that tail
          and LH_nu(u(delta))={tau |-> u(delta+tau)})].

Pressure gauge on every regular tail:
  p(t) := sum_{i,j=1}^3 R_i R_j(u_i(t)*u_j(t)),
  where R_i has multiplier -i*xi_i/|xi|.

No mean-zero hypothesis is imposed on H.
No smallness hypothesis is imposed on M.
Uniqueness from the original datum a is neither a premise nor a conclusion.
Existence of two distinct trajectories from a is not asserted.

[03] ESTABLISHED CLASSICAL INPUTS

C01. LERAY-HOPF EXISTENCE ON R^3.
  nu>0 and a in H imply existence of u in LH_nu(a), with the strong initial
  L^2 trace and the energy inequality specified in [01].
  References: [R1], [R2, Theorem 4.7 and Corollary 4.6].

C02. LOCAL H^1 SOLVABILITY AND CONTINUATION.
  nu>0, b in V, and initial time s imply a maximal strong solution v with
  lifetime T_max>s, such that for every s<R<T_max,
    v in C([s,R];V) intersect L^2(s,R;H^2),
    partial_t v in L^2(s,R;L^2),
    v(s)=b,
  and finite T_max forces unbounded H^1 norm as t approaches T_max.
  References: [R1], [R2, Theorem 3.17 and Corollary 3.12].
  The displayed H^1 energy-space formulation is the standard refinement of
  the semi-strong existence formulation in R2. On smooth approximants,
  testing against -Delta v and applying Agmon and Young yields
    D_v' + nu*||Delta v||_2^2 <= C*nu^(-3)*D_v^3
  for a universal finite C. The local scalar bound controls D_v and the
  integral of ||Delta v||_2^2; the equation gives partial_t v in L^2(L^2).
  The Hilbert-triple continuity theorem supplies C(H^1). Uniform local
  existence time on bounded H^1 sets supplies the continuation alternative.

C03. STRONG-SOLUTION IDENTITIES AND SMOOTHING.
  The solutions of C02 satisfy the energy and enstrophy identities, and are
  smooth for t>s. On compact subintervals of (s,T_max), spatial Sobolev
  regularity of every order and the corresponding time derivatives hold.
  References: [R2, Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3, Theorem 3.4].

C04. RELATIVE-ENERGY TESTING.
  A Leray-Hopf velocity can be compared with a strong solution sharing its
  initial trace. Smooth solenoidal approximation in the weak formulation
  justifies the relative-energy inequality used explicitly in [10].
  Reference: [R2, Lemma 4.9]; the estimate and Gronwall step are given in [10].

Viscosity normalization for sources stated with nu=1:
  v(t,x)=nu*U(nu*(t-s),x),
  p_v(t,x)=nu^2*P(nu*(t-s),x),
  U(0,x)=b(x)/nu.
  NSE_1(U,P) implies NSE_nu(v,p_v).

Additional standard analysis used below:
  Plancherel; weighted Cauchy-Schwarz; the fundamental theorem for absolutely
  continuous functions; the chain rule for their squares; monotone convergence;
  Sobolev embedding H^1(R^3) into L^6; Hilbert-space weak convergence plus norm
  convergence; density of smooth solenoidal test fields; integral Gronwall.

[04] LEMMA QFA_GOOD_RESTART_TIMES

CLAIM.
  For every u in LH_nu(a), there exists a measurable full-measure set
  G_u subset (0,infty) such that for every s in G_u:
    u(s) in V;
    the energy inequality begins at s;
    u(s+h) -> u(s) strongly in L^2 as h downarrow 0;
    u_s(tau):=u(s+tau) belongs to LH_nu(u(s)).

PROOF.
  The H^1-time set has full measure by the energy class.
  The admissible energy-start set has full measure by the strong energy
  inequality. Intersect these sets, using a countable exhaustion by finite
  intervals if necessary.

  Fix s in that intersection. For every h>0,
    E(s+h) + 2*nu*int_s^(s+h) D(r) dr <= E(s),
  whence
    limsup_{h downarrow 0} ||u(s+h)||_2^2 <= ||u(s)||_2^2.
  Weak continuity gives u(s+h) weakly -> u(s). Therefore the norms converge
  and Hilbert-space uniform convexity gives strong L^2 convergence.

  Time translation preserves distributional NSE and the energy classes.
  The energy inequality at time zero for u_s is the inequality beginning at s;
  the remaining admissible starts are translations of a full-measure set.
  Thus u_s in LH_nu(u(s)). QED.

[05] LEMMA QFA_ENERGY_PRODUCT_BUDGET

CLAIM.
  For every u in LH_nu(a) and every R>0,

    int_0^R E(t)*D(t) dt
      <= [M^4-F(R)^2]/(4*nu)
      <= M^4/(4*nu),

  where F(t):=M^2-2*nu*int_0^t D(r) dr.
  Consequently int_0^infty E*D <= M^4/(4*nu).

PROOF.
  The energy inequality beginning at zero yields
    0 <= E(t) <= F(t) <= M^2.
  Since D is nonnegative and locally integrable,
    F in AC([0,R]),
    F(0)=M^2,
    F'(t)=-2*nu*D(t) almost everywhere.
  In particular E*D is integrable on (0,R).

  Multiplication by D>=0 and the AC square chain rule yield
    int_0^R E*D
      <= int_0^R F*D
       = -(1/(2*nu))*int_0^R F*F'
       = [F(0)^2-F(R)^2]/(4*nu)
       = [M^4-F(R)^2]/(4*nu)
      <= M^4/(4*nu).

  Apply monotone convergence as R increases to infinity. QED.

Hypothesis audit:
  E is not differentiated.
  Energy equality before regularity is not used.
  The limit E(t)->0 is not used.
  A Poincare inequality or spectral gap is not used.

COROLLARY QFA_HHALF_QUARTIC_BUDGET.
  For almost every t, Fourier Cauchy-Schwarz gives
    ||u(t)||_{dot H^(1/2)}^2
      = int |xi|*|uhat(t,xi)|^2 dxi
      <= ||u(t)||_2*||grad u(t)||_2.
  Therefore
    int_0^infty ||u(t)||_{dot H^(1/2)}^4 dt
      <= int_0^infty E(t)*D(t) dt
      <= M^4/(4*nu). QED.

[06] LEMMA QFA_AGMON_R3

CLAIM.
  For every vector field v in H^2(R^3;R^3),
    ||v||_infty^2 <= ||grad v||_2*||Delta v||_2/(2*pi).

PROOF.
  For lambda>0, define
    w_lambda(xi):=|xi|^2+lambda^2*|xi|^4.
  A spherical-coordinate calculation gives
    (2*pi)^(-3)*int_R^3 w_lambda(xi)^(-1) dxi
      = (2*pi)^(-3)*4*pi*int_0^infty dr/(1+lambda^2*r^2)
      = 1/(4*pi*lambda).

  Fourier inversion, the Euclidean vector triangle inequality, and weighted
  Cauchy-Schwarz give
    ||v||_infty^2
      <= [||grad v||_2^2+lambda^2*||Delta v||_2^2]/(4*pi*lambda).

  Put G:=||grad v||_2 and H2:=||Delta v||_2.
  If G>0 and H2>0, choose lambda=G/H2 to obtain G*H2/(2*pi).
  If G=0 or H2=0, Plancherel implies vhat=0 almost everywhere off {0};
  since vhat is an L^2 function, v=0, and the estimate is immediate. QED.

The vector norm introduces no factor 3.
Independent literature anchor: [R3, Theorem 1 and its vector-valued extension].

[07] LEMMA QFA_NONLINEAR_ENSTROPHY_BOUND

CLAIM.
  For every solenoidal v in H^2(R^3;R^3), with E_v=||v||_2^2 and
  D_v=||grad v||_2^2,

    | <(v.grad)v, Delta v> |
      <= (2*pi)^(-1/2)*(E_v*D_v)^(1/4)*||Delta v||_2^2.

PROOF.
  Write G:=||grad v||_2 and H2:=||Delta v||_2.
  Fourier Cauchy-Schwarz gives G^2 <= ||v||_2*H2.
  By [06] and Holder,
    | <(v.grad)v, Delta v> |
      <= ||v||_infty*G*H2
      <= (2*pi)^(-1/2)*G^(3/2)*H2^(3/2)
      <= (2*pi)^(-1/2)*(||v||_2*G)^(1/2)*H2^2
       = (2*pi)^(-1/2)*(E_v*D_v)^(1/4)*H2^2.
  Zero-norm cases follow directly. QED.

For a local strong solution v supplied by C02, C03 gives almost everywhere:
  E_v' = -2*nu*D_v,
  (1/2)*D_v' + nu*||Delta v||_2^2
    = <(v.grad)v, Delta v>.
Hence

  (1/2)*D_v'
    + [nu-(2*pi)^(-1/2)*(E_v*D_v)^(1/4)]*||Delta v||_2^2 <= 0.    (7.1)

The identities are justified first on regular subintervals and extend to the
initial H^1 time by the strong-solution energy identities and H^1 continuity.

[08] THEOREM QFA_SMALL_PRODUCT_GLOBAL_STRONG

CLAIM.
  If b in V and ||b||_2*||grad b||_2 < 2*pi*nu^2, then there is a global
  strong solution v starting from b at time s. Moreover,
    v in C([s,infty);V),
    v is smooth on (s,infty)xR^3.

PROOF.
  Let v be the maximal strong solution from C02.
  Set
    E_s:=||b||_2^2,
    D_s:=||grad b||_2^2,
    q_s:=E_s*D_s,
    alpha:=4*pi^2*nu^4,
    c:=(2*pi)^(-1/2).
  The hypothesis is exactly q_s<alpha.

  Choose beta=(q_s+alpha)/2, so q_s<beta<alpha.
  By continuity, E_v(t)*D_v(t)<beta on a right neighborhood of s.
  On every interval where this remains true, (7.1) implies
    D_v'<=0,
  while E_v'=-2*nu*D_v<=0.
  Therefore E_v(t)*D_v(t)<=q_s<beta on that interval.
  A first crossing of beta is impossible by continuity.

  Consequently, throughout the maximal interval,
    E_v(t)<=E_s,
    D_v(t)<=D_s,
    E_v(t)*D_v(t)<=q_s<alpha.

  Define the fixed positive dissipation margin
    eta_s:=nu-c*q_s^(1/4)>0.
  Integrating (7.1) gives
    D_v(t)+2*eta_s*int_s^t ||Delta v(r)||_2^2 dr <= D_s.          (8.1)

  In particular ||v(t)||_{H^1}^2<=E_s+D_s uniformly before T_max.
  The continuation alternative C02 excludes finite T_max.
  Thus T_max=infty. Smoothing follows from C03. QED.

Endpoint signature:
  b in H^1_sigma(R^3)
  and ||b||_2*||grad b||_2<2*pi*nu^2
    ==> GlobalStrong_nu(b).

[09] LEMMA QFA_RESTART_BEFORE_CUTOFF

CLAIM.
  For every u in LH_nu(a) and delta>T_QFA(a,nu), there exists
  s in (0,delta) intersect G_u such that
    E(s)*D(s)<4*pi^2*nu^4.

PROOF.
  Put alpha:=4*pi^2*nu^4. Since nu>0,
    delta>M^4/(16*pi^2*nu^5)
      <==> alpha*delta>M^4/(4*nu).
  By [05],
    int_0^delta E*D <= M^4/(4*nu) < alpha*delta.

  Define S_delta:={s in (0,delta):E(s)*D(s)<alpha}.
  If S_delta had measure zero, E*D>=alpha almost everywhere on (0,delta),
  implying int_0^delta E*D>=alpha*delta, a contradiction.
  Thus S_delta has positive measure.

  G_u has full measure by [04]. Choose s in S_delta intersect G_u.
  Then b:=u(s) belongs to V, u_s belongs to LH_nu(b), and
    ||b||_2*||grad b||_2<2*pi*nu^2.
  Apply [08]. QED.

[10] THEOREM QFA_WEAK_STRONG_COMPARISON

CLAIM.
  Let v be the global strong solution constructed in [08], starting from b
  at time s. Every Leray-Hopf velocity U with the same strong trace b at s
  agrees with v for every t>=s in L^2.

PROOF OF THE INTEGRABILITY ENDPOINT.
  Fix any finite R>s. By [06], [08], and Cauchy-Schwarz in time,

    int_s^R ||v(t)||_infty^2 dt
      <= (1/(2*pi))*int_s^R sqrt(D_v(t))*||Delta v(t)||_2 dt
      <= (sqrt(D_s)/(2*pi))*sqrt(R-s)
           *(int_s^R ||Delta v(t)||_2^2 dt)^(1/2)
      <= (D_s/(2*pi))*sqrt((R-s)/(2*eta_s))
       < infty.                                                (10.1)

PROOF OF COMPARISON.
  Set w:=U-v and Y(t):=||w(t)||_2^2.
  C04 combines the weak energy inequality for U, the strong energy equality
  for v, and the cross-tested momentum equations. Divergence-free integration
  by parts changes the nonlinear difference term to a term bounded by
    ||v||_infty*||w||_2*||grad w||_2.

  Young's inequality gives
    2*||v||_infty*||w||_2*||grad w||_2
      <= nu*||grad w||_2^2 + nu^(-1)*||v||_infty^2*Y.
  Therefore

    Y(t)+nu*int_s^t ||grad w(r)||_2^2 dr
      <= Y(s)+nu^(-1)*int_s^t ||v(r)||_infty^2*Y(r) dr.           (10.2)

  The shared strong trace implies Y(s)=0. By (10.1), the Gronwall coefficient
  is integrable on every finite [s,R]. Integral Gronwall yields Y=0 almost
  everywhere on [s,R]. Weak L^2 continuity upgrades equality to every time.
  Since R>s is arbitrary, U(t)=v(t) for all t>=s. QED.

No integrability of ||v||_infty^2 over the entire infinite interval is required.
No uniqueness of LH_nu(a) before s is used.

[11] LEMMA QFA_SMOOTH_TAIL_AND_PRESSURE

CLAIM.
  For every u in LH_nu(a) and delta>T_QFA(a,nu), the velocity trajectory
  admits smooth velocity and pressure representatives on [delta,infty)xR^3.

PROOF.
  Select s<delta by [09]. Construct v by [08].
  The translated u belongs to LH_nu(u(s)) by [04].
  Apply [10] to identify u(t)=v(t) in L^2 for every t>=s.

  C03 yields positive-time smoothness on the open interval (s,infty).
  Since s<delta, this includes a temporal neighborhood of every point with
  time coordinate in [delta,infty).

  Define
    p(t):=sum_{i,j} R_i R_j(v_i(t)*v_j(t)).
  H^1 embeds in L^4 by interpolation between L^2 and L^6, so v_i*v_j in L^2.
  The multiplier -xi_i*xi_j/|xi|^2 is bounded; hence p(t) is in L^2.

  In distributions,
    -Delta p = sum_{i,j} partial_i partial_j(v_i*v_j).
  The projected NSE and this pressure formula give the full NSE.
  On compact time intervals within (s,infty), C03 supplies Sobolev regularity
  of all orders; products in H^m for m>3/2 and bounded order-zero Fourier
  multipliers transfer this regularity to p. The same argument applies to
  all time derivatives. Sobolev embedding yields joint spacetime smoothness.

  A nonzero spatial constant does not lie in L^2(R^3), so this normalization
  fixes the spatially constant pressure freedom. QED.

CONSEQUENCE: TAIL ENERGY EQUALITY.
  For every t>=r>=delta,
    ||u(t)||_2^2+2*nu*int_r^t ||grad u(q)||_2^2 dq=||u(r)||_2^2.

[12] THEOREM QFA_UNIQUE_POSTCUTOFF_CONTINUATION

CLAIM.
  Under the hypotheses of [11],
    LH_nu(u(delta))={tau |-> u(delta+tau)}.

PROOF.
  Let z(tau):=u(delta+tau), tau>=0.
  Strong continuity, NSE, and tail energy equality imply
    z in LH_nu(u(delta)).

  Let W be any other element of LH_nu(u(delta)).
  The known z is strong and smooth, and satisfies the finite-interval
  L^2_time L^infty_space bound obtained by restricting (10.1).
  Apply (10.2) and Gronwall to W and z, now with initial time zero.
  Thus W(tau)=z(tau) in L^2 for every tau>=0. QED.

[00] CARRIERS, INTEGRALS, AND TIME DOMAIN

X = EuclideanSpace R (Fin 3), with full spatial Lebesgue measure dx.
Y = EuclideanSpace C (Fin 3), regarded also as a real normed vector space.
H_R = Lp X 2 (volume : Measure X) = L2(R3;R3).
H_C = Lp Y 2 (volume : Measure X) = L2(R3;C3).
No periodic quotient, unit-cube restriction, normalized volume, or finite
total spatial volume is imposed.

The actual candidate names are
  QFA.Representation.RealSpatialL2 = H_R;
  QFA.Endpoints.Representation.RealL2 = H_R;
  QFA.Endpoints.Agmon.ComplexL2 = H_C.
The two real abbreviations describe the same carrier. Spatial L2 elements
are equivalence classes; identities of selected pointwise representatives
are asserted almost everywhere unless stated otherwise.

For v in H_R and G : Fin 3 -> H_R, define
  E_R(v) = ||v||_2^2;
  D_R(G) = sum_(j:Fin 3) ||G_j||_2^2.
The corresponding complex energies are defined by the same formulas.
For a supplied L in H_C, Z_C(L)=||L||_2^2. The sum in D_R is the Frobenius
gradient energy if the supplied G_j are actual directional derivatives.
The definitions themselves do not assert any derivative relation.

Time measure dt is one-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Ioo(a,b)=(a,b),
Ioc(a,b)=(a,b], Icc(a,b)=[a,b], and Ici(0)=[0,infinity).
In physical applications, trajectory, energy-start, and trace conclusions
concern nonnegative time. Generic translation identities retain arbitrary
real parameters where explicitly stated. Total-real physical trajectories
supply only an auxiliary extension outside the nonnegative domain. No
negative-time identification is concluded.

The notation integral_a^b denotes the oriented interval Bochner integral.
For signatures with no integrability premise, it has Lean's total convention:
an integral of a nonintegrable function is zero. Statements explicitly
identified below as total-integral identities retain that exact scope.
Every field-level energy integral and product integral used in the main
theorem is genuinely integrable; Sections 04 and 07 discharge this fact.

[01] EXACT FIELD-LEVEL PREMISES AND COMPOSED THEOREM

Let nu,delta be real, a in H_R, u:R->H_R, G:R->(Fin 3->H_R), and
Good:R->Prop. Write
  E(t)=E_R(u(t)), D(t)=D_R(G(t)), M=||a||_2,
  T_QFA=M^4/(16*pi^2*nu^5), kappa=4*pi^2*nu^4.

EnergyProfile(nu,a,u,G) is precisely the conjunction:

  (P1) For every finite R>0, u is a.e. strongly measurable on (0,R)
       as an H_R-valued function.

  (P2) For every finite R>0 and every j:Fin 3,
       t |-> G(t,j) belongs to time L2((0,R);H_R).

  (P3) For every t>=0,
       E(t)+2*nu*integral_0^t D(r)dr <= M^2.

This predicate is exactly QFA.Representation.EnergyProfile. In particular,
P3 has every nonnegative upper endpoint. No global time-L2 norm is required.
The parameter a supplies the energy bound. The predicate alone does not
identify a with u(0), impose an initial trace, or define PDE admissibility.

THEOREM [LE073]:
  QFA.Endpoints.Composition.exists_subcritical_good_time_with_strong_trace.

Assume P1-P3 and

  (P4) nu>0 and delta>T_QFA;

  (P5) For every b in H_R, the real pairing
       t |-> <u(t),b>_R is continuous on [0,infinity);

  (P6) For almost every s in (0,delta),
       Good(s) AND
       [for every r in R with s<=r,
          E(r)+2*nu*integral_s^r D(q)dq <= E(s)].

Then there exists s satisfying all of

  0<s<delta;
  Good(s);
  E(s)*D(s)<4*pi^2*nu^4;
  for every r>=s,
    E(r)+2*nu*integral_s^r D(q)dq <= E(s);
  lim_(t->s, t>s) ||u(t)-u(s)||_2 = 0;
  lim_(tau->0, tau>0) ||u(tau+s)-u(s)||_2 = 0.

Good is an arbitrary predicate with the displayed a.e. premise; no
measurability of its truth set is added. The witness s may depend on every
supplied trajectory datum and on delta. Neither uniqueness nor actual
nonuniqueness of trajectories is a premise or a conclusion. The conclusion
is a subcritical norm-product time with its energy start and strong traces.
No H1 membership follows unless the supplied G_j are independently identified
as weak derivatives of u at that time.

[02] COMPONENTWISE COMPLEXIFICATION AND ITS SPATIAL L2 LIFT

Location: D02.01-D02.02 / AD01.
Endpoints: LE031-LE046.

Define J:X->Y by J(v)_j=(v_j:C). The scalar real linear isometry
[EP001] Complex.ofRealLI supplies additivity and real homogeneity in each
coordinate. Coordinate extensionality, PiLp.ext, gives these properties for J.
Using [EP002] EuclideanSpace.norm_sq_eq on both Euclidean spaces,

  ||J(v)||^2 = sum_j |(v_j:C)|^2 = sum_j |v_j|^2 = ||v||^2.

Both norms are nonnegative; hence ||J(v)||=||v||. Also J(0)=0, and linearity
applied to v-w proves ||J(v)-J(w)||=||v-w||. Therefore J is an actual real
linear isometry, LE031 complexifyVectorLI. LE032-LE036 record its coordinate
formula, zero preservation, norm, squared norm, and isometry respectively.
There is no dimension-dependent multiplier.

The isometry is 1-Lipschitz. Apply [EP003] LipschitzWith.compLp with J, p=2,
J(0)=0, and the full spatial volume. This defines J2:H_R->H_C.
[EP004] LipschitzWith.coeFn_compLp gives
  J2(v)(x)=J(v(x)) for almost every x.                         [LE037]
Thus the representative norms agree a.e. Applying [EP005]
MeasureTheory.Lp.norm_le_norm_of_ae_le in both directions yields
  ||J2(v)||_2=||v||_2.                                       [LE038]
No finite-volume hypothesis is needed for this lift.

The a.e. representative identities [EP117] MeasureTheory.Lp.coeFn_add and
[EP118] MeasureTheory.Lp.coeFn_smul, together with J's linearity, give
  J2(v+w)=J2(v)+J2(w), J2(c*v)=c*J2(v) for c in R.           [LE039-LE040]
Equality in Lp follows from MeasureTheory.Lp.ext. Bundling these results
gives LE041 complexifyL2LI, a real linear isometry. It yields the isometry
and zero conclusions LE042-LE043.

Squaring LE038 and summing a finite family yields exactly
  E_C(J2(v))=||v||_2^2;                                     [LE044]
  D_C(j |-> J2(G_j))=sum_j ||G_j||_2^2;                      [LE045]
  Z_C(J2(L))=||L||_2^2 for each supplied L in H_R.            [LE046]
The last formula is norm preservation for a supplied field; identification
of L with an actual Laplacian remains a separate premise.

[03] COMPACT REAL TESTS AND ACTUAL WEAK-DERIVATIVE TRANSPORT

Location: D02.03 / AD01.
Endpoints: LE047-LE050.

Let F be a normed real vector space, v in L2(X;F), and psi:X->R continuous
with compact support. Let K be the compact closure of its support. Spatial
Lebesgue measure is finite on K. The selected Lp representative is a.e.
strongly measurable, and Holder's inequality gives

  integral_K ||v|| <= volume(K)^(1/2)*(integral_K ||v||^2)^(1/2) < infinity.

The continuous function psi is bounded on K and vanishes off K. Consequently
psi*v is a.e. strongly measurable and
  integral ||psi*v|| <= sup_K |psi| * integral_K ||v|| < infinity.
This proves actual integrability, including for the incomplete target spaces
allowed by LE047 integrable_compact_test_smul. The source composition is
[EP051] MeasureTheory.MemLp.locallyIntegrable at p=2, followed by
[EP119] MeasureTheory.LocallyIntegrable.integrable_smul_left_of_hasCompactSupport.

Let v in H_R and suppose psi*v is integrable, with no compactness assumption
needed for this intermediate implication. By LE037 and real linearity,
  psi(x)*J2(v)(x)=J(psi(x)*v(x)) a.e.
J is a continuous real linear map. Its image of an integrable function is
integrable. Both X and Y are complete real normed spaces, so [EP006]
ContinuousLinearMap.integral_comp_comm and integral_congr_ae give

  integral psi*J2(v) = J(integral psi*v).                     [LE048]

If v,G in H_R and both dpsi*v and psi*G are integrable, the real identity
  integral dpsi*v = -integral psi*G
therefore implies
  integral dpsi*J2(v) = -integral psi*J2(G).                  [LE049]
The minus sign is preserved by the real linear map J.

For a complete normed real vector target F, the semantic predicate
RealTestWeakDirectionalDerivative(v,G,e), e in X, means

  for every real psi in C_c^infinity(X),
    integral (Dpsi(x)[e])*v(x) dx = -integral psi(x)*G(x) dx.

For such psi, x |-> Dpsi(x)[e] is continuous. Off the compact closure of
psi's support, psi vanishes in a neighborhood, so its derivative vanishes;
the derivative test therefore has compact support. The exact supporting
declarations are [EP120] ContDiff.continuous_fderiv, [EP122]
Continuous.clm_apply, and [EP121] HasCompactSupport.fderiv_apply. LE047 supplies
integrability of both test products, and LE049 then proves

  RealTestWeakDirectionalDerivative(v,G,e)
    -> RealTestWeakDirectionalDerivative(J2(v),J2(G),e).     [LE050]

Thus the actual compact-real-test derivative identity is transported without
an extra integrability assumption in LE050. Each further supplied derivative
witness can be transported by repetition. Derivative existence, Laplacian
assembly, extension to complex tests, and compact-to-Schwartz extension are
not supplied by this implication. In particular, AD08 remains separate.

[04] CONCRETE FIELD DATA TO THE SCALAR ENVELOPE AND PRODUCT BUDGET

Location: D02.05 -> D04.01-D04.06.
Endpoints: LE051-LE058; existing scalar Energy endpoints are reused.

Assume P1-P3. For every real t,
  E(t)>=0, D(t)>=0,
by nonnegativity of a square and finite summation. These are LE051-LE052.

Fix R>0. The norm is continuous; squaring is continuous. Applying [EP123]
MeasureTheory.AEStronglyMeasurable.norm and [EP124] .pow to P1 proves scalar a.e.
strong measurability of E on (0,R).                           [LE053]
For each j, P2 and [EP125] MeasureTheory.MemLp.integrable_norm_pow at the natural
exponent p=2 give integrability of t |-> ||G(t,j)||_2^2. The finite-sum lemma
[EP126] MeasureTheory.integrable_finsetSum gives D in L1((0,R)). [LE054]

Lebesgue singletons have measure zero. [EP011]
MeasureTheory.restrict_Ioo_eq_restrict_Ioc identifies the restricted measures
on (0,R) and (0,R]. [EP012]
intervalIntegrable_iff_integrableOn_Ioc_of_le then gives
  IntervalIntegrable D volume 0 R.                           [LE055]
The same restriction argument gives genuine integrability on every finite
[s,r] with 0<=s<=r: choose a larger positive horizon and restrict to [s,r].
The interval [0,0] has zero measure. Hence every P3 and P6 energy integral
has genuine integrability for the field application.

Set M=||a||_2, with M allowed to be zero, and
  F(t)=M^2-2*nu*integral_0^t D.
Rearranging P3 gives E(t)<=F(t) at every t>=0.                [LE056]
[EP127] MeasureTheory.ae_restrict_mem supplies t in (0,R) a.e.; thus the same bound
holds a.e. on (0,R), with no new continuity premise on E.     [LE057]

Now assume nu>0. The remaining argument is the exact scalar product-budget
derivation of QFA.Energy.product_budget_Ioo, with E,D as above and M=||a||_2.

  (a) By [EP013] IntervalIntegrable.absolutelyContinuousOnInterval_intervalIntegral,
      the primitive integral_0^t D is absolutely continuous on [0,R].
      Constant functions, [EP014] ContDiffOn.absolutelyContinuousOnInterval,
      [EP015] AbsolutelyContinuousOnInterval.const_mul, and [EP016]
      AbsolutelyContinuousOnInterval.sub make F absolutely continuous.

  (b) [EP017] IntervalIntegrable.ae_hasDerivAt_integral, with basepoint 0,
      and [EP018-EP020] HasDerivAt.const_mul, .const_sub, .deriv yield
        F'(t)=-2*nu*D(t) a.e. on [0,R], F(0)=M^2.
      Differentiability of the weak energy E is never used.

  (c) [EP021] AbsolutelyContinuousOnInterval.continuousOn makes F bounded
      on the compact interval. [EP022] IntervalIntegrable.continuousOn_mul
      gives integrability of F*D. The product E*D is a.e. strongly measurable,
      and 0<=E*D<=F*D a.e. [EP023] MeasureTheory.Integrable.mono_nonneg
      therefore gives genuine integrability of E*D before integral comparison.

  (d) Apply [EP024] AbsolutelyContinuousOnInterval.integral_deriv_mul_eq_sub
      with both factors equal to F. [EP025]
      intervalIntegral.integral_congr_ae substitutes F'=-2*nu*D:
        F(R)^2-M^4 = integral_0^R (F'*F+F*F')
                   = -4*nu*integral_0^R F*D.
      Since nu>0,
        integral_0^R F*D = (M^4-F(R)^2)/(4*nu).

  (e) [EP026] MeasureTheory.integral_mono_ae, [EP027]
      intervalIntegral.integral_of_le, and the null endpoint conversion give
        integral_(0,R) E*D
          <= (M^4-F(R)^2)/(4*nu)
          <= M^4/(4*nu).

This proves LE058 EnergyProfile.energy_product_budget_Ioo, including its
Integrable conjunct. Neither continuity of D nor differentiability or
absolute continuity of E is a premise. The square identity concerns F alone.

[05] THE EXACT STRICT CUTOFF AND FULL-MEASURE SELECTION

Location: D05.01-D05.02.
Endpoint: LE059; prior composition LE014.

Assume nu>0 and delta>M^4/(16*pi^2*nu^5). Since the cutoff is nonnegative,
delta>0. Since pi>0 and nu>0, kappa=4*pi^2*nu^4 is positive. Multiplication
of the strict cutoff inequality by kappa gives exactly

  M^4/(4*nu) < kappa*delta.

Apply LE058 with R=delta. Suppose no s in (0,delta) satisfies both Good(s)
and E(s)D(s)<kappa. Then E(s)D(s)>=kappa at every good point of that interval.
If Good holds a.e. there, this lower bound holds a.e. The product is integrable
by LE058, and the constant is integrable on the finite interval. Hence

  kappa*delta <= integral_(0,delta) E*D
                 <= M^4/(4*nu) < kappa*delta,

a contradiction. This proves LE059 EnergyProfile.exists_subcritical_good_time.
The source pattern is [EP028] QuasiFeffermanB.exists_good_time_of_integral_lt,
ported into the prior scalar chain; the actual reused composition is
LE014 QFA.Endpoints.Composition.exists_subcritical_good_time_of_envelope.

The argument works with M=0 and never divides by M, E(s), or D(s). The strict
boundary delta>T_QFA is preserved. No claim at delta=T_QFA follows from this
strict-average selection argument.

[06] TEMPORAL TRANSLATION: EXACT DOMAINS AND INTEGRABILITY

Location: D05.03-D05.04.
Define timeTranslate(s,f)(t)=f(t+s), denoted f_s(t).

The map t |-> t+s is continuous, sends t>0 to t+s>s, and tends to s as
t->0+. Thus it sends the right-neighborhood filter of 0 to that of s.
This proves LE060, following [EP031]
QuasiFeffermanB.timeTranslate_tendsto_nhdsWithin_right, for every real s.

Lebesgue translation invariance and
  {t:t+s in (s,T+s]}=(0,T]
give restricted measure preservation
  (t |-> t+s)_*(dt restricted to (0,T]) = dt restricted to (s,T+s].
This is LE061, following [EP032]
QuasiFeffermanB.timeTranslate_measurePreserving_Ioc, for every real s,T.
For T<=0 both interval measures are zero.

If s>=0, then (s,T+s] is a subset of (0,T+s]. Restricting an assumed MemLp
class on the latter interval and pulling back by LE061 proves LE062:

  MemLp(f,p,(0,T+s]) -> MemLp(f_s,p,(0,T]), for every p in [0,infinity].

The target may be any normed additive group. The same restriction and pullback
for an arbitrary a.e. predicate P proves LE063:

  P holds a.e. on (0,T+s] -> P(t+s) holds a.e. on (0,T].

These are the precise patterns of [EP033]
QuasiFeffermanB.timeTranslate_memLp_Ioc and [EP034]
QuasiFeffermanB.timeTranslate_ae_Ioc. No measurability of {P} is required.

For every scalar f:R->R and real s,a,b, [EP035]
intervalIntegral.integral_comp_add_right gives the total-integral identity

  integral_a^b f(t+s)dt = integral_(a+s)^(b+s) f(t)dt.        [LE064]

Translation preserves actual integrability as well. In particular,
  IntervalIntegrable f volume (a+s) (b+s)
    -> IntervalIntegrable f_s volume a b.                    [LE065]
The exact source is [EP129] IntervalIntegrable.comp_add_right. The integral identity
also remains valid when integrability fails, under the total convention
specified in Section 00; it is not itself evidence of integrability.

If s>=0,T>=0 and D is integrable on [0,T+s], then [s,T+s] is a subinterval.
[EP128] IntervalIntegrable.mono_set restricts D, and LE065 translates it. Therefore
  D_s is interval integrable on [0,T].                       [LE066]
The hypotheses s>=0 and T>=0 are retained in this restriction statement.
For a field EnergyProfile, Section 04 supplies the required integrability
for every positive horizon T+s; when T+s=0 the interval is degenerate.

[07] ENERGY TRANSPORT WITH THE UPPER-ENDPOINT QUANTIFIERS RETAINED

Location: D05.03-D05.04.
Endpoints: LE067-LE069.

First let E,D:R->R, nu>=0, s>=0, D(r)>=0 for r>=0, and assume
  for every r>=s, E(r)+2*nu*integral_s^r D <= E(s).
For t>=s, D is nonnegative throughout [s,t]. The integral is nonnegative:
in the integrable case this follows by integral positivity, and in the
nonintegrable case its total value is zero. The exact source is [EP130]
intervalIntegral.integral_nonneg. Thus
  E(t)<=E(s).                                                [LE067]
This generic implication does not require integrability as a separate
premise. The field application has actual integrability from Section 04.

Next, for arbitrary real nu,s and E,D with that energy-start inequality,
let t>=0. Then t+s>=s, and LE064 with lower endpoint 0 gives
  E_s(t)+2*nu*integral_0^t D_s <= E(s).                       [LE068]
This is an algebraic total-integral implication; no positivity is needed
for the change of variables. In a physical application s>=0, so t+s>=0.
For the field data, all translated dissipation integrals are genuine by
LE066. The initial value of the translated velocity is exactly u_s(0)=u(s).

Finally assume s>=0 and, for a real finite T,
  for almost every r in (0,T+s], for every t in [r,T+s],
    E(t)+2*nu*integral_r^t D <= E(r).
LE063 transports this entire valid-start predicate to almost every
r in (0,T]. If t in [r,T], then t+s in [r+s,T+s]. Apply the supplied
inequality there and LE064 to obtain

  for almost every r in (0,T], for every t in [r,T],
    E_s(t)+2*nu*integral_r^t D_s <= E_s(r).                   [LE069]

The a.e. quantifier applies to the starting time; the upper-endpoint
quantifier remains every t in the stated interval. No a.e.-to-all upgrade
is claimed. The generic theorem permits T<=0, where the a.e. conclusion
is vacuous; physical positive-horizon applications use T>0. Integrability
does not follow from the energy inequality alone. It is retained separately
and, for the field data, follows by interval restriction and LE066.

[08] STRONG RIGHT TRACES AND DETERMINISTIC COMPOSITION

Location: D05.03.
Endpoints: LE070-LE073.

Let H be any real inner-product space, l any filter on an index type,
f an H-valued function, and b in H. Assume
  <f(i),b>_R -> ||b||^2 along l,
  ||f(i)||^2 <= ||b||^2 eventually along l.
By [EP030] norm_sub_sq_real,

  0 <= ||f(i)-b||^2
     = ||f(i)||^2 - 2*<f(i),b>_R + ||b||^2
     <= 2*||b||^2 - 2*<f(i),b>_R -> 0.

The squeeze theorem, [EP131] squeeze_zero', yields convergence of the squared
distance to zero. [EP132] Real.continuous_sqrt and sqrt(||x||^2)=||x|| yield norm
convergence, hence f->b. This proves LE070, the selective port of [EP029]
QuasiFeffermanB.strong_tendsto_of_inner_and_norm_bound. Completeness of H
is unnecessary; H_R is the complete Hilbert carrier in the application.

Let u:R->H, s>=0, nu>=0, D(r)>=0 for every r>=0, and assume
  t |-> <u(t),u(s)>_R is continuous on [0,infinity),
  ||u(r)||^2+2*nu*integral_s^r D <= ||u(s)||^2 for every r>=s.
The first premise gives
  <u(t),u(s)>_R -> <u(s),u(s)>_R=||u(s)||^2 as t->s+,
because (s,infinity) lies in [0,infinity). LE067 gives the eventual norm
upper bound. LE070 therefore gives
  u(t)->u(s) strongly as t->s+.                              [LE071]
No strong continuity of u or continuity of E is assumed. Composing this
limit with LE060 yields
  u_s(tau)->u(s) strongly as tau->0+.                        [LE072]
The supporting norm-pairing identity is [EP133] real_inner_self_eq_norm_sq.

For the field-level theorem in Section 01, define
  GoodStar(s) := Good(s) AND
    [for every r>=s, E(r)+2*nu*integral_s^r D <= E(s)].
P6 states precisely that GoodStar holds a.e. on (0,delta). Apply LE059
to this predicate, retaining it unchanged in the witness. Obtain
  0<s<delta, Good(s), E(s)D(s)<kappa, and the energy-start inequality.
LE052 gives D>=0 on all physical times. P5 at b=u(s) supplies the single
pairing continuity required by LE071. Use nu>0 -> nu>=0 and s>0 -> s>=0.
LE071 and LE072 give the two claimed strong traces. All remaining conjuncts
are the selected witness data. This completes the derivation of LE073.

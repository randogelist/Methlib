[A01] SPECTRAL REALIZATION IMPLIES INTERPOLATION

Exact theorem:

  LE094 / interpolation_of_nativeSpectralRealization
    {v L : ComplexL2} {G : Fin 3 -> ComplexL2}
    (hSpectral : NativeSpectralRealization v G L) :
    gradientEnergy G ^ 2 <= velocityEnergy v * laplacianEnergy L.

Fix any x in R. The quantities x*x and 2*x are constants in k. Since q and
w*q are integrable, so are

  k |-> (x*x) q(k),
  k |-> (2*x) w(k) q(k).

ENTRYPOINT AT APPLICATION:
  MeasureTheory.Integrable.const_mul
  Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/Integrable.lean:1039.

Their difference is integrable. Adding the supplied integrable w^2*q
preserves integrability. Pointwise polynomial expansion gives

  (x-w(k))^2 q(k)
    = (x*x) q(k) - (2*x) w(k) q(k) + w(k)^2 q(k).

ENTRYPOINTS AT APPLICATION:
  MeasureTheory.Integrable.sub
  Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/Integrable.lean:541.
  MeasureTheory.integral_congr_ae
  Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean:299.

The equality is pointwise and hence almost everywhere. Integral linearity
is applied only to the explicitly integrable summands. Constant extraction
and the three supplied spectral identities therefore give

  integral (x-w)^2 q
    = (x*x) integral q - (2*x) integral w*q + integral w^2*q
    = E*(x*x) + (-2*D)*x + Z.

ENTRYPOINTS AT APPLICATION:
  MeasureTheory.integral_add
  Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean:237.
  MeasureTheory.integral_sub
  Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean:261.
  MeasureTheory.integral_const_mul
  Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean:288.

Both (x-w(k))^2 and q(k) are nonnegative. Consequently

  0 <= integral (x-w)^2 q = E*(x*x) + (-2*D)*x + Z

for every x in R.

ENTRYPOINT AT APPLICATION:
  MeasureTheory.integral_nonneg
  Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean:612.

A real quadratic nonnegative at every real x has nonpositive discriminant:

  discrim E (-2*D) Z <= 0.

ENTRYPOINT AT APPLICATION:
  EP140 / discrim_le_zero
  Mathlib/Algebra/QuadraticDiscriminant.lean:124.
  Exact signature:
    variable {K : Type*} [Field K] [LinearOrder K] [IsStrictOrderedRing K]
      {a b c : K}
    theorem discrim_le_zero (h : ∀ x : K, 0 ≤ a * (x * x) + b * x + c) :
      discrim a b c ≤ 0
  Here K=R, a=E, b=-2*D, c=Z.
  Pinned source URL:
    https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d/Mathlib/Algebra/QuadraticDiscriminant.lean#L124

  4*D^2 - 4*E*Z <= 0,

[02] FINITE CLOSED-HORIZON COMPARISON

THEOREM LE098: ComparisonData.eq_on_Icc.

  ComparisonData(u,v,a0,T,g,Good) AND T>0
    ==> forall t in [0,T], u(t)=v(t).

PROOF.
  Apply the previously verified exact endpoint

    LE091:
    QFA.Assembly.Comparison.eq_on_Icc_of_ae_relative_energy.

  Its arguments, in order, are T>0 and CD01, CD02, CD03, CD04, CD05,
  CD06, CD07, CD08, CD09, CD10. No premise is weakened or omitted.

  The mathematical content of the finite comparison is as follows.
  Write e(t)=||u(t)-v(t)||^2. Then e(t)>=0 for every t.
  By CD05, choose r_n in Good intersect (0,min(T,1/(n+1))). These intervals
  have positive volume; their intersection with the full-measure Good set
  is nonempty. Thus r_n>0, r_n<T, and r_n->0.

  CD06 and CD07 imply e(r_n)->0. Fix t in (0,T]. Eventually r_n<t.
  On [r_n,T], g is continuous and nonnegative. CD04 restricts to actual
  integrability of g*e on this subinterval. CD10 is available at the
  admissible start r_n and at every upper endpoint, including t and T.

  Integral Gronwall gives

    e(t) <= e(r_n)*exp(integral_(r_n)^t g).

  This Gronwall statement does not require continuity of e. Indeed, with
  d=e(r_n), define

    F(x)=d+integral_(r_n)^x g(q)e(q)dq,
    G(x)=d+integral_(r_n)^x g(q)F(q)dq.

  The product integrability makes F continuous. Nonnegativity of g and
  e<=F imply F<=G. The function G is continuously differentiable in the
  open interval and satisfies G'=gF<=gG. The integrating factor
  exp(-integral_(r_n)^x g) gives G(x)<=d*exp(integral_(r_n)^x g).
  Consequently e<=F<=G yields the displayed estimate.

  By CD02 and CD03,

    integral_(r_n)^t g <= integral_0^T g =: M_T,

  where M_T is a finite real number. Hence

    0 <= e(t) <= e(r_n)*exp(M_T).

  Passing to the limit gives e(t)=0, and the norm separates points, so
  u(t)=v(t). At zero, CD08 and CD09 give the same equality directly.
  QED.

PROVENANCE.
  LE091 consumes LE090, which constructs admissible times using LE026 and
  applies LE030 with coefficient budget M_T equal to the actual integral.
  LE030 supplies the norm-valued form of scalar relative-energy comparison.

1. FROZEN THEOREM

Domain: T^3 = (R/Z)^3, normalized spatial volume 1.
Viscosity: nu > 0. Forcing: f = 0.
E := L^2_{sigma,0}(T^3;R^3).
sigma := divergence zero in distributions. 0 := zero spatial mean.

LH_nu(a) := global standard Leray-Hopf velocity trajectories from a:
  actual distributional NSE on the periodic domain;
  weak L^2 continuity, strong L^2 initial trace a;
  L^infinity_loc([0,infinity);L^2) intersect L^2_loc([0,infinity);H^1);
  initial energy inequality and energy inequalities from almost every
  positive starting time.
Velocity equality is equality of L^2 classes at every time, with the weakly
continuous representative fixed. Smoothness means existence of a smooth
representative. Pressure is normalized to zero spatial mean.

Define
  L(a) := ||a||_2^2,
  K(a) := L(a)/2,
  T_star(a,nu) := L(a)^2/(8*pi^2*nu^5),
  tau(a,nu) := 2*T_star(a,nu).

! THEOREM Quasi_Fefferman_B.
  For every nu>0 and every a in E:
    (i) LH_nu(a) is nonempty;
    (ii) for every u in LH_nu(a) and every delta>T_star(a,nu),
         u and its normalized pressure have smooth representatives on
         [delta,infinity) x T^3;
    (iii) for all t>=delta,
         ||u(t)||_2^2 + 2*nu*integral_delta^t ||grad u(s)||_2^2 ds
           = ||u(delta)||_2^2;
    (iv) LH_nu(u(delta)) = {r |-> u(delta+r)}.
  Consequently every such u is smooth on [tau(a,nu),infinity), with the
  zero datum treated separately by u=0 and normalized p=0.

Smoothness on a closed positive-time interval denotes smoothness inherited
from an open spacetime neighborhood; no backward NSE extension to t<0 is
asserted. The original a remains the strong trace at t=0.

Not part of the theorem:
  arbitrary delta>0 for fixed nonzero a;
  equality delta=T_star;
  uniqueness of LH_nu(a) before the cutoff;
  coincidence of different branches at the cutoff;
  global smoothness from t=0 for every smooth datum;
  analyticity at t=0.
Branch-dependent regular traces u(delta) may differ. Each trace has its own
unique future. The theorem is quantitative eventual regularity.

2. EXACT MATHEMATICAL PROOF SPINE

For v in H^1 intersect E define
  D(v) := ||grad v||_2^2,
  H(v) := ||v||_{dot H^(1/2)}^2
        = sum_{k!=0} (2*pi*|k|)|v_hat(k)|^2.
The gradient norm is the Hilbert-Schmidt matrix norm integrated in space.
For arbitrary L^2 fields, H may be defined as an extended nonnegative sum.

2.1. Existence and energy.
The standard periodic Leray-Hopf existence theorem supplies LH_nu(a)!=empty
for every a in E, by finite Fourier-Galerkin energy bounds, time-derivative
bounds, compactness, nonlinear convergence, and the energy inequality.
The strong L^2 initial trace follows from weak initial continuity and the
initial energy inequality. No smallness of a is used.
For every u in LH_nu(a), every T>0:
  L(u(t)) <= L(a),
  integral_0^T D(u(t))dt <= L(a)/(2*nu).

2.2. Physical midpoint interpolation.
Parseval and Cauchy-Schwarz, with kappa_k=2*pi*|k|, give
  H(v) = sum_{k!=0} |v_hat(k)|*(kappa_k*|v_hat(k)|)
       <= sqrt(L(v))*sqrt(D(v)).
Hence H(v)^2 <= L(v)*D(v).
The positive partial Fourier sums defining H(u(t)) are measurable; their
monotone limit is measurable. H is finite at almost every H^1 trace time.
Thus
  integral_0^T H(u(t))^2dt
    <= L(a)*integral_0^T D(u(t))dt
    <= L(a)^2/(2*nu).                                    (2.1)

2.3. Exact strict admission.
Section 3 proves, with actual NSE semantics,
  b in H^(1/2) intersect E,
  H(b)<2*pi*nu^2 ==> Reg_nu(b),                            (2.2)
where Reg_nu includes actual LH existence, positive-time smoothness,
strong L^2 initial trace, and uniqueness among all standard LH solutions.
Only H^1 restart inputs are needed by the present root.
The closed critical endpoint H(b)<=2*pi*nu^2 is unnecessary here.

2.4. Conull restart times.
Let R be the intersection of the conull H^1-trace times and admissible
starting times for the LH energy inequality. Choose compatible full-measure
sets over all integer horizons and intersect them countably.
For s in R, weak continuity and the energy inequality starting at s imply
  limsup_{t downarrow s} ||u(t)||_2 <= ||u(s)||_2.
Weak lower semicontinuity gives the reverse liminf. Hilbert weak convergence
plus norm convergence implies strong right continuity at s.
Time translation preserves the weak equation, spatial constraints, local
energy bounds, and a.e.-start energy inequalities. Therefore
  Shift(s,u) in LH_nu(u(s)),
with the required new strong initial trace. Merely selecting H^1 times
would not, by itself, establish this entire restart statement.

2.5. Good-time selection with exact constant.
Fix delta>T_star(a,nu). Suppose H(u(s))>=2*pi*nu^2 almost everywhere on
(0,delta). Equation (2.1) would imply
  4*pi^2*nu^4*delta <= L(a)^2/(2*nu),
contradicting delta>L(a)^2/(8*pi^2*nu^5).
Consequently the set of strict-admission times has positive measure.
Intersecting it with R gives
  0<s<delta,
  u(s) in H^1 intersect E,
  Shift(s,u) in LH_nu(u(s)),
  H(u(s))<2*pi*nu^2.                                    (2.3)
No square roots, infinite-time integral, or separate Markov theorem is
needed for this selection argument.

2.6. Identification of the actual tail.
Apply (2.2) to b=u(s). The regular solution from b is unique in LH_nu(b).
By (2.3), Shift(s,u) is that same LH solution. Therefore u is smooth for
t>s, including an open neighborhood of [delta,infinity). The normalized
pressure is recovered uniquely from
  -Delta p = sum_{i,j} partial_i partial_j(u_i*u_j).
The smooth representative agrees with the original distributional solution;
it is not a separately selected, unrelated trajectory.

2.7. Energy and uniqueness from the regular trace.
Classical integration of NSE on [delta,t] gives conclusion (iii).
On every finite interval [delta,T], u belongs to L^2_t L^infinity_x.
Relative energy and Gronwall identify every LH solution from u(delta)
with Shift(delta,u) on [0,T-delta]. Exhausting T proves (iv).

2.8. Zero datum and safe cutoff.
L(a)=0 implies a=0 and the energy inequality forces u=0; normalized p=0.
Otherwise T_star>0 and tau=2*T_star>T_star. This proves the closed-cutoff
conclusion, and completes the exact frozen theorem. QED.

Energy-slice corollary:
  K(a)=Ekin ==> T_star=Ekin^2/(2*pi^2*nu^5).
Thus one cutoff works for every initial datum and every LH trajectory on
the same kinetic-energy slice. This does not assert optimality.

3. EXACT NUMERICAL ADMISSION: COMPLETE SUBDERIVATION

Notation:
  F_b(t)=sum_{k!=0}|b_hat(k)| exp(-4*pi^2*|k|^2*t),
  Q(b)^2=integral_0^infinity F_b(t)^2dt.
All sums below use nonnegative summands where Tonelli is invoked.

3.1. Gaussian lattice estimate.
Let Theta(t)=sum_{k in Z^3}exp(-t|k|^2), t>0.
Gaussian Poisson summation gives
  Theta(t)=(pi/t)^(3/2)*Theta(pi^2/t).
Since pi>3 and exp(3)>131/8>16,
  sum_{n in Z}exp(-pi*n^2)<1+2*sum_{n>=1}16^(-n)=17/15,
and Theta(pi)<(17/15)^3<2.
For t>=pi:
  Theta(t)-1 <= exp(pi-t)*(Theta(pi)-1) < exp(pi-t)
             <= (pi/t)^(3/2).
The last inequality follows from the nonnegative increasing function
t-pi-(3/2)log(t/pi) on [pi,infinity).
For t<pi apply the already proved bound at s=pi^2/t and the transformation.
It follows that Theta(t)-1 <= (pi/t)^(3/2) for every t>0.

3.2. Lattice resolvent and weighted Schur.
For r>0, Laplace representation and Tonelli give
  sum_{l!=0}1/[|l|^2*(r^2+|l|^2)]
   = r^(-2)*integral_0^infinity
       (1-exp(-r^2*t))*(Theta(t)-1)dt
   <= pi^(3/2)*r^(-2)*integral_0^infinity
       (1-exp(-r^2*t))*t^(-3/2)dt
   = 2*pi^2/r.
The last integral is 2*r*sqrt(pi), by integration by parts and the Gaussian
integral; its boundary terms vanish at zero and infinity.

Set x_k=sqrt(2*pi*|k|)*|b_hat(k)| and w_k=|k|^(-3/2).
Then
  Q(b)^2=sum_{k,l!=0} M_kl*x_k*x_l,
  M_kl=1/[8*pi^3*sqrt(|k|*|l|)*(|k|^2+|l|^2)].
The row bound is
  w_k^(-1)*sum_l M_kl*w_l
     = |k|/(8*pi^3)*sum_{l!=0}1/[|l|^2*(|k|^2+|l|^2)]
     <=1/(4*pi).
Using symmetry and
  2*x_k*x_l <= x_k^2*w_l/w_k + x_l^2*w_k/w_l,
followed by monotone convergence of finite sums, yields
  Q(b)^2 <= H(b)/(4*pi).                                 (3.1)
The Schur weight need not belong to ell2. Strict row defects and an
equality-case analysis are not needed for the frozen root.

3.3. Symmetric Leray multiplier and caloric bilinear estimate.
Let Y=L^2((0,infinity);ell1(Z^3\{0};C^3)), restricted to real, solenoidal,
mean-zero Fourier fields, with chi_0(v)=sum_k |v_hat(k)|.
Define
  B_nu(v,w)(t)=-(1/2)*integral_0^t exp(nu*(t-s)*Delta)
                 P div(v tensor w+w tensor v)(s)ds.
For a real unit vector e, write z=alpha*e+Z and w=beta*e+W with transverse
complex vectors Z,W. The Hermitian Euclidean norm gives
  |P_e[(e.z)w+(e.w)z]|
    =|alpha*W+beta*Z|
    <=|alpha|*|W|+|beta|*|Z|
    <=|z|*|w|.
Thus the symmetric multiplier is bounded by kappa_k/2 at output k!=0.
The temporal kernel satisfies
  ||kappa_k*exp(-nu*kappa_k^2*t)||_L2(0,infinity)=1/sqrt(2*nu).
Temporal Young L1*L2->L2, Minkowski, the pairwise convolution sum, and
Cauchy-Schwarz in time imply
  ||B_nu(v,w)||_Y <= b_nu*||v||_Y*||w||_Y,
  b_nu=1/(2*sqrt(2*nu)).                                 (3.2)
The same bound holds uniformly under orthogonal Fourier truncation.
The heat norm is ||exp(nu*t*Delta)b||_Y=Q(b)/sqrt(nu).

3.4. Uniform Galerkin budget.
Take standard solenoidal orthogonal Fourier-Galerkin solutions u_N with
initial states P_N b. Their exact L2 energy identity gives global finite
dimensional existence. Assume Q(b)<nu/sqrt(2), and put h=Q(b)/sqrt(nu).
Then 4*b_nu*h<1. Set
  r_minus=(1-sqrt(1-4*b_nu*h))/(2*b_nu),
  r_plus =(1+sqrt(1-4*b_nu*h))/(2*b_nu).
For X_N(T)=||u_N||_Y(0,T), Duhamel and (3.2) give
  X_N(T)<=h+b_nu*X_N(T)^2.
Continuity, X_N(0)=0, and the gap (r_minus,r_plus) imply
  X_N(T)<=r_minus<=2*h.
Hence, uniformly in N,
  integral_0^infinity chi_0(u_N(t))^2dt
    <= S := 4*Q(b)^2/nu.                                (3.3)

3.5. Actual NSE limit and smoothing.
Energy bounds give L^infinity_t L2_x and L2_t H1_x bounds on finite horizons.
The nonlinear H^(-1) norm is bounded by
  C*||u_N||_4^2 <= C*||u_N||_2^(1/2)*||u_N||_H1^(3/2),
so time derivatives are bounded in L^(4/3)_t H^(-1)_x.
The standard periodic Galerkin compactness argument gives a diagonal
subsequence strongly converging in L2_loc(t;L2_x), an actual LH limit u,
and its strong initial trace b. The a.e.-start energy inequality is retained
at a conull set of strong L2 convergence times and extended to endpoints
by weak continuity. Fourier coefficient convergence and Fatou give
  integral_0^infinity ||u(t)||_infinity^2dt
    <= integral_0^infinity chi_0(u(t))^2dt <= S.          (3.4)

For E1_N=||grad u_N||_2^2 and E2_N=||Delta u_N||_2^2, the finite enstrophy
identity, Holder, and Young give
  E1_N' + nu*E2_N <= nu^(-1)*chi_0(u_N)^2*E1_N.
Gronwall between s and t and integration over 0<s<t yield
  E1_N(t) <= ||b||_2^2*exp(S/nu)/(2*nu*t), t>0.         (3.5)
Integrating the inequality gives uniform H2 dissipation on every [eta,T],
eta>0. Standard periodic local H1 theory, its continuation criterion, and
parabolic bootstrapping imply positive-time smoothness of the actual limit.
The normalized pressure is recovered by the spatial Poisson equation.
Smooth positive-time energy equality and the strong initial trace give
the energy equality from zero.

3.6. All-LH uniqueness.
Let v be any other LH solution from b. Relative energy against the smooth
reference u from common admissible times epsilon>0 gives
  ||v(t)-u(t)||_2^2
    <= ||v(epsilon)-u(epsilon)||_2^2
       *exp(nu^(-1)*integral_epsilon^t ||u(s)||_infinity^2ds).
Equation (3.4) bounds the exponential uniformly by exp(S/nu).
Take epsilon down to zero through admissible times and use both strong
initial traces. Then v=u as L2 trajectories, including every time by weak
continuity. Therefore Q(b)<nu/sqrt(2) implies Reg_nu(b).
Finally (3.1) and H(b)<2*pi*nu^2 imply Q(b)^2<nu^2/2.
This proves the strict numerical admission (2.2). QED.

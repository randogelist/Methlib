2. COMPLETE SCOPED DERIVATION: IMPLICATION HALF

2.1 Native dictionary and frequency gap.
For each nonzero lattice mode, |k|>=1, hence the critical weight is at least
2*pi. The audited coefficient embedding yields
  L(a)^2 <= H(a)^2/(2*pi).
R01 transports its coefficient sums to the native datum and physical L2 class.
The zero coefficient is zero; the native convention omega(0)=1 contributes 0.

2.2 Coercivity.
Since L(a)^2>=0,
  H(a)^2-(5/2)L(a)^2 <= H(a)^2.
The frequency gap gives
  H(a)^2-(5/2)L(a)^2
    >= [1-5/(4*pi)]H(a)^2
    = gamma H(a)^2.
Thus R02 holds.

2.3 Stage zero and positivity.
The elementary pi bound pi>2 implies gamma>3/8>0.
C>0 gives gamma/(4*C^2)>0, so kappa>0.
The actual constant moment m_0=2 integral_0^1 1=2 gives L_0=2<pi.

2.4 Strict native smallness.
If mathfrak_A_nu(a)<2, then
  A_nu(a) < 2*kappa/pi < kappa.
Consequently,
  gamma H(a)^2 <= 2*nu^2 A_nu(a) < 2*nu^2*kappa,
  C^2 H(a)^2 < 2*nu^2*C^2*kappa/gamma <= nu^2/2.
All divided constants are strictly positive.
Taking nonnegative square roots yields
  C H(a)<nu/sqrt(2)<nu.
This proves R03, including strictness.

2.5 Native application.
Apply the exact audited theorem to nu, a, the actual CriticalSigmaZero
certificate and C H(a)<nu. Its conclusion is Reg_nu(a).
This proves R04 and IMPLICATION_ROOT.
The esssup premise is compatible with the conclusion and is not an analytic
input needed by the inherited small-data theorem.

3. COMPLETE SCOPED DERIVATION: NONVACUITY HALF

3.1 Finite blocks.
The j-th block contains 2^j terms and each coefficient is 1/(j*2^j).
The first two sums in R05 follow immediately.
For the weighted sum, the arithmetic progression has sum
  2^j(3*2^j-1)/2.
Multiplication by 1/(j^2*2^(2*j)) gives the third identity.

Positivity of R and S follows from their positive first terms.
Since 1/j^2<=1, with strict inequality at j=2,
  R < sum_{j>=1} 2^(-j)=1.
Also
  S < 3 sum_{j>=1} 1/j^2 < 6.
For the latter strict bound, use monotonicity of x^(-2):
  sum_{j>=2} 1/j^2 < integral_1^infinity x^(-2)dx=1.

3.2 Native critical datum.
Insert the stated coefficients at k=(0,+/-n,0).
They are real and equal on opposite modes; their first-component direction
is orthogonal to k; k=0 is absent.
The block sums imply finite weighted square sum, so the native constructor
produces b_nu in D. Parseval gives
  L(b_nu)^2 = 2*(nu/4)^2 sum_{n>=2} d_n^2 = nu^2 R/8;
  H(b_nu)^2 = 2*(nu/4)^2*(2*pi) sum_{n>=2} n*d_n^2
             = pi*nu^2 S/8.
Since nu>0 and S>0, H(b_nu)>0.

3.3 Pointwise cosine-series representative.
The sequence d_n is nonincreasing and tends to zero.
For y notin Z and integers N>=2, geometric sums and Abel summation give
  |sum_{n>N}d_n cos(2*pi*n*y)|
    <= d_{N+1}/|sin(pi*y)|.
The series therefore converges uniformly on compact sets away from y=0.
Fourier truncations converge in the native Hhalf norm and hence in physical L2.
An L2-convergent subsequence converges a.e.; its pointwise limit away from
the null plane is the cosine series. Thus the native physical L2 class equals
(nu/2)g(x_2)e_1 a.e. This proves R07.

3.4 Positive-measure divergence.
For 0<|y|<1/24, set
  N=floor(1/(6|y|)), j=floor(log_2(N+1)).
For 2<=n<=N,
  cos(2*pi*n*y)>=1/2.
Complete dyadic blocks 1,...,j-1 lie below N. Moreover,
  d_{N+1}<=2/[j(N+1)];
  |sin(pi*y)|>=2|y|;
  (N+1)|y|>1/6.
Therefore
  g(y)>=(1/2)sum_{k=1}^{j-1}1/k - 6/j.
As y->0 through nonzero values, j->infinity, so g(y)->+infinity.

Fix M>=0. Choose 0<delta<1/24 such that
  0<y<delta ==> g(y)>1+(2/nu)*sqrt(2*M).
On the strip {x:0<x_2<delta}, outside the null exceptional set from R07,
  rho_{b_nu}(x)=nu^2 g(x_2)^2/8 > M.
The strip has measure delta>0. Thus
  mu{x:rho_{b_nu}(x)>M}>0.
This proves R08 and the infinite essential supremum.
For the stronger local version, intersect the strip with a sufficiently
small product neighborhood contained in the specified open neighborhood.

3.5 Scaling identities.
Physical and Fourier scalar compatibility give H(ra)=rH(a), L(ra)=rL(a)
for r>0. Both score terms are quadratic, hence
  mathfrak_A_nu(ra)=r^2 mathfrak_A_nu(a).
The physical density satisfies rho_{ra}=r^2 rho_a a.e.
For each finite M>=0,
  mu{rho_{ra}>M}=mu{rho_a>M/r^2}>0
whenever EssUnbounded(a). This proves R09.

3.6 Dyadic scale selection.
Put
  h=H(b_nu)>0,
  s=mathfrak_A_nu(b_nu)>=0,
  delta_* = min{1/2, epsilon/[2(1+h)], 1/sqrt(1+s)} > 0.
Choose m>=1 with r=2^(-m)<=delta_*.
Then
  0<H(r*b_nu)=r*h
    <= epsilon*h/[2(1+h)] < epsilon/2 < epsilon;
  mathfrak_A_nu(r*b_nu)=r^2*s
    <= s/(1+s) < 1 < 2.
This proves R10. It strengthens the required strict score bound.
The choice is existential; no executable numerical value of m is claimed.

The normalized coefficients remain rational:
  (r*b_nu)_hat(0,+/-n,0)/nu = d_n e_1/(4*2^m).

3.7 Nonvacuity and root.
Set a=r*b_nu. R06 and R09 give a in D.
R10 gives the positive norm and strict admission bounds.
R08 and R09 give esssup rho_a=+infinity.
Therefore P(nu,epsilon,a); this is R11.
R04 gives Reg_nu(a) for this identical a; this is R12.
QED.

4. MEASURE-THEORETIC ENDPOINT TYPE

Recommended internal predicate:
  EssUnbounded(a) :=
    forall M : Real, 0<=M ->
      0 < mu {x | M < (1/2)*||a_phys(x)||^2}.

The measure comparison is in the native extended nonnegative measure type.
An equivalent endpoint may state
  eLpNorm rho_a infinity mu = infinity,
provided rho_a is proved measurable and nonnegative.
The final exported mathematical statement remains
  esssup rho_a=+infinity.

Auxiliary equivalences:
  finite L-infinity norm of a
    <=> finite L-infinity norm of rho_a;
  rho_a in L1, since integral rho_a=(1/2)L(a)^2<infinity.
Hence the constructed density belongs to L1 \ L-infinity.
Modification on a null set does not change any endpoint.

Finite integrated local energy and unbounded density are distinct:
  forall measurable E subset T3,
    integral_E rho_a <= (1/2)L(a)^2 < infinity.
No infinite integrated-energy conclusion is asserted.

CRITICAL_HHALF_UNIQUENESS_SMOOTHING
B_TIER_DERIVATION

THEOREM.
Let ν>0. Let T³ be a fixed three-dimensional flat torus. All velocity fields
below are real, periodic, divergence free, and have spatial mean zero. Let
Λ=(-Δ)^(1/2) on the nonzero Fourier modes and write H^s_σ,0 for the associated
Sobolev spaces. Homogeneous and inhomogeneous norms are equivalent on these
mean-zero spaces.

Let a∈H^(1/2)_σ,0. Suppose an actual distributional solution u of

    ∂_t u + P div(u⊗u) = νΔu                         (1)

has distributional initial trace a and, for EVERY finite T>0, satisfies

    u ∈ L∞(0,T;H^(1/2)) ∩ L²(0,T;H^(3/2)),
    ∂_t u ∈ L²(0,T;H^(-1/2)).                       (2)

The intervals in (2) include integrability arbitrarily close to t=0.
The stronger hypothesis sup_{t≥0} ‖u(t)‖H^(1/2)≤ε ν, with ε a prescribed
small constant, is permitted but is unnecessary for the implication proved
here. Its role in a small-data theorem is to establish the solution and (2).

Then:
(a) u has a representative in C([0,T];H^(1/2)) for every finite T and u(0)=a.
(b) u satisfies the exact L² energy equality and is a Leray–Hopf solution.
(c) Every Leray–Hopf solution with datum a equals u, including at every time
    in the standard weakly continuous L² representatives.
(d) For every 0<δ<T and every integer m≥0,

    u ∈ C([δ,T];H^m) ∩ L²(δ,T;H^(m+1)).

    The velocity and the mean-zero pressure are C∞ for t>0.
(e) If a is smooth, all the preceding integer-order continuity statements
    hold on [0,T]. Every spatial and right-time derivative extends
    continuously to t=0. Thus velocity and normalized pressure are smooth
    up to the initial-time boundary.

This theorem assumes the actual solution (1) with (2); it does not assume
critical-space global well-posedness, a Serrin regularity theorem, or the
sought uniqueness/smoothing conclusion. The proof below derives those
conclusions through energy estimates and local Sobolev constructions.

PROOF.

1. Strong initial trace and exact energy.

Put z=Λ^(1/2)u. On every finite interval,

    z∈L²H¹,                  z_t=Λ^(1/2)u_t∈L²H^(-1).

The Hilbert-triple time-continuity lemma applied to
H¹⊂L²⊂H^(-1) gives z∈C([0,T];L²). Consequently
u∈C([0,T];H^(1/2)). Its value at zero equals a by the assumed distributional
trace; equality in distributions identifies the H^(1/2) elements uniquely.

Also u∈L²H¹ and u_t∈L²H^(-1). The same Hilbert-triple lemma gives the
absolutely continuous L² norm identity

    (1/2)d/dt ‖u‖₂² = ⟨u_t,u⟩H^(-1),H¹.

For almost every t, u(t)∈H^(3/2)⊂H¹. Periodic integration by parts and
incompressibility give b(u,u,u)=0, where

    b(f,g,h)=∫T³ (f·∇)g·h.

This cancellation extends from smooth fields by H¹ approximation: b is a
continuous trilinear form on H¹×H¹×H¹, using the L⁶, L², L³ Hölder exponents.
Pairing (1) with u therefore yields, for every 0≤s≤t≤T,

    (1/2)‖u(t)‖₂² + ν∫_s^t ‖∇u(r)‖₂² dr = (1/2)‖u(s)‖₂².       (3)

Thus u satisfies the Leray–Hopf conditions, with strong initial convergence.
The argument also supplies energy equality starting at every positive time.

2. The decisive integrable coefficient.

Cauchy–Schwarz in Fourier variables gives the exact homogeneous interpolation
inequality

    ‖u(t)‖Ḣ¹² ≤ ‖u(t)‖Ḣ^(1/2) ‖u(t)‖Ḣ^(3/2).

Hence

    I_T := ∫_0^T ‖∇u(t)‖₂⁴ dt
         ≤ ‖u‖L∞Ḣ^(1/2)² ‖u‖L²Ḣ^(3/2)² <∞.                  (4)

The mean-zero Sobolev inequality ‖f‖₆≤C‖∇f‖₂ implies

    u∈L⁴(0,T;L⁶),          ∫_0^T ‖u(t)‖₆⁴dt≤C I_T.           (5)

The exponents satisfy 2/4+3/6=1. No regularity theorem is invoked from that
identity; the needed conclusions are derived below.

3. Uniqueness against every Leray–Hopf solution.

Let v be any Leray–Hopf solution with datum a and put w=v−u. Conservation of
the mean implies that w is mean zero. Combining the energy inequality for v,
the energy equality (3) for u, and the cross-tested weak equations gives

    (1/2)‖w(t)‖₂² + ν∫_0^t ‖∇w‖₂²
       ≤ (1/2)‖w(0)‖₂² + ∫_0^t |b(w,w,u)|.                 (6)

Here (6) is a legitimate relative-energy inequality, not a formal test by an
unavailable smooth function. Indeed, the energy class of v implies
v∈L⁴L³ by ‖v‖₃≤C‖v‖₂^(1/2)‖∇v‖₂^(1/2). Thus

    ‖u‖L⁴L⁶ ‖v‖L⁴L³ ‖∇v‖L²L² <∞,

which controls the nonlinear cross term after moving its derivative onto v.
Moreover u_t∈L²H^(-1/2) pairs with v∈L²H^(1/2). Time smoothing and Fourier
truncation of u converge in these norms and in L⁴L⁶, and therefore permit u
as a limit of admissible tests in the v equation. The u equation is tested
against similarly regularized v; its nonlinear and viscous terms converge
by the same bounds. The endpoint products converge using strong L²
continuity of u and weak L² continuity of v. These limits produce (6) first
at the energy-inequality times, which suffice for the argument below.

The integrand obeys

    |b(w,w,u)|
      ≤ ‖u‖₆ ‖w‖₃ ‖∇w‖₂
      ≤ C ‖u‖₆ ‖w‖₂^(1/2) ‖∇w‖₂^(3/2)
      ≤ (ν/2)‖∇w‖₂² + Cν^(-3)‖u‖₆⁴ ‖w‖₂².              (7)

The final line is Young's inequality with conjugate exponents 4/3 and 4.
Since w(0)=0, (5)–(7) and integral Grönwall give ‖w(t)‖₂²=0 for almost every
t. Weak continuity then gives w(t)=0 for every t. The same proof applies on
any translated interval where the two solutions have the same initial
trace and the comparison field has the stated integrability.

4. Local H¹ construction and its continuation criterion.

The local result used here is subcritical and follows directly from finite
Fourier Galerkin equations. For initial b∈H¹_σ,0, set

    X=‖∇U‖₂²,               Y=‖ΔU‖₂².

For the smooth Galerkin equations, the H¹ energy calculation yields

    (1/2)X' + νY = −b(U,U,−ΔU).

Sobolev embedding and interpolation give

    |b(U,U,−ΔU)|
      ≤ ‖U‖₆ ‖∇U‖₃ ‖ΔU‖₂
      ≤ C X^(3/4)Y^(3/4)
      ≤ (ν/2)Y + Cν^(-3)X³.

After adjusting C,

    X' + νY ≤ Cν^(-3)X³.                                (8)

The scalar comparison inequality supplies X(t)≤2X(0) on a time interval
of length cν³/X(0)² when X(0)>0. When X(0)=0, mean zero gives b=0 and the
zero solution is global. Integrating (8) supplies a uniform L²H² bound.
Also

    ‖P(U·∇U)‖₂² ≤ C X^(3/2)Y^(1/2),

so the time derivatives are bounded in L²L² on this local interval.
Compactness of the Galerkin sequence gives strong L²H¹ convergence and
passes the quadratic term in distributions. The limit has

    U∈L∞H¹∩L²H²,                U_t∈L²L².

The Hilbert-triple lemma, after applying Λ, gives U∈C H¹ and the prescribed
H¹ initial trace. It satisfies (3). Step 3 gives uniqueness on the local
interval, since an H¹ strong solution belongs to L⁴L⁶. This constructs the
local H¹ solution without any global critical-space theorem.

The local existence interval depends only on ν, the fixed torus, and the H¹
norm of the starting datum. Therefore an H¹ solution can fail to continue
at a finite maximal time only if its H¹ norm becomes unbounded. Equivalently,
if X and ∫Y stay bounded up to that time, the above derivative estimate and
Hilbert-triple continuity give a terminal H¹ trace; the local construction
at that trace extends the solution. Local uniqueness glues the extensions.

5. Exclusion of finite H¹ blowup for the given critical solution.

Fix 0<δ<T. Since u∈L²H^(3/2), choose s∈(0,δ) with u(s)∈H¹. Construct the
local H¹ solution U with initial datum u(s). Equation (3) makes the restriction
of u to [s,T] Leray–Hopf with that datum. Step 3 identifies U=u on their
common interval.

On that interval (8) has the form

    X' ≤ [Cν^(-3)X²]X.

The bracket is integrable by (4), because U=u. Grönwall therefore gives

    X(t) ≤ X(s) exp(Cν^(-3)∫_s^t X(r)²dr)
         ≤ X(s) exp(Cν^(-3)I_T).                          (9)

Integrating (8) and using the bound in (9) also bounds ∫Y up to any proposed
finite maximal time ≤T. Step 4 then extends the H¹ solution past that time,
a contradiction. Since T is arbitrary, U continues for all t≥s. In
particular u∈C([δ,T];H¹)∩L²(δ,T;H²).

The additional information (4) is essential: the cubic differential
inequality X'≤CX³ alone permits finite-time blowup. The argument uses the
integrable linear-growth coefficient X² furnished by the critical bounds.

6. Spatial smoothing by integer-order continuation.

For integer m≥2, H^m(T³) is a multiplication algebra. On mean-zero fields,
its norm is equivalent to the homogeneous norm. Write

    Z_m=‖U‖Ḣ^m²,              W_m=‖U‖Ḣ^(m+1)².

The product bound

    ‖(U·∇)U‖Ḣ^m ≤ C_m ‖U‖Ḣ^m ‖U‖Ḣ^(m+1)

and the H^m energy identity imply

    Z_m' + ν W_m ≤ C_m ν^(-1) Z_m².                       (10)

The same Galerkin argument as in step 4 constructs a local H^m solution,
with lifespan depending only on its H^m datum and ν. It lies in
C H^m∩L²H^(m+1), with time derivative in L²H^(m−1), and it agrees with the
H¹ solution by step 3.

Start with the H¹ solution from step 5. Its L²H² bound allows a time
s_2∈(s,δ) with u(s_2)∈H². For the local H² solution, the coefficient Z_2 in
Z_2'≤C_2ν^(-1)Z_2 Z_2 is integrable on [s_2,T], by the existing L²H² bound.
Thus Grönwall bounds Z_2 and then (10) bounds ∫W_2. The H² continuation
criterion gives C H²∩L²H³ up to T.

Inductively, suppose C H^(m−1)∩L²H^m has been obtained from a time
s_(m−1)<δ. Choose s_m∈(s_(m−1),δ) with u(s_m)∈H^m. On the common interval,
∫Z_m is already finite by the induction hypothesis. Equation (10) gives

    Z_m(t) ≤ Z_m(s_m) exp(C_mν^(-1)∫_(s_m)^t Z_m(r)dr).

The resulting bounded H^m norm and integrated H^(m+1) norm prevent any
finite H^m endpoint. This proves C H^m∩L²H^(m+1) on [δ,T] for every m.
Only finitely many induction steps are needed for each fixed m; the choices
s_m<δ introduce no common-time obstruction. All representatives agree
with the fixed C H^(1/2) representative.

7. Time derivatives and pressure.

For every integer q≥2, the conclusions of step 6 place the right side of

    u_t = νΔu − P div(u⊗u)

in C([δ,T];H^q), by using two additional spatial orders. The distributional
identity therefore yields u∈C¹([δ,T];H^q). Differentiating the polynomial
right side repeatedly and using arbitrarily high spatial orders gives
u∈C^j([δ,T];H^q) for all j,q. Sobolev embedding then gives joint C∞
regularity on (0,∞)×T³.

Define the pressure by spatial mean zero and

    −Δp = ∂_i∂_j(u_i u_j).

The nonzero-mode Fourier multiplier defining p is bounded on every H^q.
Thus p has the same positive-time joint smoothness. Its spatial mean-zero
normalization removes the arbitrary function of time.

8. Smooth initial datum.

If a is smooth, step 4 starts at s=0. The uniqueness argument identifies
this local H¹ solution with u immediately, and (4), (8), and (9) continue it
globally. For every m≥2, a∈H^m, so the local H^m construction starts at zero
instead of at a selected positive time. Induction using (10) and the
preceding order's L²H^m estimate gives C([0,T];H^m)∩L²(0,T;H^(m+1)).

The equation now has a continuous H^q right side on [0,T] for every q.
Its integral form gives the right derivative at zero, and differentiation
of the polynomial equation inductively gives every right-time derivative
there. The pressure formula behaves identically. This proves smoothness
up to the time boundary in the precise one-sided sense claimed in (e).
For merely H^(1/2) datum, no smoothness of u at t=0 is asserted; the strong
H^(1/2) trace in (a) is the applicable conclusion.

The proof of (a)–(e) is complete.

SCOPE AND STATUS.
The theorem is a mathematical consequence of the literal distributional
NSE, its given initial trace, and the concrete bounds (2). No small-data
existence theorem is used as a premise of the derivation. This file does
not itself construct the global critical solution from a; that upstream
construction is a separate obligation. No Lean compilation or theorem-level
axiom audit is asserted by this B-tier artifact.

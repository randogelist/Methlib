0. EXACT THEOREM AND SCOPE
------------------------
Fix any nu>0. Define the previously frozen directional shear class

  S_DIR_L2 = union over a in Z^3\{0} of
    { u0=a*phi : phi in L2_0(T^3;R),
                 a dot grad phi=0 in distributions }.

Let u0 be any member. Then there exists a canonical trajectory

  u(t) = exp(nu*t*Delta) u0,  t>=0,

with all properties:

  (T1) u in C([0,infinity);L2_{sigma,0}(T^3)); u(0)=u0 strongly.
  (T2) u in C^infinity((0,infinity) x T^3).
  (T3) div u=0 and dt u+(u dot grad)u=nu*Delta u, with p=0.
  (T4) u(t) in S_DIR_L2 for all t>=0 (same direction a).
  (T5) ||u(t)||_2^2+2*nu*int_0^t||grad u(s)||_2^2 ds=||u0||_2^2.
  (T6) int_0^infinity ||u(t)||_{L^infinity(T^3)}^2 dt
         <= [45/(8*pi*nu)] ||u0||_2^2.
  (T7) u is the unique periodic three-dimensional Leray--Hopf solution
       with initial datum u0 (not merely uniqueness within the shear sector).
  (T8) Under the separately specified Fourier-nonlinearity M7 realization
       in Section 7, M7(delta,T)=0 for every 0<=delta<T<infinity.
  (T9) S_DIR_L2 has nonempty intersection with
       L2_{sigma,0}(T^3)\H^{1/2}_{sigma,0}(T^3).

1. EXACT FOURIER SUPPORT OF THE INITIAL DATA
-------------------------------------------
Take a nonzero a in Z^3 and phi in L2_0(T^3;R) with a dot grad phi=0
in D'(T^3).  Write

  u0=a*phi,
  phi(x)=sum_{k in Z^3} c_k exp(2*pi*i*k dot x)

in the L2 Fourier sense.  Mean zero gives c_0=0.  Testing the directional
constraint against the k-th Fourier character gives

  (2*pi*i)(a dot k)c_k=0.

Hence c_k=0 whenever a dot k != 0.  Define the lattice

  Lambda_a = {k in Z^3 : a dot k=0},
  Lambda_a^* = Lambda_a\{0}.

Since a is a nonzero integer vector, Lambda_a is a rank-two lattice in the
Euclidean plane P_a={x in R^3:a dot x=0}.  Its distinct points have mutual
Euclidean separation at least 1.  No primitivity assumption on a is needed.

The vector coefficients b_k=a*c_k satisfy

  support(b) subset Lambda_a^*,
  sum_{k in Lambda_a^*}|b_k|^2=||u0||_2^2

by Parseval, using the unit-volume torus convention.

2. HEAT CONSTRUCTION AND NONLINEAR CANCELLATION
-----------------------------------------------
Define, for t>=0,

  theta(t,x)=sum_{k in Lambda_a^*}
    exp(-4*pi^2*nu*|k|^2*t) c_k exp(2*pi*i*k dot x),
  u(t,x)=a*theta(t,x).

At t=0, convergence is in L2; for every t>0 it is C^infinity.  The
Fourier multipliers preserve the support condition a dot k=0, hence

  a dot grad theta(t)=0,
  div u(t)=a dot grad theta(t)=0,
  (u dot grad)u=theta*(a dot grad theta)*a=0.

The heat coefficients satisfy

  dt b_k(t)=-4*pi^2*nu*|k|^2*b_k(t).

Therefore

  dt u+(u dot grad)u=nu*Delta u, p=0,

in the distributional sense at t=0 and classically for t>0.  The same
structural constraint remains valid for each t>=0 in distributions, so
u(t) belongs to S_DIR_L2.

3. STRONG L2 TRACE, SMOOTHNESS, ENERGY EQUALITY
-----------------------------------------------
Dominated convergence gives

  ||u(t)-u0||_2^2
  =sum_k |b_k|^2 |exp(-4*pi^2*nu*|k|^2*t)-1|^2 ->0

as t decreases to zero.  Continuity at any nonnegative time follows by
the same argument.

For any delta>0 and integers j>=0 and multiindices alpha, the formal
space-time derivative has Fourier coefficients of absolute value bounded by

  C(j,alpha,nu) * |k|^{2*j+|alpha|}
     * exp(-4*pi^2*nu*delta*|k|^2)|b_k|.

The sum of these bounds is finite by Cauchy--Schwarz and Gaussian lattice
summability.  Thus all mixed derivatives converge uniformly for t>=delta
on compact time intervals.  As delta>0 is arbitrary,

  u in C^infinity((0,infinity) x T^3).

Tonelli on nonnegative Fourier energy densities yields

  2*nu*int_0^t ||grad u(s)||_2^2 ds
  =sum_{k!=0} |b_k|^2 (1-exp(-8*pi^2*nu*|k|^2*t)).

Adding ||u(t)||_2^2=
  sum_{k!=0}|b_k|^2 exp(-8*pi^2*nu*|k|^2*t)
proves the exact energy equality T5.  Therefore u is a Leray--Hopf
solution (in particular, L^infinity_t L2_x intersect L2_t H1_x).

4. UNIFORM RANK-TWO LATTICE ANNULUS BOUND
----------------------------------------
Fix any a!=0 in Z^3.  For integer j>=1 set

  A_j={k in Lambda_a : j<=|k|<j+1}.

Every two distinct points of Lambda_a have distance >=1.  Therefore the
open disks of radius 1/3 about the points in A_j, drawn within P_a, are
pairwise disjoint.  These disks lie in the planar annulus

  j-1/3 <= |x| < j+4/3.

A comparison of two-dimensional Euclidean areas gives

  #A_j * pi/9
    <= pi*((j+4/3)^2-(j-1/3)^2)
     = pi*(5/3)*(2*j+1),

so

  #A_j <= 15*(2*j+1) <= 45*j.                    (4.1)

The estimate is uniform in the integer direction a.  In particular,
this derivation does not require any bound on the lattice covolume.

5. WEIGHTED SCHUR ESTIMATE AND SHARPLY SCOPED SERRIN ENDPOINT
------------------------------------------------------------
For k,l in Lambda_a^* let

  K(k,l)=1/(|k|^2+|l|^2),       h(k)=1/|k|.

For r=|k|>=1, partition the l sum into A_j.  By (4.1),

  sum_{l in Lambda_a^*} K(k,l)*h(l)
  <= sum_{j>=1} (#A_j)/[j*(r^2+j^2)]
  <=45*sum_{j>=1}1/(r^2+j^2)
  <=45*int_0^infinity dx/(r^2+x^2)
   =45*pi/(2*r)
   =(45*pi/2)*h(k).                              (5.1)

Here the integral comparison applies because
x |->1/(r^2+x^2) is positive and decreasing for x>=0.

The kernel K is nonnegative and symmetric.  The weighted Schur test,
first applied to finite subsets and then extended by monotone limits,
therefore gives

  sum_{k,l in Lambda_a^*} |b_k||b_l| K(k,l)
   <=(45*pi/2)*sum_{k in Lambda_a^*}|b_k|^2.    (5.2)

For every t>0 the heat Fourier series converges absolutely, yielding

  ||u(t)||_infinity
   <=sum_{k in Lambda_a^*}|b_k|exp(-4*pi^2*nu*|k|^2*t).

Square and integrate.  Tonelli permits the exchange of nonnegative sums
and the time integral, giving

  int_0^infinity ||u(t)||_infinity^2 dt
  <=sum_{k,l}|b_k||b_l|
      *int_0^infinity exp(-4*pi^2*nu*(|k|^2+|l|^2)*t)dt
  =[1/(4*pi^2*nu)]*sum_{k,l}|b_k||b_l| K(k,l)
  <=[1/(4*pi^2*nu)]*(45*pi/2)*||u0||_2^2
  =[45/(8*pi*nu)]*||u0||_2^2.                  (5.3)

This proves the GLOBAL, direction-uniform estimate

  ||u||_{L2((0,infinity);L-infinity(T^3))}^2
    <=(45/(8*pi*nu))||u0||_2^2.

The exponents satisfy the 3D Prodi--Serrin equality

  2/2+3/infinity=1.

Mean-zero is essential for this global-in-time bound: a nonzero constant
mode would give a non-decaying contribution and infinite L2_t L-infinity
norm over (0,infinity).

The numerical constant above is explicit but not claimed optimal.

6. UNRESTRICTED THREE-DIMENSIONAL LERAY--HOPF UNIQUENESS
------------------------------------------------------
Let v be any three-dimensional periodic Leray--Hopf solution with v(0)=u0.
Let w=v-u.  The heat solution u satisfies the energy equality and is in
L2_t H1_x intersect L2_t L-infinity_x, with strong L2 initial trace.
Standard time-mollification and spatial approximation of u justify it as
a comparison field in the weak relative-energy identity for v.

The relative-energy inequality (using div u=div v=0) is

  (1/2)||w(t)||_2^2+nu*int_0^t||grad w(s)||_2^2 ds
    <=int_0^t |int_{T^3}(w dot grad)u dot w dx|ds.

For almost every s, incompressibility of w permits integration by parts:

  int (w dot grad)u dot w
      =-int (w dot grad)w dot u.

Holder's and Young's inequalities imply

  |int (w dot grad)u dot w|
      <=||u||_infinity ||w||_2 ||grad w||_2
      <=(nu/2)||grad w||_2^2
        +(1/(2*nu))||u||_infinity^2||w||_2^2.

Therefore

  ||w(t)||_2^2
    <=(1/nu)*int_0^t||u(s)||_infinity^2||w(s)||_2^2 ds.

The Gronwall coefficient belongs to L1(0,T) for every T by (5.3), and
||w(0)||_2=0.  Integral Gronwall gives ||w(t)||_2=0 for every t>=0,
interpreted first almost everywhere and then by weak continuity.
Hence v=u and uniqueness holds among ALL 3D Leray--Hopf solutions with
this datum, not only among shear representatives.

This uniqueness conclusion is specific to the S_DIR_L2 data class; it
does not assert uniqueness for arbitrary 3D L2 divergence-free data.

7. EXACT FOURIER-NONLINEARITY M7 ZERO-CERTIFICATE
------------------------------------------------
Define B(f,g)=P((f dot grad)g), the Leray-projected NSE nonlinearity.
For the established family,

  B(u(t),u(t))=0 for all t>0.

Let u^(m) be the Fourier/Galerkin projection of the heat orbit to a finite
frequency set (or its heat evolution from a Fourier-truncated datum).
A Fourier multiplier preserves both the fixed polarization a and the
support constraint a dot k=0.  Thus

  B(u^(m)(t),u^(m)(t))=0.                        (7.1)

Consider the project-specific *Fourier-nonlinearity-flux* realization:

  A=-Delta on the mean-zero solenoidal sector,
  P_j=A^(1/2)*Pi_j^(1/2) u^(m),
  Q_j=A^(-1/2)*Pi_j^(1/2) B(u^(m),u^(m)),
  kappa_j=<Q_j,P_j>,
  Phi_N^(m)=sum_{j<=N} kappa_j.

Here Pi_j are the selected spectral projectors; the composite operators
act on t>0, where all terms are smooth.  Equation (7.1) gives

  Q_j=0, kappa_j=0, Phi_N^(m)=0 for every j,N,m,t>0.

For q in (0,1), nonnegative capacity D_N^(m), and the frozen weights

  w_N=(q^(-1/2)-1)*q^(-N/2) >= 0,

consider the discrete Fourier-flux M7 action

  M7_Fourier(delta,T)
   =sup_{m,L} int_delta^T sum_{N=0}^L
      w_N*[max(Phi_N^(m),0)]^2/D_N^(m) dt.

Use the explicitly stated null-numerator convention 0/D=0 for D>=0,
including 0/0=0.  Therefore every integrand is exactly zero, and

  M7_Fourier(delta,T)=0,  for all 0<=delta<T<infinity. (7.2)

SCOPE WARNING: The original BKQR/GP overlapping-tail or radial M7
functional, if defined through a distinct energy current Psi or checker,
needs an independent semantic transfer proving that its flux vanishes
for the same shear-null state.  Equation (7.2) alone does not certify
all alternative M7 encodings or the universal BKQR M7-to-frame transfer.

In particular, "M7_Fourier=0 implies Serrin for arbitrary 3D NSE"
is neither a premise nor a consequence of this proof.  The estimate
(5.3) independently uses the heat identity and rank-two Fourier support.

8. ADDITIONAL POSITIVE-TIME SERRIN-4/6 CERTIFICATE
-------------------------------------------------
For delta>0, define

  M(t)=||(-Delta)^(1/4)u(t)||_2^2,
  D(t)=||(-Delta)^(3/4)u(t)||_2^2.

Since the NSE nonlinearity vanishes,

  dM/dt+2*nu*D=0.

The elementary scalar maximization

  sup_{lambda>=0} sqrt(lambda)*exp(-2*nu*delta*lambda)
   =1/[2*sqrt(e*nu*delta)]

implies

  M(delta) <= ||u0||_2^2/[2*sqrt(e*nu*delta)].

Consequently

  sup_{delta<=t<=T} M(t)+2*nu*int_delta^T D(t)dt <=M(delta).

Using mean-zero periodic H1 -> L6 Sobolev embedding with constant C_S,
and spectral interpolation,

  ||u||_6^4
   <= C_S^4 ||(-Delta)^(1/2)u||_2^4
   <= C_S^4 M(t)D(t).

Integrating,

  int_delta^T ||u(t)||_6^4 dt
   <= [C_S^4/(2*nu)] M(delta)^2
   <= [C_S^4/(8*e*nu^2*delta)] ||u0||_2^4.

Hence u belongs to L4(delta,T;L6(T^3)), and

  2/4+3/6=1.

This is another Prodi--Serrin certificate, but it starts at delta>0.
The endpoint L2(0,infinity;L-infinity) established in Section 5 is
strictly stronger concerning access to the initial time.

9. STRICT-ROUGH NONEMPTY SUBFAMILY
----------------------------------
Take a=e1 and phi depending only on x2:

  phi(x2)=sum_{n>=1}(1/n)*cos(2*pi*n*x2),
  u0=e1*phi.

The Fourier coefficients are 1/(2n) at both +n and -n axis modes.
Thus

  ||u0||_2^2=(1/2)*sum_{n>=1}1/n^2<infinity,

while the critical homogeneous half-Sobolev sum is

  sum_k (2*pi*|k|)*|u0_hat(k)|^2
   =pi*sum_{n>=1}1/n=infinity.

Therefore u0 in S_DIR_L2 \ H^{1/2}_{sigma,0}.  It satisfies every
conclusion T1--T8 for each nu>0.  This is an explicit mathematical witness,
not a formalized physical-space torus-synthesis theorem in Lean.

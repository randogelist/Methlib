==========================================================================
0. EXACT ROOT THEOREM (NO ADDITIONAL ADMISSIONS)
==========================================================================

Definition S_DIR_L2.

  S_DIR_L2 := UNION over a in Z^3 \ {0} of
    { u0 = a phi : phi is REAL in L^2_0(T^3),
                   a . grad(phi) = 0 in distributions }.

Here L^2_0 denotes zero spatial mean, a is a fixed NONZERO integer
vector for each datum, and all spatial integrals use unit-torus measure.

Theorem S_DIR_L2_GLOBAL_REGULARITY.

  For every nu>0 and every u0 in S_DIR_L2, there exists a vector field U
  such that:

  (R1) U(0)=u0 strongly in L^2_sigma,0(T^3), and
       U belongs to C([0,infinity); L^2_sigma,0(T^3));

  (R2) U belongs to C^infinity((0,infinity) x T^3);

  (R3) partial_t U + (U . grad) U + grad p = nu Delta U,
       div U=0, with p=0 on (0,infinity) x T^3;

  (R4) U(t)=exp(nu t Delta) u0 for every t>=0;

  (R5) For every t>=0, the exact energy equality holds:
       ||U(t)||_2^2 + 2 nu integral_0^t ||grad U(s)||_2^2 ds
          = ||u0||_2^2;

  (R6) The GLOBAL critical Serrin estimate holds:
       integral_0^infinity ||U(t)||_infinity^2 dt
          <= [45 / (8 pi nu)] ||u0||_2^2;

  (R7) If V is ANY unforced periodic three-dimensional Leray--Hopf
       solution with initial trace u0, then V=U.

  Consequence: U is the unique Leray--Hopf solution of the same datum,
  and every such solution is smooth at all strictly positive times.

  There is NO smallness, H^(1/2), M7, GP, BKQR, certificate or other
  analytic premise in this theorem. Its initial admissibility restriction
  is exactly u0 in S_DIR_L2. The zero-mean requirement is essential for
  the global-in-time Serrin integral (a nonzero constant flow would have
  a nonintegrable infinite-time L^infinity norm).

==========================================================================
1. COORDINATE/FREQUENCY REPRESENTATION
==========================================================================

Fix nu>0. Take u0=a phi in S_DIR_L2 with
  a=(a1,a2,a3) in Z^3 \ {0},
  phi real-valued, mean zero, L^2(T^3),
  a . grad phi = 0 in D'(T^3).

Use Fourier characters e_k(x)=exp(2 pi i k.x), k in Z^3.
Let c_k=hat(phi)(k), b_k=hat(u0)(k)=a c_k.

Parseval and reality imply
  c_0=0,
  c_{-k}=conj(c_k),
  sum_{k in Z^3}|c_k|^2=||phi||_2^2<infinity,
  sum_k |b_k|^2=||u0||_2^2<infinity.

The directional distributional identity has Fourier transform
  2 pi i (a.k) c_k=0 for each k in Z^3.
Since a.k is an integer, a.k != 0 implies c_k=0.

Define
  P_a={x in R^3: a.x=0},
  Lambda_a=P_a intersection Z^3.

Then c_k and b_k are supported in Lambda_a. Since a!=0, P_a is a
Euclidean plane of dimension 2. Its integer lattice points have mutual
Euclidean distance >=1, independently of the direction a.

ALSO: a.b_k=|a|^2 c_k, whereas k.b_k=(k.a)c_k=0.
This is the precise divergence-free Fourier condition.

==========================================================================
2. PHYSICAL FOURIER SYNTHESIS AND JOINT POSITIVE-TIME SMOOTHNESS
==========================================================================

For t>0 define

  psi(t,x) = SUM_{k in Lambda_a} c_k
                        exp(-4 pi^2 nu |k|^2 t) e_k(x),
  U(t,x) = a psi(t,x).

For t=0 define U(0)=u0 as an L^2-equivalence class.

Let delta>0, and let r be any nonnegative integer. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

  SUM_k |b_k| |k|^r exp(-4 pi^2 nu |k|^2 delta)
   <= (SUM_k |b_k|^2)^(1/2)
      (SUM_k |k|^(2r) exp(-8 pi^2 nu |k|^2 delta))^(1/2)
   < infinity.

The last sum converges even when taken over ALL Z^3, since Gaussian
spatial decay dominates every polynomial.

The Fourier coefficient of partial_t^q partial_x^beta U is
  (-4 pi^2 nu |k|^2)^q (2 pi i k)^beta b_k
  exp(-4 pi^2 nu |k|^2 t).

Thus for every q>=0 and every spatial multi-index beta the differentiated
Fourier series converges uniformly on [delta,T] x T^3 for every T>delta.
By the Weierstrass test and termwise differentiation,

  U in C^infinity((0,infinity) x T^3).

The Gaussian multiplier is real and even, so U is real, and
  hat(U(t))(-k)=conj(hat(U(t))(k)).
The zero mode remains zero.

Since k.b_k=0, distributional div U(t)=0, and since U(t) is smooth
for t>0, div U(t,x)=0 pointwise.

Since a.k c_k=0 at every frequency,
  a.grad psi(t,x)=0 for t>0.

Consequently
  (U.grad)U
   = (a psi . grad)(a psi)
   = a psi (a.grad psi)
   = 0.

Termwise heat differentiation gives
  partial_t U = nu Delta U
on (0,infinity) x T^3.
Hence with p=0,
  partial_t U + (U.grad)U + grad p = nu Delta U,
  div U=0.

The directional structure and zero mean persist for every t>0:
  U(t)=a psi(t),  a.grad psi(t)=0,  integral psi(t)=0.
Thus S_DIR_L2 is invariant under the explicit NSE evolution.

==========================================================================
3. STRONG L^2 TRACE AND GLOBAL ENERGY EQUALITY
==========================================================================

By Parseval, for t>=0,

  ||U(t)-u0||_2^2
    = SUM_k |b_k|^2 |exp(-4 pi^2 nu |k|^2 t)-1|^2.

Each summand tends to zero as t downarrow 0 and is bounded by
4|b_k|^2, a summable majorant. Dominated convergence for series gives
  lim_{t downarrow 0} ||U(t)-u0||_2 = 0.

The same argument at arbitrary t0>=0 proves
  U in C([0,infinity);L^2_sigma,0(T^3)).

For t>0 Parseval gives
  ||U(t)||_2^2
    = SUM_{k != 0} |b_k|^2 exp(-8 pi^2 nu |k|^2 t),

and
  ||grad U(t)||_2^2
    = SUM_{k != 0} 4 pi^2 |k|^2 |b_k|^2
                    exp(-8 pi^2 nu |k|^2 t).

Tonelli's theorem applies to the latter nonnegative series. For any T>0,

  2 nu INT_0^T ||grad U(t)||_2^2 dt
    = SUM_{k != 0} |b_k|^2
                     [1-exp(-8 pi^2 nu |k|^2 T)].

Adding the L^2 norm identity yields

  ||U(T)||_2^2 + 2 nu INT_0^T ||grad U(t)||_2^2 dt
    = SUM_{k != 0}|b_k|^2=||u0||_2^2.

This proves the exact Leray--Hopf energy equality, hence the energy
inequality, and the existence of an admissible global Leray--Hopf
solution U of the unforced periodic three-dimensional equations.

Letting T tend to infinity also gives

  INT_0^infinity ||grad U(t)||_2^2 dt
     = ||u0||_2^2 / (2 nu),

since the mean-zero Fourier support has no k=0 term.

==========================================================================
4. GEOMETRIC LEMMA: UNIFORM ANNULAR LATTICE COUNT
==========================================================================

For integer j>=1 set

  A_j(a)={ k in Lambda_a : j <= |k| < j+1 },
  N_j(a)=#A_j(a).

CLAIM (uniform in a != 0):

  N_j(a) <= 30j+15 <=45j.

PROOF.

The points k in Lambda_a are distinct points of Z^3; hence the distance
between any two distinct points is >=1. Consider, inside the two-
dimensional Euclidean plane P_a, the open disks B_{P_a}(k,1/3).
These disks are pairwise disjoint and have planar area pi/9.

For k in A_j(a), any x in B_{P_a}(k,1/3) obeys

  j-1/3 < |x| < j+4/3.

Thus all N_j(a) disjoint disks lie in the planar annulus bounded by
radii j-1/3 and j+4/3. Planar area comparison implies

  N_j(a) pi/9
   <= pi[(j+4/3)^2-(j-1/3)^2]
   = pi[(10/3)j+(5/3)].

Therefore N_j(a)<=30j+15<=45j for every j>=1. QED.

The constants are independent of direction, primitiveness of a, angle
of P_a or covolume of Lambda_a. No coordinate-choice-dependent lattice
basis or number-theoretic bound is needed.

==========================================================================
5. GEOMETRIC LEMMA: UNIFORM WEIGHTED SCHUR ROW SUM
==========================================================================

For each k in Lambda_a \ {0}, put r=|k|>=1. Then

  SUM_{l in Lambda_a \ {0}}
    [1/(|l| (r^2+|l|^2))]

  = SUM_{j>=1} SUM_{l in A_j(a)}
      [1/(|l| (r^2+|l|^2))]

  <= SUM_{j>=1} N_j(a) / [j(r^2+j^2)]

  <= 45 SUM_{j>=1} 1/(r^2+j^2).

Since x |-> 1/(r^2+x^2) is decreasing on [0,infinity),

  SUM_{j>=1} 1/(r^2+j^2)
   <= INT_0^infinity dx/(r^2+x^2)
   = pi/(2r).

Therefore

  SUM_{l in Lambda_a \ {0}}
     1/(|l| (|k|^2+|l|^2))
  <= (45 pi)/(2|k|).

Define for nonzero frequencies

  K(k,l)=1/(|k|^2+|l|^2),
  w(k)=1/|k|,
  C_S=45 pi/2.

Then K(k,l)=K(l,k)>=0 and

  SUM_{l != 0} K(k,l)w(l) <= C_S w(k).

This is the exact uniform Schur row hypothesis.

==========================================================================
6. FINITE AND INFINITE WEIGHTED SCHUR INEQUALITY
==========================================================================

Take any finite F subset Lambda_a \ {0} and any nonnegative b_k.
For positive weights w(k), the elementary square inequality yields

  2b_k b_l <= b_k^2 w(l)/w(k) + b_l^2 w(k)/w(l).

Multiply by K(k,l), sum over k,l in F, use symmetry of K, and apply
the row inequality from section 5:

  SUM_{k,l in F} K(k,l)b_k b_l
  <= SUM_{k in F} [b_k^2/w(k)]
                     SUM_{l in F}K(k,l)w(l)
  <= C_S SUM_{k in F}b_k^2.

For infinite b in ell^2(Lambda_a \ {0}), take an increasing exhaustion
by finite sets F_n. The terms are nonnegative, and monotone convergence
implies

  SUM_{k,l in Lambda_a \ {0}} K(k,l)b_k b_l
     <= (45 pi/2) SUM_{k != 0} b_k^2.

This completes the uniform rank-two Schur bound. Its infinite-sum
passage depends only on positivity and monotone convergence, not on any
assumed convergence of the unbounded double sum.

==========================================================================
7. GLOBAL CRITICAL SERRIN ESTIMATE FROM t=0
==========================================================================

Let b_k=|hat(u0)(k)|>=0. Parseval gives

  SUM_{k in Lambda_a} b_k^2=||u0||_2^2.

Since U(t) has absolutely convergent Fourier series for t>0,

  ||U(t)||_{L^infinity(T^3)}
    <= SUM_{k in Lambda_a \ {0}}
         b_k exp(-4 pi^2 nu |k|^2 t).

The right side is finite for every t>0 by the Gaussian Cauchy--Schwarz
bound from section 2. Expand its square and use Tonelli (all terms
nonnegative). Since |k|,|l|>=1,

  INT_0^infinity [SUM_{k != 0} b_k exp(-4 pi^2 nu |k|^2 t)]^2 dt

    = SUM_{k,l != 0} b_k b_l
           INT_0^infinity
             exp(-4 pi^2 nu (|k|^2+|l|^2)t) dt

    = [1/(4 pi^2 nu)]
      SUM_{k,l != 0}
        b_k b_l/(|k|^2+|l|^2)

    <= [1/(4 pi^2 nu)] [45 pi/2]
            SUM_{k != 0}b_k^2

    = [45/(8 pi nu)] ||u0||_2^2.

Consequently,

  INT_0^infinity ||U(t)||_infinity^2 dt
     <= [45/(8 pi nu)] ||u0||_2^2 < infinity.

This is the scale-critical three-dimensional Prodi--Serrin pair
  (time exponent p, spatial exponent q)=(2,infinity),
  2/p+3/q=1.

The estimate is UNIFORM over all integer directions a!=0 and over
arbitrary amplitudes ||u0||_2. No H^(1/2) smallness is assumed.

Crucially, the integration is from the rough trace t=0, NOT just from
positive times delta>0. Absolute Fourier summability was used only
for t>0, while Tonelli controlled the improper integral down to t=0.

==========================================================================
8. UNRESTRICTED LERAY--HOPF UNIQUENESS
==========================================================================

Let V be an arbitrary periodic three-dimensional Leray--Hopf solution
of the unforced NSE with the SAME datum u0, and define W=V-U.

The Leray--Hopf bounds give
  V in L^infinity_t L^2_x intersection L^2_t H^1_x;
  U in L^infinity_t L^2_x intersection L^2_t H^1_x;
  W in L^infinity_t L^2_x intersection L^2_t H^1_x.

By section 7,
  U in L^2(0,T;L^infinity_x) for every T<infinity.

8.1. Cross-energy identity at the rough trace.

The identity can be justified without assuming U(0) in H^1.
Introduce the heat-shift tests
  U_epsilon(t)=U(t+epsilon), epsilon>0.
They are smooth on [0,T] x T^3, solenoidal, and solve the heat equation.

Testing the weak equation for V against U_epsilon yields

  (V(t),U_epsilon(t))-(u0,U_epsilon(0))
     = INT_0^t INT_{T^3}
           (V tensor V):grad U_epsilon dx ds
       -2 nu INT_0^t
           (grad V,grad U_epsilon) ds.

For every finite T, as epsilon downarrow 0,
  U_epsilon(0) -> u0 in L^2;
  U_epsilon(t) -> U(t) in L^2 for each t>=0;
  grad U_epsilon -> grad U in L^2(0,T;L^2).

Moreover, for t>0, the heat L^infinity contraction gives
  ||U(t+epsilon)||_infinity <= ||U(t)||_infinity,
and U(t+epsilon)->U(t) uniformly in x.
Dominated convergence therefore gives
  U_epsilon -> U in L^2(0,T;L^infinity).

For the convective integral, periodic integration by parts (div V=0)
gives, for smooth tests G,
  INT (V tensor V):grad G = -INT G . (V.grad)V.
The difference between the epsilon term and its limit is bounded by
  ||U_epsilon-U||_{L^2_t L^infinity_x}
  ||V||_{L^infinity_t L^2_x}
  ||grad V||_{L^2_t L^2_x},
which tends to zero.

Thus the cross-energy identity extends to U from initial time zero:

  (V(t),U(t))-||u0||_2^2
     = INT_0^t INT (V tensor V):grad U
       -2 nu INT_0^t (grad V,grad U).

8.2. Relative-energy inequality.

Combine this identity with the energy equality of U and the energy
inequality of V. The usual polarization gives

  ||W(t)||_2^2 + 2 nu INT_0^t ||grad W||_2^2 ds
    <= -2 INT_0^t INT (V.grad U).V dx ds.

Since (U.grad)U=0, V=U+W, and div W=0,

  INT (V.grad U).V
      = INT (W.grad U).W.

Indeed, the additional term INT(W.grad U).U equals
  (1/2) INT W.grad |U|^2 = 0 by periodic integration by parts.

Integrate the remaining term by parts using div W=0:

  -INT (W.grad U).W
      = INT U.(W.grad)W.

Therefore

  ||W(t)||_2^2 + 2 nu INT_0^t ||grad W||_2^2
     <= 2 INT_0^t
         ||U(s)||_infinity ||W(s)||_2 ||grad W(s)||_2 ds.

Young's inequality gives

  2 ||U||_infinity ||W||_2 ||grad W||_2
     <= nu ||grad W||_2^2
        + nu^(-1) ||U||_infinity^2 ||W||_2^2.

Thus for almost every t (and then in the canonical Leray--Hopf
representative sense),

  ||W(t)||_2^2
     <= nu^(-1) INT_0^t
          ||U(s)||_infinity^2 ||W(s)||_2^2 ds.

Since W(0)=0 in L^2 and ||U||_infinity^2 belongs to L^1(0,T),
Volterra--Gronwall implies
  ||W(t)||_2^2=0 for every t>=0.

Hence V=U. No uniqueness-in-the-shear-sector restriction was imposed
on V: it was any three-dimensional Leray--Hopf solution of the same
datum. This proves (R7).

9. STRICTLY ROUGH SUBCLASS IS NONEMPTY
==========================================================================

Take a=e1 and define
  phi(x1,x2,x3) = 2 SUM_{n>=1} cos(2 pi n x2)/n,
where convergence is in L^2(T^3).

The nonzero Fourier coefficients are
  hat(phi)(0,n,0)=1/n,
  hat(phi)(0,-n,0)=1/n,  n>=1.

Therefore
  ||phi||_2^2 = 2 SUM_{n>=1} 1/n^2 < infinity,
  phi has mean zero,
  partial_{x1}phi=0 distributionally.

However, using the homogeneous H^(1/2) Fourier norm,

  ||phi||_{dot H^(1/2)}^2
    = SUM_{k !=0} 2 pi |k| |hat(phi)(k)|^2
    = 4 pi SUM_{n>=1} 1/n
    = infinity.

Thus
  u0=e1 phi belongs to S_DIR_L2,
  u0 is in L^2_sigma,0(T^3),
  u0 is NOT in H^(1/2)_sigma,0(T^3).

Nevertheless the full main theorem applies, including positive-time
C^infinity regularity, global L^2_tL^infinity_x, energy equality, and
unrestricted three-dimensional Leray--Hopf uniqueness.

This proves
  S_DIR_L2 \ H^(1/2)_sigma,0 is nonempty.

No statement that ALL of S_DIR_L2 is outside H^(1/2) is made. The full
class contains both smooth and strictly rough admissible data.

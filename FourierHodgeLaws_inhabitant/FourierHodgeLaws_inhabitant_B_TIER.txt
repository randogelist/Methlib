1. COORDINATE, MEASURE, AND SCALAR/VECTOR ADAPTERS
------------------------------------------------

Unit coordinate periodicity extends to every integer translation by induction
and substitution x -> x-e_j. Hence a continuous periodic scalar component
descends continuously to (R/Z)^3 with normalized Haar measure.

The standard basis of EuclideanSpace R (Fin 3) is orthonormal; coordinate
Lebesgue measure is product Lebesgue measure. The coordinate map to the Pi
space preserves these measures. No equality between the Euclidean norm and
the default Pi maximum norm is asserted. The comparison is

  |x|_infinity <= |x|_2 <= sqrt(3) |x|_infinity.

The difference of [0,1]^3 and (0,1]^3 is contained in finitely many coordinate
hyperplanes. Each is null by product measure and the nullity of real singletons.
Consequently both domains give the same integral for every integrable input.
This identifies the physical coefficient integral below with
UnitAddTorus.mFourierCoeff_eq_integral after character identification.

Scalar and vector Fourier calculations are componentwise compatible because
coordinate projections and real part are continuous linear maps and therefore
commute with Bochner integrals and summable series. If a vector Methlib endpoint
is used for scalar f, define iota_j(f)(x)=f(x)e_j, apply the vector endpoint,
and project onto component j. Smoothness and periodicity of iota_j(f) follow
by continuous linear composition. No divergence condition is needed for the
selected derivative and decay endpoints.

2. SPATIAL FOURIER-HODGE DERIVATION
---------------------------------
A00. EXACT SPATIAL OBJECTS

Q = [0,1]^3; |Q| = 1; Frequency = Z^3; E = R or R^3.
All means are unnormalized Lebesgue integrals over Q, equal to averages because |Q|=1.
f is C^infinity on R^3 and unit-periodic in each coordinate.
lambda(0)=0; lambda(k)=(4*pi^2*|k|^2)^(-1), k != 0.

L f(x) = sum_{k in Z^3} lambda(k) integral_Q cos(2*pi*k.(x-y)) f(y) dy.
For E=R this is scalarL. For E=R^3 this is vectorL.
K B = curl(vectorL B); U_m B = m + K B.
G_{nu,m}(B) = B cross U_m B + nu curl B.
Q_{nu,m}(B) = div G_{nu,m}(B).
headPotential_{nu,m}(B) = scalarL(Q_{nu,m}(B)).

A01. PERIODIC INTEGRATION AND FOURIER DECAY

e_k(x)=exp(2*pi*i*k.x).
fhat(k)=integral_Q exp(-2*pi*i*k.y) f(y) dy, componentwise in the complexification.

For every multi-index alpha, partial^alpha f is periodic: differentiate the identity
f(x+e_j)=f(x). For each coordinate j and periodic C^1 scalar g,

  integral_Q partial_j g = 0.

Indeed, Fubini and the one-dimensional fundamental theorem of calculus give the
integral of g at the two opposite faces, which cancel by periodicity.
Integration by parts, with the same face cancellation, gives

  (partial_j f)^hat(k) = 2*pi*i*k_j fhat(k),
  ((1-Delta)^N f)^hat(k) = (1+4*pi^2*|k|^2)^N fhat(k).

Consequently

  ||fhat(k)|| <= A_N(f) (1+4*pi^2*|k|^2)^(-N),
  A_N(f) = integral_Q ||(1-Delta)^N f|| < infinity.

The bound applies to real scalar functions and to real vector fields componentwise
(or directly using the finite-dimensional complex norm and the integral triangle inequality).

For q>3, sum_k (1+|k|)^(-q)<infinity. One direct proof uses max-norm shells:
#{k: |k|_infinity=n}=(2n+1)^3-(2n-1)^3=24n^2+2 for n>=1;
Euclidean norm dominates max norm, so comparison with sum_n n^(2-q) applies.
It follows that sum_k (1+|k|)^r ||fhat(k)||<infinity for every integer r>=0.

A02. FOURIER INVERSION WITHOUT AN UNRESOLVED INVERSION ASSUMPTION

Absolute summability makes s(x)=sum_k e_k(x) fhat(k) uniformly convergent.
Orthogonality integral_Q e_k e_{-l}=delta_{kl} and uniform convergence imply
s^hat(l)=fhat(l). Thus h=f-s is continuous, periodic, and all its Fourier
coefficients vanish.

To prove h=0, use the one-dimensional Fejer kernel

  Phi_n(t)=(1/n)|sum_{r=0}^{n-1} exp(2*pi*i*r*t)|^2, n>=1,

and the product kernel Psi_n(y)=product_{j=1}^3 Phi_n(y_j).
Phi_n>=0, integral_[0,1] Phi_n=1, and for torus-distance(t,0)>=delta>0,
Phi_n(t)<=1/(n sin^2(pi*delta)) (take 0<delta<=1/2).
Hence Psi_n has integral one and its integral outside the coordinate delta-box
tends to zero by a union bound over the three coordinate complements.
Uniform continuity of h on the torus yields Psi_n*h -> h uniformly.
Each Psi_n is a finite trigonometric polynomial, and every Fourier coefficient
of h is zero; therefore Psi_n*h=0 for all n. Thus h=0.

Therefore f(x)=sum_k e_k(x) fhat(k) for every x. This argument also proves
Fourier uniqueness for every continuous periodic scalar or finite-dimensional
vector function. No PDE existence statement is used.

A03. IDENTIFICATION OF THE FROZEN REAL COSINE OPERATOR

For real f,

  integral_Q cos(2*pi*k.(x-y)) f(y) dy = Re(e_k(x) fhat(k)).

The coefficient bound in (A01) proves absolute summability of the defining real
series, uniformly in x. Continuous real-linear maps commute with summable sums,
so

  Lf(x) = Re sum_k lambda(k)e_k(x)fhat(k).

Because lambda(-k)=lambda(k) and fhat(-k)=conj(fhat(k)), the complex sum on the
right is real and Lf=sum_k lambda(k)e_k fhat(k). Equivalently, the cosine term
is one half of the k exponential term plus one half of the -k exponential term;
reindexing the second full-lattice sum proves the same identity.

NORMALIZATION: the sum already runs over all k and -k. No additional factor 2
may be inserted. A single cosine input at a nonzero frequency receives exactly
the multiplier lambda(k).

A04. SMOOTH MULTIPLIER CALCULUS

For |alpha|=r and k!=0,

  ||partial^alpha[lambda(k)e_k(x)fhat(k)]||
    <= (2*pi)^r |k|^r lambda(k) A_N(f)(1+4*pi^2|k|^2)^(-N)
    <= C_{r,N} A_N(f) (1+|k|)^(-2N-2+r).

The k=0 term is zero. Choose 2N>r+1. The lattice bound in (A01) is summable,
uniformly in x. Every differentiated series converges uniformly. Repeated
termwise differentiation gives Lf in C^infinity, spatially periodic, and

  partial^alpha Lf = L(partial^alpha f),
  mean(Lf)=0,
  -Delta Lf=f-mean(f).

The first equality follows also by comparing the absolutely convergent Fourier
series using the derivative-coefficient identity in (A01). The second follows
from termwise integration and lambda(0)=0. The third follows from
4*pi^2|k|^2 lambda(k)=1 for k!=0 and inversion (A02).

Finite-dimensional coordinate projections commute with the integrals and sums,
so the scalar/vector forms are compatible. In particular

  div(vectorL F)=scalarL(div F),
  curl(vectorL F)=vectorL(curl F),
  grad(scalarL f)=vectorL(grad f).

Linearity and L(0)=0 hold on these smooth periodic inputs.

A05. GENERAL SPATIAL HODGE IDENTITY

For every smooth periodic real vector field F, smoothness from (A04) justifies
the standard coordinate identity curl curl H = grad div H - Delta H.
Using the derivative commutations in (A04),

  K(curl F)
    = curl(vectorL(curl F))
    = curl curl(vectorL F)
    = grad(scalarL(div F)) - Delta(vectorL F)
    = F - mean(F) + grad(scalarL(div F)).                     [HODGE]

Likewise

  curl(KF)=F-mean(F)+grad(scalarL(div F)),
  div(KF)=0,
  mean(KF)=0.

The divergence identity is div curl=0, using equality of mixed second
derivatives of vectorL F. The mean identity follows from periodic integration
of the first derivatives comprising curl. These are proved directly; they do
not use any field of FourierHodgeLaws.

A06. H1: RECONSTRUCT_VELOCITY

Let Data(a), i.e. a smooth, periodic, div(a)=0. Then HODGE gives

  K(curl a)=a-mean(a),
  U_{mean(a)}(curl a)=mean(a)+a-mean(a)=a.

This is the exact field equality required by reconstruct_velocity. It is
pointwise on all R^3, and mean(a) is unrestricted.

A07. H2: RECONSTRUCT_CURL, PLUS DIRECT AUXILIARY CONCLUSIONS

Let MagneticData(B), i.e. B smooth, periodic, div(B)=0, mean(B)=0, and let m in R^3.
By (A04) U_m B is smooth and periodic. By (A05),

  curl(U_m B)=curl(KB)=B,
  div(U_m B)=0,
  mean(U_m B)=m.

The last identity uses |Q|=1 and mean(KB)=0. None of these statements uses H1,
canonical_smoothness, a solution trajectory, or an inhabitant of FourierHodgeLaws.
This proves the exact reconstruct_curl field and supplies an independent
spatial route to reconstructed admissibility and the harmonic-mean identity.

A08. H4: ELECTRIC_HODGE

Let nu in R, m in R^3, MagneticData(B), and u=U_m B.
By (A07), curl u=B, div u=0, and u is smooth periodic. Hence G=B cross u+nu curl B
is smooth periodic. The right-handed component conventions in Geometry.lean give

  (curl u) cross u = (u.nabla)u - grad(|u|^2/2).              [LAMB]

For each component i, periodic integration by parts gives

  integral_Q [(u.nabla)u]_i
    = sum_j integral_Q u_j partial_j u_i
    = - integral_Q u_i sum_j partial_j u_j
    = 0.

Also integral_Q grad(|u|^2/2)=0 and integral_Q curl B=0.
Consequently mean(G)=0, for every real nu and every m; no smallness, positivity,
mean restriction on u, or evolution equation occurs.

Apply HODGE to F=G:

  K(curl G)=G+grad(scalarL(div G))
           =G+grad(headPotential_{nu,m}(B)).

Reversing the displayed equality gives exactly electric_hodge.

SIGN CHECK: -Delta L=identity-minus-mean. Therefore the gradient term in HODGE
has a PLUS sign. At k!=0,

  hhat(k)=[headPotential]^hat(k)
    =lambda(k) (2*pi*i*k.Ghat(k))
    =i*(k.Ghat(k))/(2*pi*|k|^2),
  (grad h)^hat(k)=-k*(k.Ghat(k))/|k|^2.

Thus G+grad h is its transverse projection. The frozen canonical pressure
p=h-|u|^2/2+mean(|u|^2)/2 has mean zero and the required scalar sign.


3. JOINT PARAMETER AND BOUNDARY DERIVATION
------------------------------------------

STRONGER PARAMETER LEMMA
Let E = R or R^3, S = [0,infinity) x R^3, O = (0,infinity) x R^3.
Let F : R x R^3 -> E be ContDiffOn R infinity on S and spatially
Z^3-periodic at every nonnegative time. No conditions are imposed at t<0.
Then H(t,x) = L(F(t,.))(x), with the exact frozen cosine-kernel operator,
is ContDiffOn R infinity on S and spatially periodic there.
Neither div F=0 nor mean F=0 is a premise.

B01. INPUT JETS
S is convex with nonempty interior and hence UniqueDiffOn R S.
The finite-order consequences of ContDiffOn infinity give continuous,
compatible multilinear jets on S. Their contractions along time and
space coordinate vectors define partial jets F_(r,alpha) on S. For t>0
these are ordinary mixed derivatives. For t=0 these are within jets;
spatial slice derivatives are ordinary spatial derivatives because each
entire spatial slice lies in S. Unit translations preserve S, so periodicity
of every partial jet follows by differentiating the input periodicity
identity within S. This includes time jets at zero by UniqueDiffOn.

B02. COEFFICIENT JETS AND UNIFORM DECAY
For k in Z^3 and r>=0 define
 A_r(t,k) = integral_Q exp(-2 pi i k.y) F_(r,0)(t,y) dy,
with componentwise complexification for E=R^3. Compactness of [0,T] x Q
and continuity of every input jet give, for all finite T>=0 and integers r,N>=0,
 M_(T,r,N) = sup_{0<=t<=T} integral_Q
             |(1-Delta_y)^N F_(r,0)(t,y)| dy < infinity.
Repeated spatial integration by parts has canceling opposite-face terms:
 (1+4 pi^2 |k|^2)^N A_r(t,k)
 = integral_Q exp(-2 pi i k.y) (1-Delta_y)^N F_(r,0)(t,y) dy.
Thus |A_r(t,k)| <= M_(T,r,N)(1+4 pi^2 |k|^2)^(-N), uniformly on [0,T].
For t>0, differentiation under the compact spatial integral gives
 d_t A_r(t,k)=A_(r+1)(t,k). Each A_r is continuous on [0,infinity),
including zero, by compact domination. This does not assume ambient
differentiability at zero.

B03. FOURIER JETS
Let lambda(0)=0 and lambda(k)=(4 pi^2 |k|^2)^(-1) for k!=0.
For r>=0 and alpha in N^3 define
 H_(r,alpha)(t,x)=Re sum_k lambda(k)(2 pi i k)^alpha
                       exp(2 pi i k.x) A_r(t,k).
For k!=0, each summand is bounded on [0,T] x R^3 by
 C_(T,r,N,alpha)(1+|k|)^(-2N-2+|alpha|).
The k=0 summand is exactly zero. Choosing 2N>|alpha|+1 makes this
majorant summable on Z^3. Hence every H_(r,alpha) is continuous on S by
locally uniform absolute convergence. The same estimate for the next
derivative justifies differentiation on O. Therefore
 d_t H_(r,alpha)=H_(r+1,alpha),
 d_(x_j) H_(r,alpha)=H_(r,alpha+e_j) on O.
The original cosine-integral formula equals H_(0,0) on S by
Re(exp(2 pi i k.(x-y)))=cos(2 pi k.(x-y)), compact integrability,
and the absolute convergence just proved. There is no factor 2.

B04. BOUNDARY DIFFERENTIABILITY
Assemble the order-q mixed partial sums into the symmetric multilinear
jet J_q(t,x). Finite dimensionality turns the component estimates into
norm estimates C_(T,q,N)(1+|k|)^(-2N-2+q); choose 2N>q+1.
Each J_q is continuous on S, and on O its ordinary Frechet derivative is
the currying of J_(q+1). Fix z in S. Continuity of J_(q+1) gives
 fderiv_R J_q(y) -> curry(J_(q+1)(z)) as y -> z within O.
Since O is open and convex, closure O=S, and J_q is continuous on S,
Mathlib's hasFDerivWithinAt_closure_of_tendsto_fderiv applies:
 HasFDerivWithinAt J_q (curry(J_(q+1)(z))) S z.
The global O need not have a single uniform-in-time bound: continuity
and differentiation are established locally using any T>z.time.
Each J_q can be defined arbitrarily outside S; the conclusion is within S.
J_0 need only agree with the exact total cosine operator on S, because
ContDiffOn respects equality on its domain.

B05. ALL FINITE ORDERS, THEN INFINITY
For every n, prove simultaneously for all q that J_q is ContDiffOn R n S.
Base n=0 is continuity. At successor n+1 use the preceding derivative
witness and the induction hypothesis for curry(J_(q+1)); currying is
continuous linear. The exact Mathlib theorem is
 contDiffOn_succ_iff_hasFDerivWithinAt_of_uniqueDiffOn.
Its additional implication (n=omega -> AnalyticOn ...) is vacuous at
every finite natural n; no analytic or C^omega hypothesis is introduced.
Then contDiffOn_infty gives ContDiffOn R infinity J_0 S.
This proves the stronger parameter lemma without a Seeley/Whitney extension.

B06. KINETIC SPATIAL AVERAGE
For any smooth periodic u on S, set e(t,x)=|u(t,x)|^2 and
 a_r(t)=integral_Q partial_t^r e(t,x) dx (within jets at zero).
Compactness gives continuity of a_r on [0,infinity); dominated
differentiation gives a_r'=a_(r+1) on (0,infinity). The same one-dimensional
boundary-extension and finite-order induction prove that a_0 is smooth
within [0,infinity). Its lift (t,x)->a_0(t) is smooth on S.
This argument requires only compact local domination, not any global
time bound or smooth extension to negative time.

B07. EXACT H3 ASSEMBLY
Assume MagneticPath B; only its smooth and periodic fields are used here.
Apply the vector parameter lemma: W(t,x)=vectorL(B(t,.))(x) is smooth on S.
Spatial differentiation preserves this smoothness, so
 u(t,x)=m+curl W(t,x)=canonicalVelocity m B(t,x) is smooth on S.
For each t>=0, W(t,.) is C^infinity on R^3; commuting spatial derivatives
gives div(curl W)=0, hence div u=0. No reconstruction identity is needed.
The smooth functions B,u and curl B give G=B cross u + nu curl B smooth.
Spatial derivative closure gives Q=div G smooth; G and Q are periodic.
Apply the scalar parameter lemma to Q: h=scalarL Q is smooth on S.
The kinetic term |u|^2/2 is smooth, and B06 gives the smoothness of
its canonical mean correction. Therefore the exact frozen formula
 p=h-|u|^2/2+(integral_Q |u|^2)/2
is SmoothPressure. These three conclusions fill CanonicalSmoothness:
 velocity_smooth;
 pressure_smooth;
 velocity_divergence_free.
The proof works for all nu,c in R and m in R^3. Parameter c is absent
from these definitions; neither positivity assumption is needed for H3.
In fact H3 holds for every smooth spatially periodic B, independently
of magnetic mean-zero and divergence-free conditions.

MINIMAL ROUTE AND ADAPTER BOUNDARIES
Route A (Mathlib inversion): M01 + coefficient decay + M05 -> M02 -> spatial inverse Laplacian. Methlib T02/T03 can supply actual derivative/decay endpoints after transparent namespace adapters.
Route B (Methlib uniqueness): T02/T03 + M05 + T04 -> coefficient computation of the frozen cosine sum -> T06. T06 needs equality of actual coefficients, continuity and periodicity only, not an additional l1 premise.
H3 boundary route: M06 on open positive-time slabs + continuous summable jets on closure -> M08 -> M09 at each finite natural order -> M10. M07 by itself is an ambient spatial theorem and is insufficient at t=0.
Both routes require scalar as well as vector identities. T02/T03/T06 are stated for real vector fields. Scalar reuse can be derived by embedding a scalar into a fixed coordinate, or proved directly; do not silently apply the vector theorem to scalarL.

LEMMA 2.1 -- EXACT BERNSTEIN REFINEMENT                             !
  sum_(j=0)^d w_(d,j)=1.
  (d+1)w_(d,j)
    =(d+1-j)w_(d+1,j)+(j+1)w_(d+1,j+1).
  V_(d+1)>=V_d>=1.

PROOF
  sum_j B_(d,j)=1 and ell(1)=1 give the first identity.
  Apply ell to the polynomial degree-elevation identity to obtain the
  second identity. Apply the triangle inequality and sum over j.
  Every next-row absolute weight receives total coefficient d+1,
  including the two boundary cells. Therefore V_d<=V_(d+1).
  Finally 1=|sum_j w_(d,j)|<=V_d.
  QED.

LEMMA 2.2 -- CANCELLATION BUDGET GIVES SUBEXPONENTIAL VARIATION       !
  H^xi_(X,Y) => forall d>=0, V_d<=exp(Y)V_X(d+1)^Y.
  Consequently,
    forall eps>0, exists K>0, forall d>=0, V_d<=K exp(eps d).

PROOF
  For d>=X, the budget rearranges to
    V_(d+1)<=(1+Y/(d+1))V_d.
  Induction gives
    V_d<=V_X product_(j=X)^(d-1)(1+Y/(j+1)).
  The product is <=exp(Y sum_(j=X)^(d-1)1/(j+1))
  and hence <=exp(Y)(d+1)^Y.
  For d<X use V_d<=V_X. The case d=X uses the empty product 1.
  For eps>0 the sequence (d+1)^Y exp(-eps d) is bounded; enlarge the
  resulting constant to a positive K. X=0 and Y=0 are included.
  QED.

LEMMA 2.3 -- ACTUAL OFF-RAY ZERO DETECTOR                            !
  Subexponential(V) => Z(F) subset (-infinity,-1].

PROOF
  Suppose F(rho)=0 and rho is outside (-infinity,-1].
  Since F(0)=1, rho!=0. Define
    tau:=-1/rho;
    B:=|tau|+|1-tau|;
    alpha:=conj(1-tau)/|1-tau|;
    beta:=conj(tau)/|tau|;
    p(t):=alpha(1-t)+beta*t.

  tau is outside [0,1]. Thus tau!=0, tau!=1, B>1,
  |alpha|=|beta|=1, alpha!=beta, and p(tau)=B.

  The exact Bernstein expansion is
    p(t)^d=sum_(j=0)^d alpha^(d-j)beta^j B_(d,j)(t).
  Therefore |ell_C(p^d)|<=V_d.

  Define A(z):=sum_(d>=0)ell_C(p^d)z^d.
  For each r<1 choose eps>0 with r exp(eps)<1. The subexponential
  bound gives uniform absolute convergence for |z|<=r. Hence A is
  holomorphic throughout the unit disk.

  Coefficient expansion near zero, using g(-w)=sum_n u_n w^n, gives
    A(z)=(1-alpha*z)^(-1)g(w(z)),
    w(z):=-(beta-alpha)z/(1-alpha*z).
  The rearrangement is justified for sufficiently small |z| by the
  positive convergence radius of the original analytic germ g.

  On the unit disk, 1-alpha*z never vanishes. Define
    D(z):=(1-alpha*z)A(z)F(w(z))-F'(w(z)).
  D is holomorphic throughout the disk and is zero near zero.
  Hence D is identically zero.

  At z0:=1/B, one has 0<z0<1,
    B-alpha=(beta-alpha)tau,
    w(z0)=rho,
    w'(z0)=-(beta-alpha)/(1-alpha*z0)^2 !=0.

  Let m>=1 be the multiplicity of rho as a zero of F.
  F(w(z)) has order m at z0. The term
    (1-alpha*z)A(z)F(w(z))
  has order at least m, whereas F'(w(z)) has order m-1.
  This contradicts D=0. Therefore no such rho exists.
  QED.

THEOREM 2.4 -- ORIGINAL H_XY EXIT                                   !
  forall X,Y in N, H^xi_(X,Y) => RH.

PROOF
  Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3 give Z(F) subset (-infinity,-1].
  Since kappa>0, Z(Theta_xi) subset (-infinity,-1/kappa].
  Apply the fixed actual square-descent calibration.
  QED.

THEOREM 3.1 -- CANONICAL PROBABILITY MEASURE                         !
  RH => exists a Borel probability measure mu on I,
    forall n in N, u_n=integral_I t^n dmu(t).

PROOF
  Assume hRH:RH temporarily.
  The zeros of Theta_xi are -b_j, with b_j>0 and multiplicities m_j>=1.
  Genus-zero Hadamard factorization applies because Theta_xi is entire
  of order <1 and Theta_xi(0)!=0:
    Theta_xi(z)/Theta_xi(0)=product_j(1+z/b_j)^(m_j),
    sum_j m_j/b_j<infinity.

  Logarithmic differentiation near zero gives
    kappa=sum_j m_j/b_j.
  Put a_j:=kappa*b_j. Then
    F(z)=product_j(1+z/a_j)^(m_j),
    sum_j m_j/a_j=1.
  Each summand is nonnegative, so m_j/a_j<=1 and
    a_j>=m_j>=1.

  The empty-zero-set case would give F=1 by Hadamard factorization,
  contradicting F'(0)=1. A finite nonempty or countable divisor is
  sufficient; no zero-infinitude theorem is used.

  Define
    mu:=sum_j (m_j/a_j) delta_(1/a_j).
  Its atoms lie in I, all weights are nonnegative, and its mass is 1.

  For |z|<=r<1,
    |m_j/(z+a_j)|<=(m_j/a_j)/(1-r).
  Thus the logarithmic-derivative series converges normally near zero:
    g(z)=sum_j m_j/(z+a_j)
        =integral_I (1+z*t)^(-1) dmu(t).
  Expanding the geometric series uniformly for |z|<=r<1 gives
    g(z)=sum_(n>=0)(-z)^n integral_I t^n dmu(t).
  Equality with the exact actual Taylor coefficients proves the moments.
  Discharge hRH.
  QED.

COROLLARY 3.2 -- RETURN TO EVERY ORIGINAL BUDGET                     !
  RH => forall X,Y in N, H^xi_(X,Y).

PROOF
  Under the probability representation,
    w_(d,j)=integral_I B_(d,j)(t) dmu(t)>=0.
  Consequently V_d=sum_j w_(d,j)=1 and C_d=0 for every d.
  Since Y>=0, C_d<=Y V_d holds for every d, hence for d>=X.
  Discharge RH.
  QED.

THEOREM 5.1 -- BANACH EXTENSION GIVES HOLOMORPHIC EXTENSION           !
  BanachExt_xi => HolExt_xi.

PROOF
  Assume L in C(I,R)^* with exact matching on every polynomial.
  Define the complex-linear extension
    L_C(f):=L(Re f)+i L(Im f), f in C(I,C).
  It is bounded: |L_C(f)|<=2||L|| ||f||_infinity.

  For z in Omega, let k_z(t):=(1+z*t)^(-1).
  Fix z0 in Omega and put delta:=min_(t in I)|1+z0*t|>0.
  For |h|<delta,
    k_(z0+h)(t)
      =sum_(n>=0)(-h)^n t^n/(1+z0*t)^(n+1).
  The n-th term is bounded in C(I,C) norm by
    |h|^n delta^(-n-1).
  Thus z -> k_z is locally a convergent C(I,C)-valued power series.

  Define G(z):=L_C(k_z). Bounded linearity makes G holomorphic.
  For |z|<1,
    k_z=sum_(n>=0)(-z)^n t^n
  converges in the supremum norm, hence
    G(z)=sum_(n>=0)(-z)^n L_C(t^n)
        =sum_(n>=0)(-z)^n u_n.
  This equals the original germ g on a neighborhood of zero.
  Therefore HolExt_xi. No positivity hypothesis on L was used.
  QED.

ALTERNATIVE HVR CHECK
  Put e_j:=sign(w_(d,j)), p_d:=sum_j e_j B_(d,j).
  On I, ||p_d||_infinity<=1 and ell(p_d)=V_d.
  Therefore V_d=L(p_d)<=||L|| for every d.
  Bounded variation is subexponential; Lemma 2.3 then yields RH.

6. HILBERT REALIZATION: REVERSE DIRECTION
======================================================================

THEOREM 6.1 -- HILBERT REALIZATION GIVES HOLOMORPHIC EXTENSION        !
  HilbertRep_xi => HolExt_xi.

PROOF
  Assume (H,T,v) as in Definition 1.3.
  Bounded selfadjoint spectral theory gives
    spectrum(T) subset [0,1], ||T||<=1.

  For z in Omega, 0 is outside the spectrum of I_H+z*T:
    1+z*t!=0 for every t in [0,1].
  Thus R_T(z):=(I_H+z*T)^(-1) exists as a bounded operator.

  Fix z0 in Omega, R0:=R_T(z0).
  The factorization
    I_H+(z0+h)T=(I_H+z0*T)(I_H+h*R0*T)
  and the Neumann series, for ||h*R0*T||<1, give
    R_T(z0+h)=sum_(n>=0)(-h)^n(R0*T)^n R0.
  Hence R_T is operator-norm holomorphic throughout Omega.

  Define G(z):=<v,R_T(z)v>.
  This scalar function is holomorphic on Omega.
  For |z|<1, the operator Neumann series gives
    G(z)=sum_(n>=0)(-z)^n <v,T^n v>
        =sum_(n>=0)(-z)^n u_n.
  Therefore G=g near zero, proving HolExt_xi.
  QED.

7. HOLOMORPHIC EXTENSION: REVERSE DIRECTION

THEOREM 7.1 -- HOLOMORPHIC EXTENSION FORCES RH                       !
  HolExt_xi => RH.

PROOF
  Assume G in O(Omega) with G=g near zero.
  By Theorem 1.6, F'=GF throughout connected Omega.

  Suppose F(z0)=0 for z0 in Omega.
  F is nonzero as an entire function because F(0)=1.
  Let m>=1 be the finite multiplicity of z0.
  Then F' has order m-1 at z0, whereas GF has order at least m.
  Contradiction. Thus F has no zero in Omega.

  Therefore Z(F) subset (-infinity,-1].
  Positive scaling gives Z(Theta_xi) subset (-infinity,-1/kappa].
  The actual square-descent calibration yields RH.
  Discharge HolExt_xi.
  QED.

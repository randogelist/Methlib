BEGINNING ENDPOINT

PARAMETERS.
  n>=1 is an integer.
  A,B are specified real n x n matrices with nonnegative entries.
  PSD is not needed for the basic combinatorial theorem.
  Additional PSD or HVR hypotheses are introduced explicitly below.

DEFINITIONS.
  S_n is the permutation group of {1,...,n}.
  For sigma,tau in S_n, set
    a_sigma := product_i a_(i,sigma(i)),
    b_tau   := product_i b_(i,tau(i)).
  P := per(A) := sum_sigma a_sigma.
  Q := per(B) := sum_tau b_tau.
  (A o B)_ij := a_ij b_ij.
  Delta(A,B) := P Q - per(A o B).

ROOT THEOREM.
  (C1) Delta(A,B)>=0.
  (C2) If P,Q>0, the normalized permutation distributions satisfy
         per(A o B)/(P Q) = <pi_A,pi_B>,
       with exact gap and equality formulas in Sections 3-5.
  (C3) Delta(A,B)=0 iff
         P=0, or Q=0, or A and B have the same unique permutation with
         positive permutation product.
  (C4) If A,B are additionally symmetric PSD, then Delta(A,B)=0 iff
         some diagonal entry of A is zero,
         or some diagonal entry of B is zero,
         or both A and B are diagonal.
  (C5) The positive-operator moment/resolvent Gram matrices in Sections
       7-9 satisfy the hypotheses above. Their stated strictness,
       degeneracy, and diagonal-unitary extensions follow exactly.

FULL NUMBERED DERIVATION

1. EXACT PERMUTATION RESIDUAL.

  The definition of the Hadamard product gives
    per(A o B) = sum_sigma a_sigma b_sigma.
  Expanding P Q gives
    P Q = sum_(sigma,tau) a_sigma b_tau.
  Subtracting the diagonal part yields the identity
    Delta(A,B) = sum_(sigma!=tau) a_sigma b_tau.                (1)

  Identity (1) is algebraic and holds for arbitrary complex entries.
  Under the stated entrywise nonnegative hypotheses, each summand is
  a nonnegative real number. Therefore C1 follows.

2. ZERO-PERMANENT BRANCHES.

  Since all a_sigma>=0, P=0 iff every a_sigma=0.
  In that case per(A o B)=0 and Delta=0 for every nonnegative B.
  The same conclusion holds when Q=0.

  Normalized distributions are defined only on the remaining branch
  P>0 and Q>0. No division by zero is used in any equality statement.

3. NORMALIZED PERMUTATION OVERLAP.

  Assume P,Q>0 and define
    pi_A(sigma):=a_sigma/P,
    pi_B(sigma):=b_sigma/Q.
  Then
    pi_A(sigma),pi_B(sigma)>=0,
    sum_sigma pi_A(sigma)=sum_sigma pi_B(sigma)=1.

  Therefore
    theta(A,B):=per(A o B)/(P Q)
      = sum_sigma pi_A(sigma)pi_B(sigma)
      = <pi_A,pi_B>,
    Delta(A,B)/(P Q)=1-<pi_A,pi_B>.

  If sigma and tau are independently sampled from these two finite
  distributions, theta is exactly the probability sigma=tau. This is
  an interpretation of the finite sum, with no stochastic premise in
  the algebraic theorem.

  The following bounds are immediate finite-sum consequences:
    0<=theta<=min{max_sigma pi_A(sigma), max_sigma pi_B(sigma)}<=1.
  For example,
    sum_sigma pi_A(sigma)pi_B(sigma)
      <= (max_sigma pi_A(sigma)) sum_sigma pi_B(sigma).
  Interchanging A,B gives the other bound.

  Hence, if either distribution has maximum at most 1-epsilon with
  epsilon>0, then
    Delta(A,B)>=epsilon P Q>0.

4. EXACT NONNEGATIVE DECOMPOSITION OF THE NORMALIZED GAP.

  Regard pi_A,pi_B as real Euclidean vectors indexed by S_n. Expansion
  of their squared distance gives
    1-<pi_A,pi_B>
      = (1/2)||pi_A-pi_B||_2^2
        +(1/2)(1-||pi_A||_2^2)
        +(1/2)(1-||pi_B||_2^2).                              (2)

  For any probability vector pi on a finite set,
    1-||pi||_2^2 = 2 sum_(sigma<tau) pi(sigma)pi(tau)>=0,
  where any fixed ordering of S_n may be used.
  Thus every term on the right of (2) is nonnegative. This proves again
  that theta<=1, now separating distribution mismatch from nontrivial
  support within each distribution.

5. COMPLETE EQUALITY CLASSIFICATION FOR NONNEGATIVE MATRICES.

  Continue to assume P,Q>0. Equality Delta=0 is equivalent to theta=1.
  Equality in (2) implies
    pi_A=pi_B,
    ||pi_A||_2^2=||pi_B||_2^2=1.
  For a nonnegative probability vector, squared norm one implies
    sum_(sigma<tau) pi(sigma)pi(tau)=0.
  Consequently at most one coordinate is positive; its value must be 1.
  Thus
    Delta=0 <=> there exists sigma_0 with
      pi_A=pi_B=delta_(sigma_0).

  In matrix terms, each matrix has exactly one permutation with positive
  permutation product, and the two permutations are the same.
  Conversely, this condition gives theta=1 directly.

  Combining with Section 2 proves C3.
  This condition is not a claim that arbitrary nonnegative matrices must
  be permutation-diagonal: entries outside their unique positive
  matching can remain positive without creating another matching.
  For example, [1 1; 0 1] has only the identity positive permutation
  product, although its upper-right entry is positive.

  Also, on the branch P,Q>0,
    theta=0 <=> supports(pi_A) and supports(pi_B) are disjoint,
  because every summand pi_A(sigma)pi_B(sigma) is nonnegative.

6. SHARPENED EQUALITY CLASSIFICATION FOR NONNEGATIVE PSD MATRICES.

  Suppose now A=A^T>=_PSD0 and B=B^T>=_PSD0, still with nonnegative entries.

  Every PSD matrix is a Gram matrix, so
    |a_ij|^2<=a_ii a_jj.
  Thus a_ii=0 forces the entire i-th row and column to vanish, and hence
  per(A)=0. Conversely, if every a_ii>0, then the identity-permutation
  product is positive:
    a_id=product_i a_ii>0,
  so P>0. Therefore
    P=0 <=> some a_ii=0,
  and likewise for B.

  Assume all diagonals of A,B are positive. Their identity-permutation
  products are positive, so a unique positive permutation, if present,
  must be the identity.

  If a_ij>0 for some i!=j, symmetry and positivity of the remaining
  diagonals give the positive transposition product
    a_((ij))=a_ij^2 product_(k!=i,j) a_kk>0.
  This is a second positive permutation product. Hence the identity is
  the unique positive permutation iff all off-diagonal entries vanish.
  The same argument holds for B.

  Section 5 now proves C4. Equivalently, for nonzero Gram vectors,
  equality holds iff each of the two Gram families is pairwise orthogonal.
  For n=1 both matrices are diagonal, so equality always holds, as required.

7. POSITIVE-FUNCTION HVR GRAM CALIBRATION.

  Let K be a positive selfadjoint operator on a complex Hilbert space H,
  possibly unbounded, and v in H. Let f_1,...,f_n be bounded, real,
  nonnegative Borel functions on [0,infinity). Define
    u_i:=f_i(K)v,
    A_ij:=<u_i,u_j>.
  Let mu_v be the finite positive spectral measure of K at v. Then
    A_ij=integral f_i(lambda)f_j(lambda) dmu_v(lambda)>=0.
  The integrals are real. Also, for every complex coefficient vector c,
    sum_(i,j) conjugate(c_i) A_ij c_j
      = ||sum_j c_j u_j||^2>=0.
  Thus A is a real symmetric PSD matrix with nonnegative entries.

  An independently chosen positive operator, vector, and nonnegative
  functions define B with the same properties. Sections 1-6 therefore
  apply to these actual HVR Gram matrices without any additional
  positivity conjecture.

8. MOMENT AND RESOLVENT SPECIALIZATIONS.

  For a>0,
    T_a:=K(I+aK)^(-1)
  is the bounded nonnegative spectral function lambda/(1+a lambda).
  For i,j=0,...,n-1, the moment Gram matrix is
    A_(i,j)=<T_a^i v,T_a^j v>
            =<v,T_a^(i+j)v>.
  Every entry is nonnegative by the spectral integral. This is a Hankel
  matrix of the actual moments of the specified positive operator.

  For a_1,...,a_n>0, the resolvent Gram matrix is
    A_ij=<T_(a_i)v,T_(a_j)v>
         =integral lambda^2/[(1+a_i lambda)(1+a_j lambda)] dmu_v(lambda).

  Put F(a):=<v,T_a v>. For a!=b, the scalar resolvent identity gives
    [F(a)-F(b)]/(b-a)
      =integral lambda^2/[(1+a lambda)(1+b lambda)] dmu_v(lambda)
      =<T_a v,T_b v>.
  At a=b the continuous diagonal value is
    -F'(a)=||T_a v||^2.
  Differentiation is justified locally by the bound
    lambda^2/(1+a lambda)^2<=1/a^2
  and finiteness of mu_v; on a compact positive-a neighborhood the same
  finite bound dominates all difference quotients.

  Hence the divided-difference kernel is both PSD and entrywise
  nonnegative, and is calibrated to the specified operator and vector.

9. DEGENERACY AND STRICTNESS IN THE HVR SUBCLASS.

  For the general positive-function construction of Section 7,
    A_ii=||u_i||^2.
  Therefore per(A)=0 iff at least one u_i=0. Otherwise per(A)>0.
  By Section 6, equality between two such nondegenerate Gram families
  holds iff both families are pairwise orthogonal.

  For the resolvent family T_(a_i)v, the measure condition
    mu_v((0,infinity))=0
  makes every T_(a_i)v zero. If instead mu_v((0,infinity))>0, then every
  integrand in the resolvent Gram formula is strictly positive on that
  set, so every A_ij is strictly positive.

  If n>=2 and every entry of A is strictly positive, then all n!
  permutation products are positive. Its probability vector has at
  least two positive entries, and max pi_A<1. Thus, for every nonnegative
  B with per(B)>0,
    per(A o B)<per(A)per(B).

  In particular, two nondegenerate common-vector resolvent Gram matrices
  satisfy strict Chollet inequality for n>=2. The zero-vector branch and
  n=1 branch retain equality as already classified.

10. DIAGONAL-UNITARY EXTENSION OF THE PROVED SUBCLASS.

  Let A_0,B_0 be matrices from the proved nonnegative subclass, and let
    D=diag(d_1,...,d_n), E=diag(e_1,...,e_n),
    |d_i|=|e_i|=1.
  Define A=D^*A_0D and B=E^*B_0E.
  In each permutation product the phase factor is
    product_i conjugate(d_i)d_(sigma(i))=1.
  Consequently every permutation product, and therefore every permanent,
  agrees with the corresponding quantity for A_0. The same holds for B.
  Furthermore
    A o B=(DE)^*(A_0 o B_0)(DE).
  Thus
    Delta(A,B)=Delta(A_0,B_0),
  and all proved inequalities and equality statements transfer exactly.
  This is a specified complex subclass, not all complex PSD pairs.

11. GENERAL GRAM/TENSOR IDENTITY AND ITS LIMIT.

  For arbitrary complex Gram vectors u_1,...,u_n, put
    x=u_1 tensor ... tensor u_n,
    Sym=(1/n!) sum_(sigma in S_n) U_sigma,
  where U_sigma permutes the tensor factors. The group average Sym is
  selfadjoint and idempotent, hence an orthogonal projection. Thus
    ||Sym x||^2=<x,Sym x>
      =(1/n!) sum_sigma product_i <u_i,u_(sigma(i))>
      =per(Gram(u_1,...,u_n))/n!.

  For A=Gram(u_i) and B=Gram(w_i),
    A o B=Gram(u_i tensor w_i).
  Therefore the general conjectural inequality is exactly the comparison
    n! ||Sym tensor_i(u_i tensor w_i)||^2
      <= (n!)^2 ||Sym tensor_i u_i||^2 ||Sym tensor_i w_i||^2.
  The nonnegativity of these separate squared norms does not prove this
  comparison. Sections 1-10 prove it only under their specified subclass
  premises.

12. EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO DROPPING THE SIGN PREMISE.

  Let J_3 be the 3 x 3 all-ones matrix and set
    A=(3I_3-J_3)/2.
  Its eigenvalues are 0,3/2,3/2, so A is PSD. Its diagonal entries are 1
  and its off-diagonal entries are -1/2. The 3-cycle product is
    a_((123))=-1/8<0.
  Thus PSD alone does not imply the nonnegative permutation-product
  premise used in (1).

  For explicit calibration,
    per(A)=3/2,
    per(A o A)=39/32,
    per(A)^2-per(A o A)=33/32>0.
  This example witnesses failure of the subclass premise, not failure
  of Chollet's inequality.

13. FIXED-INPUT EXACT ARITHMETIC.

  For rational nonnegative A,B, the permutation products, permanents,
  Delta, and the normalized distributions on the branch P,Q>0 are
  rational and computable by finite sums and products. Equality can be
  checked exactly by the classifications above.

  For finite rational symmetric PSD K, rational v, and positive rational
  a_i, the matrices T_(a_i)=K(I+a_iK)^(-1) and their Gram entries are
  rational. The inverse exists by positivity. Hence these calibrated
  HVR instances admit exact rational checking.

  No rationality is asserted for arbitrary infinite-dimensional spectral
  data or arbitrary real parameters. Their subclass theorem is analytic
  via the specified positive spectral measure, not an unprovided finite
  rational certificate.

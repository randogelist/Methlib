1. DEFINITIONS AND EXACT ENDPOINTS
---------------------------------

Throughout, n is an integer with n>=1. Every matrix is finite of order n.
The unnormalized permanent is

  per(A) = sum_{sigma in S_n} product_{i=1}^n A[i,sigma(i)].

Write A o B for entrywise multiplication and bar(A) for entrywise complex
conjugation. The pair gap and self gap are

  Delta(A,B) = per(A) per(B) - per(A o B),
  delta(C)   = per(C)^2 - per(C o bar(C)).

For Hermitian matrices these quantities are real. A correlation matrix is
a Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix with every diagonal entry equal
to one. For any Hermitian unit-diagonal matrix C, set

  r(C)  = max_{i<j} |C[i,j]|, with r(C)=0 if n=1,
  E(C)  = sum_{i<j} |C[i,j]|^2,
  E4(C) = sum_{i<j} |C[i,j]|^4.

For n>=2 put

  d_n = 4 n^2 - 9 n + 6,
  g_n(E) = (4/n - 1/d_n) E + 4 E^2/n^2.

THEOREM R1: UNIFORM SMALL-CORRELATION CERTIFICATE.

  Let n>=2 and let C be Hermitian with C[i,i]=1 and
  r(C)<=1/[2(n-1)]. Then

    C >=_PSD (1/2) I,
    delta(C) >= g_n(E(C)) >= 0.

  The coefficient 4/n-1/d_n is strictly positive. Consequently

    delta(C)=0 iff C=I.

  No phase or sign restriction is imposed on the off-diagonal entries.

THEOREM R3: ONE-SIDED QUANTITATIVE CHOLLET.

  Let C,D be correlation matrices. Assume that C is entrywise nonnegative,
  or can be made entrywise nonnegative by diagonal-unitary conjugation.
  Set

    P=per(C), Q=per(D), rho=max_{i<j}|D[i,j]|^2,

  with rho=0 for n=1. Then

    Delta(C,D) >= P+Q-2 >= E(C)+rho >=0.

  The original pair inequality is an equality, Delta(C,D)=0, iff C=D=I.

  For unnormalized PSD A,B with positive diagonal, apply this result after
  diagonal normalization. If either matrix has a zero diagonal entry,
  the pair gap is zero. Thus, within the one-sided phase-adjustable class,
  equality is equivalent to a zero diagonal entry in either matrix or to
  both matrices being diagonal.

THEOREM R4: PARAMETER-EXPLICIT RESOLVENT GRAM MARGIN.

  Let mu be a finite positive Borel measure on [0,infinity), with
  mu((0,infinity))>0. Let a_1,...,a_n>0 and set

    f_i(lambda)=lambda/(1+a_i lambda),
    A[i,j]=integral f_i f_j dmu.

  Define C[i,j]=A[i,j]/sqrt(A[i,i] A[j,j]) and

    H(a)[i,j]=2 sqrt(a_i a_j)/(a_i+a_j),
    L(a)=per(H(a)).

  Then C is an entrywise positive correlation matrix,

    C[i,j]>=H(a)[i,j],
    per(C)>=L(a)>=1+sum_{i<j} 4 a_i a_j/(a_i+a_j)^2.

  For two such independently constructed Gram matrices A,B, with
  parameter lists a,b and positive permanent,

    per(A o B)/(per(A)per(B)) <= 1/max(L(a),L(b)).

  Further dimension/parameter-spread bounds are derived in Section 7.

THEOREM R6: NONNEGATIVE REAL PARTS OF CYCLE PRODUCTS.

  Let C be a correlation matrix. Assume that every simple support cycle
  gamma of length at least three satisfies

    Re(product_{(i,j) along gamma} C[i,j])>=0.

  Let M be the sum over all matchings of the support graph, including the
  empty matching, with edge weight |C[i,j]|^2. Then

    delta(C)>=2(M-1)>=2E(C)>=0.

  The self gap is zero iff C=I. The coefficient 2 in front of E(C) is
  optimal uniformly over this class. The cycle premise permits nonzero
  complex phases; it is weaker than requiring positive real cycle products.

  Any two matrices satisfying R6 at the same order satisfy the pair
  inequality by the proved Cauchy-Schwarz reduction in Section 2.5.

2. PSD FOUNDATIONS, NORMALIZATION, AND SELF-REDUCTION
---------------------------------------------------

2.1 PSD entries and zero diagonal.

Every finite Hermitian PSD matrix is a Gram matrix:

  C[i,j]=<v_i,v_j>.

Cauchy-Schwarz gives |C[i,j]|^2<=C[i,i]C[j,j]. Thus a zero diagonal
entry forces the corresponding row and column to be zero. Every permanent
monomial then vanishes. If A or B has a zero diagonal entry, both its
permanent and per(A o B) vanish, so Delta(A,B)=0.

2.2 Permanent positivity and a two-vector lower bound.

The following finite polynomial argument proves all permanent positivity
facts used below, including the diagonal-product inequality.

For finitely many commuting variables z_1,...,z_m, give monomials the
inner product

  <z^alpha,z^beta> = 0 if alpha!=beta,
  <z^alpha,z^alpha> = alpha! = product_j alpha_j!.

The inner product is conjugate linear in the first argument. For a vector
v in C^m, define multiplication by

  L_v(z)=sum_j v_j z_j

and the differential operator

  D_v=sum_j conjugate(v_j) partial/partial z_j.

The monomial definition gives L_v^*=D_v. The product rule gives

  D_v L_v - L_v D_v = ||v||^2 I.

Consequently, for every polynomial p,

  ||L_v p||^2
    = <p,D_v L_v p>
    = ||v||^2 ||p||^2 + <p,L_v D_v p>
    = ||v||^2 ||p||^2 + ||D_v p||^2.                 (2.1)

Expansion of the product polynomial gives

  || product_i L_{v_i} ||^2 = per(Gram(v_1,...,v_n)). (2.2)

For completeness, a term of the product expansion assigns a coordinate
to each vector. Two assignments contribute to its squared norm precisely
when each coordinate is used equally often. If the common multiplicities
are alpha, there are alpha! permutations pairing the assignments. Summing
over such pairings is exactly the permanent expansion. This proves (2.2).

Repeated use of (2.1), starting from the constant polynomial 1, proves

  per(Gram(v_i)) >= product_i ||v_i||^2 >=0.         (2.3)

For unit Gram vectors, multiplication by each remaining L_v cannot
decrease squared norm. Starting with any two indices i!=j yields

  ||L_{v_i}L_{v_j}||^2 = 1+|<v_i,v_j>|^2.

Therefore every correlation matrix C satisfies

  per(C)>=1+max_{i<j}|C[i,j]|^2>=1.                (2.4)

For n=1, per(C)=1. Formula (2.4) also shows that per(C)=1 iff C=I:
per(C)=1 forces every off-diagonal modulus to be zero, and the converse
follows from the permanent of I.

2.3 Reality and Schur products.

For a Hermitian C, conjugation of a permanent monomial corresponds to
inverting its permutation. Therefore per(C) is real. If A=Gram(v_i) and
B=Gram(w_i), then A o B=Gram(v_i tensor w_i), so A o B is PSD and its
permanent is nonnegative by (2.2). Entrywise conjugation preserves PSD,
as witnessed by conjugating the Gram vectors.

2.4 Diagonal normalization and phase invariance.

Suppose A has positive diagonal and define d_A=product_i A[i,i]. Set

  C[i,j]=A[i,j]/sqrt(A[i,i]A[j,j]).

Each permanent monomial acquires the same factor d_A. Thus

  per(A)=d_A per(C).

If B similarly normalizes to D, then

  per(A o B)=d_A d_B per(C o D),
  Delta(A,B)=d_A d_B Delta(C,D).                    (2.5)

For a diagonal unitary U=diag(u_i), every monomial of U^* A U contains
the phase product

  product_i conjugate(u_i) u_{sigma(i)}=1.

Hence each permutation monomial, each permanent, the self gap, and the
pair gap are invariant under independent diagonal-unitary conjugations
of their matrix arguments. For the pair gap this also follows directly
from the monomial products in per(A o B).

2.5 Self-conjugate reduction.

For arbitrary complex matrices A,B, put

  a_sigma=product_i A[i,sigma(i)],
  b_sigma=product_i B[i,sigma(i)].

Finite Cauchy-Schwarz implies

  |per(A o B)|^2
    <= (sum_sigma |a_sigma|^2)(sum_sigma |b_sigma|^2)
    = per(A o bar(A)) per(B o bar(B)).              (2.6)

If A,B are PSD and their two self gaps are nonnegative, then (2.6) and
permanent nonnegativity give

  per(A o B)<=per(A)per(B).

Conversely, the universal PSD pair inequality implies the self inequality
by taking B=bar(A); per(bar(A))=per(A). This proves the equivalence between the universal pair and self forms.
The reduction is recorded in [S1] and is fully derived here.

In particular, a violating PSD pair requires a self-violating matrix
among its two factors. A family of individually self-certified matrices
can be paired arbitrarily, even if the two certificates have different
forms. If per(A),per(B)>0 and delta(A)>=g_A>=0, delta(B)>=g_B>=0, then

  per(A o B)/(per(A)per(B))
    <= sqrt((1-g_A/per(A)^2)(1-g_B/per(B)^2)).       (2.7)

3. PHASE ADJUSTMENT AND PERMUTATION POSITIVITY
---------------------------------------------

3.1 Phase-balanced support criterion.

Let A be Hermitian with strictly positive diagonal. The following are
equivalent:

  (i) Every permutation monomial of A is a nonnegative real number.
  (ii) Every simple cycle of nonzero off-diagonal entries has a positive
       real product around that cycle.
  (iii) There is a diagonal unitary U such that U^* A U is entrywise
        nonnegative.

Proof of (i)=>(ii): choose the cycle as one permutation cycle and fix all
remaining vertices. The remaining diagonal factors are strictly positive.
The cycle entries are nonzero, so its product must be strictly positive.

Proof of (ii)=>(iii): choose a spanning tree in each connected component
of the support graph. Choose a unit phase at the root. Along each tree
edge, choose the next vertex phase to make the transformed edge positive.
For every non-tree edge, its fundamental cycle has phase one. The already
positive tree edges then force the transformed non-tree edge to be
positive as well. Hermitian symmetry gives the reverse edge. The diagonal
is unchanged and positive; zero edges remain zero.

Proof of (iii)=>(i): diagonal-unitary phases cancel in every permutation
monomial, and all monomials of U^* A U are nonnegative.

Thus abstract permutation positivity is an exact recognition criterion;
for positive-diagonal Hermitian matrices it is equivalent to phase
adjustment to the original nonnegative class. A zero PSD diagonal belongs
to the already settled zero-row branch of Section 2.

4. FINITE CYCLE ESTIMATES USED IN THE PROOF OF R1
-------------------------------------------

4.1 Counting coefficients.

Let D_k be the number of derangements of k labelled objects:

  D_0=1, D_1=0,
  D_k=(k-1)(D_{k-1}+D_{k-2}) for k>=2.

Let J_k be the number of fixed-point-free involutions:

  J_k=0 for odd k,
  J_k=k!/[2^(k/2)(k/2)!] for even k.

Set L_k=D_k-J_k. Then L_k counts derangements containing a cycle of
length at least three. For n>=2 define the nonnegative polynomials

  H_n(x)=sum_{k=3}^n
          [2 binom(n-2,k-2) L_k/(k(k-1))] x^(k-2),

  K_n(x)=sum_{k=3}^n
          [2 binom(n-2,k-2) D_k/(k(k-1))] x^(k-2).

An empty sum, including n=2, equals zero.

4.2 Product bound.

If k>=2 and 0<=x_1,...,x_k<=r, then

  product_j x_j <= r^(k-2) (sum_j x_j^2)/k.         (4.1)

Indeed, for every distinct pair a,b,

  product_j x_j <= r^(k-2) x_a x_b
                 <= r^(k-2)(x_a^2+x_b^2)/2.

Average over the binom(k,2) pairs. Each square occurs k-1 times, giving
(4.1). The case r=0 follows directly; when k=2 use the displayed
two-factor inequality without any ambiguous zero-power convention.

4.3 Non-involution contribution.

Let C be Hermitian with unit diagonal. A permutation consisting only of
fixed points and transpositions has a nonnegative monomial, since a
transposition (i j) contributes |C[i,j]|^2. Denote the sum over all such
permutations by M. Identity and single-transposition terms give

  M>=1+E(C).                                       (4.2)

Let T be the sum of absolute monomials over all remaining permutations.
Hermitian reality gives

  per(C)>=M-T>=1+E(C)-T.                           (4.3)

There are binom(n,k)L_k remaining permutations moving exactly k indices.
Their class is invariant under relabelling. Each has k directed moved
edges. Hence a specified directed edge (i,j), i!=j, appears in exactly

  binom(n,k)L_k k/[n(n-1)]
    =binom(n-2,k-2)L_k/(k-1)

members of this class. Apply (4.1) to each monomial and sum. Since
sum_{i!=j}|C[i,j]|^2=2E(C), the k-moved contribution is bounded by

  E(C) [2 binom(n-2,k-2)L_k/(k(k-1))] r(C)^(k-2).

Summing gives T<=E(C)H_n(r(C)). Equation (4.3) now proves

  per(C)>=1+[1-H_n(r(C))]E(C)=ell.                 (4.4)

4.4 Squared-modulus permanent.

The matrix C o bar(C) has unit diagonal and nonnegative entries
|C[i,j]|^2. Its identity contribution is 1 and its two-moved contribution
is E4(C). For each k>=3 include all derangements, not only non-involutions.
Apply the same counting argument and (4.1) with entry bound r(C)^2 and
edge-square sum 2E4(C). Therefore

  per(C o bar(C))
    <=1+[1+K_n(r(C)^2)]E4(C)=U.                   (4.5)

Equations (4.4) and (4.5) are universal estimates under the stated
Hermitian unit-diagonal hypotheses. Section 5 supplies the explicit
radius bound that makes their resulting gap positive.

5. UNIFORM CORRELATION REGION: COMPLETE PROOF OF R1
--------------------------------------------------

5.1 A derangement bound.

For k>=2,

  D_k<=k!/2.                                      (5.1)

The bases D_2=1 and D_3=2 satisfy (5.1). For k>=4 the recurrence and
induction give

  D_k<=(k-1)[(k-1)!+(k-2)!]/2=k!/2.

Since L_k<=D_k, every coefficient in H_n and K_n is at most

  binom(n-2,k-2)(k-2)!
    =(n-2)(n-3)...(n-k+1)
    <=(n-2)^(k-2).

Thus, whenever x>=0 and (n-2)x<1,

  H_n(x)<= (n-2)x/[1-(n-2)x],
  K_n(x)<= (n-2)x/[1-(n-2)x].                     (5.2)

This includes n=2, where both sides vanish. It follows by bounding the
finite sum by the convergent geometric series over positive powers.

5.2 Apply the stated radius.

Suppose r<=1/[2(n-1)]. Then

  t=(n-2)r <= (n-2)/[2(n-1)]<1,
  H_n(r)<=t/(1-t)<=(n-2)/n.

Equation (4.4) gives

  per(C)>=1+2E(C)/n.                              (5.3)

Also E4(C)<=r^2 E(C). With u=(n-2)r^2<1, (4.5) gives

  per(C o bar(C))
    <=1+E4(C)/(1-u)
    <=1+[r^2/(1-(n-2)r^2)]E(C)
    <=1+E(C)/[4(n-1)^2-(n-2)]
    =1+E(C)/d_n.                                 (5.4)

The last comparison follows because x/(1-(n-2)x) is increasing for
0<=x<1/(n-2), with the n=2 case read directly.

Square (5.3), whose right side is positive, and subtract (5.4):

  delta(C)
    >=(4/n-1/d_n)E(C)+4E(C)^2/n^2
    =g_n(E(C)).                                  (5.5)

For n>=2,

  d_n=4(n-2)^2+7(n-2)+4>0,
  4d_n-n=(n-2)(16n-5)+14>0.

Hence 4/n-1/d_n>0. Equation (5.5) is strictly positive whenever E(C)>0.
Because C has unit diagonal, E(C)=0 iff C=I, and delta(I)=0.

5.3 PSD verification.

For every vector z,

  z^* C z
    >=sum_i |z_i|^2-2 sum_{i<j}|C[i,j]||z_i||z_j|
    >=sum_i [1-sum_{j!=i}|C[i,j]|]|z_i|^2
    >=(1/2)sum_i |z_i|^2.

The middle step uses 2ab<=a^2+b^2. Thus C>_PSD0, proving every assertion
of R1 without an additional PSD certificate.

5.4 Consequences for pairs and original scaling.

Any two matrices satisfying R1 at the same order satisfy the pair
inequality by (2.6), including arbitrary and independently chosen phases.
If either matrix differs from I, the corresponding self gap is positive,
and Cauchy-Schwarz then makes the pair inequality strict.

For positive-diagonal PSD A, the condition is equivalently

  |A[i,j]|/sqrt(A[i,i]A[j,j]) <=1/[2(n-1)]
  for every i!=j.

The self gap is scaled by d_A^2, so (5.5) becomes

  delta(A)>=d_A^2 g_n(E(C)).

If an original pair has a zero diagonal entry, use Section 2.1 instead
of dividing by the diagonal. Order n=1 always gives equality.

6. ONE-SIDED QUANTITATIVE THEOREM: COMPLETE PROOF OF R3
-----------------------------------------------------

Assume first that C is entrywise nonnegative. Set

  a_sigma=product_i C[i,sigma(i)]>=0,
  b_sigma=product_i D[i,sigma(i)].

Every diagonal is one, so a_id=b_id=1. For n>=2, 0<=rho<=1 by the PSD
entry bound. Every nonidentity permutation moves at least two indices.
All moved entries of D have modulus at most sqrt(rho), and all remaining
entries have modulus at most one. Hence

  |b_sigma|<=rho for sigma!=id.                   (6.1)

Taking real parts and using (6.1),

  per(C o D)<=1+rho sum_{sigma!=id}a_sigma
             =1+rho(P-1).                         (6.2)

Section 2.2 gives Q>=1+rho. Therefore

  Delta(C,D)
    >=PQ-1-rho(P-1)
    =(P-1)(Q-rho)+(Q-1)
    >=P+Q-2.                                    (6.3)

Identity and single-transposition monomials of C give P>=1+E(C).
Together with Q>=1+rho, (6.3) yields

  Delta(C,D)>=P+Q-2>=E(C)+rho.                    (6.4)

The right side vanishes only if C=D=I. Conversely, both identity matrices
give equality. The n=1 case is immediate. If C is only phase-adjustable
to a nonnegative matrix, use the invariance in Section 2.4; E(C) is also
unchanged. Diagonal normalization and the zero-row branch prove the
unnormalized equality classification in R3.

An equivalent dimensional form for positive-diagonal A,B is

  Delta(A,B)>=per(A)d_B+per(B)d_A-2d_A d_B.

Known baseline versus present quantitative derivation:

  The simpler one-sided inequality |per(A o B)|<=per(A)d_B for nonnegative
  A and PSD B is recorded by Zhang [S1], citing his 1989 result [S2].
  Its proof is immediate from |b_sigma|<=d_B. The complete quantitative
  derivation used here is (6.1)-(6.4).

7. POSITIVE RESOLVENT GRAM BOUNDS: COMPLETE PROOF OF R4
-----------------------------------------------------

7.1 Well-defined matrices.

For a_i>0, 0<=f_i(lambda)<=1/a_i. Since mu is finite, all integrals in R4
are finite. Each f_i is strictly positive on (0,infinity), which has
positive measure. Thus every A[i,j]>0. The matrix is Gram in L^2(mu), so
it is PSD, and its diagonal is positive. Its normalization C is an
entrywise positive correlation matrix.

7.2 Pairwise parameter estimate.

Fix i,j. Write f=f_i, g=f_j and

  m=min(1,a_j/a_i), M=max(1,a_j/a_i).

For lambda>0,

  m<=f(lambda)/g(lambda)=(1+a_j lambda)/(1+a_i lambda)<=M.

At lambda=0, f=g=0. Therefore (f-mg)(Mg-f)>=0 everywhere. Integrating,

  ||f||^2+mM||g||^2 <=(m+M)<f,g>.

The nonnegative square (||f||-sqrt(mM)||g||)^2 gives

  ||f||^2+mM||g||^2 >=2sqrt(mM)||f||||g||.

Division by the positive norms yields

  C[i,j]>=2sqrt(mM)/(m+M)
          =2sqrt(a_i a_j)/(a_i+a_j)=H(a)[i,j].     (7.1)

All entries are nonnegative, so permanent monotonicity follows directly
by multiplying entrywise bounds in each monomial and summing. Hence

  per(C)>=per(H(a))=L(a).

Keeping identity and transposition terms of H(a) proves the additional
lower bound in R4. For n>=2, this gives L(a)>1.

7.3 Permanent-ratio margin.

For entrywise nonnegative correlation matrices C,D, each permutation
monomial lies in [0,1]. Consequently

  per(C o D)<=min(per(C),per(D)).

Since both permanents are positive,

  per(C o D)/(per(C)per(D)) <=1/max(per(C),per(D)). (7.2)

Apply (7.1) to each family and then (7.2). The ratio is invariant under
positive diagonal normalization, so R4 follows for the original A,B.
Independent diagonal-unitary conjugations preserve the result as well.

7.4 Dimension and parameter-spread bound.

Let q=max_i a_i/min_i a_i>=1 and c=2sqrt(q)/(1+q). For every pair,

  H(a)[i,j]>=c,

because 2sqrt(t)/(1+t) is invariant under t->1/t and decreases for t>=1.
Define

  F_n(c)=sum_{k=0}^n binom(n,k)D_k c^k.

There are binom(n,k)D_k permutations moving k indices. The corresponding
monomial of H(a) is at least c^k, so

  L(a)>=F_n(c)>=1+binom(n,2)c^2.                  (7.3)

For two resolvent families, let c_a,c_b be their spread parameters. Then

  per(A o B)/(per(A)per(B))
    <=1/max(F_n(c_a),F_n(c_b)).                   (7.4)

Example: if n=3 and at least one parameter list has spread q<=4, then
c>=4/5 and

  F_3(4/5)=1+3(4/5)^2+2(4/5)^3=493/125.

Thus the pair ratio is at most 125/493, and the relative gap is at least
368/493. Both matrices here remain within the specified positive
resolvent Gram class. This is not a bound for arbitrary PSD matrices.

7.5 Operator interpretation and scope.

For a positive selfadjoint operator K and vector v, the finite positive
spectral measure mu_v gives

  A[i,j]=<K(I+a_i K)^(-1)v, K(I+a_j K)^(-1)v>
        =integral f_i f_j dmu_v.

The bounded functions f_i avoid unbounded-domain assumptions on v. R4
applies when mu_v((0,infinity))>0. If this measure is zero, every resolvent
vector is zero and the permanent comparison is the zero equality branch.

8. CYCLE-PHASE THEOREM: COMPLETE PROOF OF R6
----------------------------------------------

8.1 A weighted matching inequality.

Let X be real symmetric, with X[i,i]=1 and 0<=X[i,j]<=1. For an undirected
matching m, set w(m)=product_{edges {i,j} in m}X[i,j], with w(empty)=1.
Write M(X)=sum_m w(m). Then

  per(X)<=1+(M(X)-1)^2.                            (8.1)

To prove (8.1), encode each permutation as an ordered pair of matchings,
whose edges are called red and blue. Work on its disjoint cycles.

  A fixed point supplies no edge.

  A transposition supplies its edge in both colors.

  An even cycle of length at least four receives alternating edge colors.
  Choose the coloring whose outgoing edge at its least-labelled vertex
  is red. Thus its two orientations yield its two distinct colorings.

  For an odd cycle of length at least three, remove its lexicographically
  least unordered edge. This choice depends on the undirected cycle, not
  on its orientation. The remaining path has positive even length. Start
  at the endpoint whose original outgoing edge lies in the path, color
  that edge red, and alternate along the path. The final edge is blue.

Each color is a matching. The colored union reconstructs the permutation:
doubled edges recover transpositions; alternating even cycles recover
their orientation from the red edge at their least vertex; even-length
paths recover their orientation from the red endpoint to the blue
endpoint, followed by the missing closing edge. Isolated vertices recover
fixed points. These are distinct component types, so the encoding is
injective.

For transpositions and even cycles the image weight equals the original
permutation weight. An odd cycle loses one factor in [0,1], so its image
weight is at least the original weight. Disjoint components preserve this
comparison on multiplication. The identity maps to (empty,empty), of
weight one. Every nonidentity permutation maps to two nonempty matchings.
Thus its total weight is bounded by

  1+sum_{m1 nonempty}sum_{m2 nonempty}w(m1)w(m2)
    =1+(M(X)-1)^2,

which proves (8.1). Zero edges cause no exception: an original zero weight
is still bounded by its image weight, and the combinatorial encoding is
defined independently of the weights.

8.2 Group opposite cycle orientations.

Let C satisfy R6 and set X[i,j]=|C[i,j]|^2. PSD gives X[i,j]<=1.
The identity and all products of disjoint transpositions contribute
exactly M(X) to per(C). Group each remaining set of monomials by its
underlying undirected disjoint cycles. For every cycle gamma of length
at least three, its two orientations have conjugate products z_gamma
and conjugate(z_gamma). Summing its orientations gives

  z_gamma+conjugate(z_gamma)=2Re(z_gamma)>=0.

Orientations of disjoint cycles are independent. Hence the complete group
sum is the product of these nonnegative factors and the nonnegative
transposition factors. It follows that

  per(C)>=M(X)>=1+E(C).

Apply (8.1) to X=C o bar(C). Squaring the positive lower bound gives

  delta(C)>=M(X)^2-[1+(M(X)-1)^2]
           =2(M(X)-1)>=2E(C).                      (8.2)

Thus delta(C)=0 forces E(C)=0, hence C=I. The converse is immediate.
The same proof is valid for any Hermitian unit-diagonal C with entries
bounded in modulus by one; PSD is needed only for the original domain.

8.3 Sharpness of coefficient 2.

For n>=2, let C=I except C[1,2]=z and C[2,1]=bar(z), with 0<|z|<=1.
This is a direct sum of a PSD 2-by-2 correlation block and identities.
There are no support cycles of length at least three, so the premise
holds. Direct expansion gives

  per(C)=1+|z|^2,
  per(C o bar(C))=1+|z|^4,
  delta(C)=2|z|^2=2E(C)=2(M(X)-1).

Consequently no larger universal coefficient can replace 2 in (8.2).
This proves sharpness of that estimate.

9. EXACT NONREAL EXAMPLE
-----------------------

Let i denote the imaginary unit and set

  C = [ 1     1/2   -i/2 ]
      [ 1/2   1      1/2 ]
      [ i/2   1/2    1   ].

Its leading principal minors are 1,3/4,1/4, so C is positive definite.
Its oriented triangle product is i/8. This excludes phase adjustment
to a nonnegative matrix, but its real part is zero, so R6 applies.

  E=3/4,
  M=per(C)=7/4,
  per(C o bar(C))=39/32,
  delta(C)=59/32>=2E=3/2.

This is the pair (C,bar(C)) for the original conjecture. Entrywise C o C
is different: its permanent is 37/32. Both C and bar(C) satisfy R6 and
fail exact phase balancing. This verifies the phase scope of R6 beyond exact phase balancing.

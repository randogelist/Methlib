1. DEFINITIONS AND EXACT QUANTIFIERS

Let n be any natural number. Let A,B : Fin n -> Fin n -> R satisfy
  for every i,j, A(i,j)>=0 and B(i,j)>=0.
Let S_n be the finite permutation group of Fin n. For sigma in S_n define
  a_sigma = product_i A(i,sigma(i)),
  b_sigma = product_i B(i,sigma(i)),
  P = sum_sigma a_sigma,
  Q = sum_sigma b_sigma,
  C = sum_sigma a_sigma*b_sigma,
  D = sum_sigma sum_(tau!=sigma) a_sigma*b_tau,
  Delta = P*Q-C.

Each row-product definition is the ordinary permanent convention. Mathlib's
native permanent uses product_i A(sigma(i),i); invariance under transpose
identifies the two finite sums exactly. The formal root uses native
Matrix.permanent and Matrix.hadamard.

Set (A circ B)(i,j)=A(i,j)*B(i,j). Then C=per(A circ B), P=per(A), Q=per(B).

The case n=0 is admitted by the finite-sum conventions: S_0 has one element,
the empty product is 1, P=Q=C=1 and D=Delta=0. The original source's n>=1
scope follows by specialization.

2. UNIVERSAL FINITE RESIDUAL IDENTITY

Distributivity over the two finite sums gives
  P*Q = sum_sigma sum_tau a_sigma*b_tau.

For each fixed sigma, split the tau sum into tau=sigma and tau!=sigma:
  sum_tau a_sigma*b_tau
    = a_sigma*b_sigma + sum_(tau!=sigma) a_sigma*b_tau.

Summing that equality in sigma gives
  P*Q = C+D,
and consequently
  per(A)*per(B)-per(A circ B)
    = sum_sigma sum_(tau!=sigma) a_sigma*b_tau.

This identity is algebraic and requires no entrywise sign assumption.

3. NONNEGATIVITY AND THE PERMANENT INEQUALITY

A finite product of nonnegative real numbers is nonnegative. Thus
  a_sigma>=0 and b_tau>=0
for every sigma,tau. Every term of D is nonnegative, so D>=0.

Using Section 2,
  Delta=D>=0,
hence
  per(A circ B)<=per(A)*per(B).

The proof is universal in n. No asymptotic passage, numerical observation,
spectral hypothesis, certificate search or unprovided witness is used.

4. VANISHING CROSS TERMS

A finite sum of nonnegative reals is zero exactly when every summand is zero.
Apply this fact twice to D:
  Delta=0
    iff for all sigma,tau with sigma!=tau, a_sigma*b_tau=0.       (E1)

This is an exact equality criterion, including zero-permanent cases.

5. ZERO-PERMANENT CASES

Since all a_sigma are nonnegative,
  P=0 iff a_sigma=0 for every sigma.
Similarly,
  Q=0 iff b_tau=0 for every tau.

Either condition makes every cross term zero and therefore makes Delta=0.
No normalization or division is used on these branches.

6. COMMON SINGLETON SUPPORT

Suppose Delta=0, P!=0 and Q!=0. Nonnegativity of P and Q implies P>0,Q>0.
There is a sigma with a_sigma>0 and a tau with b_tau>0, because the finite
sums P,Q are positive.

If sigma!=tau, their product is positive, contradicting (E1). Thus sigma=tau;
call the common permutation kappa. Then a_kappa>0 and b_kappa>0.

For any rho!=kappa, (E1) gives
  a_rho*b_kappa=0 and a_kappa*b_rho=0.
The positive factors imply a_rho=0 and b_rho=0.

Conversely, suppose there exists kappa such that
  a_kappa>0, b_kappa>0,
  and for every rho!=kappa, a_rho=b_rho=0.
For sigma!=tau, at least one of sigma,tau differs from kappa. The associated
factor is zero; therefore all cross terms vanish and Delta=0.

Combining Sections 5 and 6 gives the exact full classification:
  Delta=0
    iff P=0
      or Q=0
      or exists kappa in S_n:
           a_kappa>0 and b_kappa>0
           and for every rho!=kappa, a_rho=0 and b_rho=0.        (E2)

Equivalently, when P,Q>0, each matrix has a unique positive permutation
product, and the two positive permutations coincide.
This does not require either arbitrary nonnegative matrix to be diagonal.

7. NORMALIZED OVERLAP

Assume P>0 and Q>0. Define
  pi_A(sigma)=a_sigma/P,
  pi_B(sigma)=b_sigma/Q.
Then both functions are nonnegative and their finite sums equal 1.

Using distributivity of division over a finite sum and
  (a_sigma/P)*(b_sigma/Q)=(a_sigma*b_sigma)/(P*Q),
obtain
  per(A circ B)/(per(A)*per(B))
    = sum_sigma pi_A(sigma)*pi_B(sigma).                       (N1)

Subtracting this expression from 1 gives
  Delta/(P*Q)=1-sum_sigma pi_A(sigma)*pi_B(sigma).              (N2)

Since P*Q>0 and 0<=C<=P*Q, the normalized overlap belongs to [0,1].
Equality at 1 has exactly the common-singleton-support condition in (E2),
with the zero-permanent alternatives excluded by P,Q>0.

8. FINITE NONNEGATIVE WEIGHTED-GRAM SPECIALIZATION

Let alpha,beta be finite index sets. Let w:alpha->R and v:beta->R
be nonnegative. Let f:Fin n->alpha->R and g:Fin n->beta->R be
entrywise nonnegative. Define
  A(i,j)=sum_k w(k)*f(i,k)*f(j,k),
  B(i,j)=sum_l v(l)*g(i,l)*g(j,l).
Each summand is nonnegative, so every entry of A and B is nonnegative.
Section 3 therefore yields per(A circ B)<=per(A)*per(B).
This finite corollary requires no operator-domain or spectral bridge.
The Lean source asserts the entrywise sign and the permanent inequality;
it does not assert a separate positive-semidefiniteness theorem.

9. EXACT SCOPE BOUNDARY

The original AIM025 problem asks for all complex Hermitian positive
semidefinite matrices, without entrywise sign conditions.
The proof in Section 3 uses nonnegative permutation products. Hermitian
positive semidefiniteness alone does not imply that sign property.

Consequently this endpoint proves the stated nonnegative-entry subclass
in every dimension. It does not resolve the unrestricted AIM025 target.
No replacement of an unresolved full-target premise by an axiom is made.

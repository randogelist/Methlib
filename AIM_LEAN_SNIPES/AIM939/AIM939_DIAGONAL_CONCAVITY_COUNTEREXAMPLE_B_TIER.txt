ORIGINAL TARGET.

For 1<=p<=n and nonnegative coefficients a_0,...,a_p, not all zero, define

    sigma_0(x)=1,
    sigma_k(x)=sum_(i_1<...<i_k) x_(i_1)...x_(i_k),
    f_a(x)=sum_(k=0)^p a_k sigma_k(x),
    P_a(t)=f_a(t,...,t)=sum_(k=0)^p a_k binomial(n,k)t^k.

The diagonal-concavity assertion requires that concavity of P_a^(1/p) on
(0,infinity) imply concavity of f_a^(1/p) on (0,infinity)^n.

THEOREM AIM939_COUNTEREXAMPLE.

Set n=p=4 and

    (a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)=(1,2,3,5,1),
    f(x)=1+2sigma_1(x)+3sigma_2(x)+5sigma_3(x)+sigma_4(x),
    P(t)=1+8t+18t^2+20t^3+t^4.

Then all coefficients are strictly positive, P(t)=f(t,t,t,t), and

    (i)  P(t)^(1/4) is strictly concave for all t>0;
    (ii) f(x)^(1/4) is not concave on (0,infinity)^4.

Consequently, the original universally quantified diagonal-concavity
assertion is false. No additional real-rootedness premise is assumed or
disproved by this theorem.

==========================================================================
DEFINITIONS AND PREMISES
==========================================================================

All variables and coefficients are real. The notation u^(1/4), for u>0,
means the unique positive fourth root. The functions considered below are
positive on their stated domains. Concavity has the usual Jensen meaning:

    g((1-lambda)x+lambda y)
      >=(1-lambda)g(x)+lambda g(y)

for all points of the convex domain and every lambda in [0,1].

The elementary symmetric polynomials are the ordinary unnormalized
polynomials. In four variables,

    sigma_1=x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4,
    sigma_2=x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_1x_4+x_2x_3+x_2x_4+x_3x_4,
    sigma_3=x_1x_2x_3+x_1x_2x_4+x_1x_3x_4+x_2x_3x_4,
    sigma_4=x_1x_2x_3x_4.

No unknown existence premise, numerical approximation premise, semidefinite
certificate premise, or finite sampling premise is used in the derivation.

NUMBERED FULL DERIVATION

1. Admissible coefficients and positivity.

The vector (1,2,3,5,1) has five strictly positive entries. For every
x in (0,infinity)^4, each sigma_k(x) is nonnegative and f(x)>=1>0.
Likewise P(t)>=1>0 for every t>0. Therefore both fourth-root functions are
well-defined; the diagonal fourth root is twice continuously differentiable.

2. Exact diagonal restriction.

On the diagonal x=(t,t,t,t),

    sigma_1=4t,
    sigma_2=6t^2,
    sigma_3=4t^3,
    sigma_4=t^4.

It follows that f(t,t,t,t)=1+8t+18t^2+20t^3+t^4=P(t).

3. Global scalar concavity certificate.

Differentiation gives

    P'(t)=8+36t+60t^2+4t^3,
    P''(t)=36+120t+12t^2.

Exact polynomial multiplication yields

    4P(t)P''(t)-3P'(t)^2
      =-48-96t-288t^2-1248t^3-1056t^4
      =-48(1+2t+6t^2+26t^3+22t^4).              (3.1)

All terms in the parenthesis are nonnegative for t>0, and the constant
term is positive. Hence the expression in (3.1) is strictly negative
for every t>0, without a finite parameter restriction.

For g(t)=P(t)^(1/4), the chain rule gives

    g''(t)
      =[4P(t)P''(t)-3P'(t)^2]/[16P(t)^(7/4)]<0.  (3.2)

The denominator is positive by paragraph 1. A twice continuously
differentiable function with negative second derivative on an interval
has strictly decreasing first derivative. The mean value theorem then
gives the strict Jensen inequality between any two distinct points of
that interval. Therefore g is strictly concave on all of (0,infinity).

4. Three explicit interior points.

Define

    x_minus=(1/200, 397/100, 1/200, 1/200),
    x_mid  =(1/100, 4,       1/100, 1/100),
    x_plus =(3/200, 403/100, 3/200, 3/200).

Every coordinate is positive, and coordinatewise arithmetic gives

    x_mid=(x_minus+x_plus)/2.                   (4.1)

Equivalently, these are points on the line through x_mid with direction
v=(1,6,1,1) and steps plus or minus 1/200.

5. Exact values of the multivariate polynomial.

For x=(s,M,s,s), direct evaluation of the elementary symmetric
polynomials gives

    sigma_1=M+3s,
    sigma_2=3Ms+3s^2,
    sigma_3=3Ms^2+s^3,
    sigma_4=Ms^3.

Thus

    f(s,M,s,s)
      =1+6s+9s^2+5s^3+M(2+9s+15s^2+s^3).       (5.1)

For the three points in paragraph 4, M=197/50+6s. Substitution in
(5.1) gives the one-variable expression

    F(s)=222/25+(2673/50)s+(1221/10)s^2
           +(4947/50)s^3+6s^4.

Evaluation at s=1/200,1/100,3/200 proves

    f(x_minus)=7320291897/800000000,
    f(x_mid)  =9426909/1000000,
    f(x_plus) =7767765381/800000000.              (5.2)

These are exact rational equalities.

6. Rational fourth-root brackets.

Set D=10000000 and

    q_minus=17392403/D,
    q_mid  =17522350/D,
    q_plus =17652309/D.

All three numbers are positive. Exact integer arithmetic proves

    f(x_minus)-q_minus^4
      =11014638419023034580719/10^28>0,

    q_mid^4-f(x_mid)
      =19279346223978006250000/10^28>0,

    f(x_plus)-q_plus^4
      =6325176619132502946639/10^28>0.           (6.1)

The map r->r^4 is strictly increasing for r>=0. Therefore (6.1) implies

    q_minus<f(x_minus)^(1/4),
    f(x_mid)^(1/4)<q_mid,
    q_plus<f(x_plus)^(1/4).                     (6.2)

No floating-point approximation is used in (6.1) or (6.2).

7. Strict midpoint violation.

The rational bounds themselves satisfy

    (q_minus+q_plus)/2-q_mid
      =(17392403+17652309-2*17522350)/(2*10^7)
      =12/(2*10^7)
      =3/5000000>0.                             (7.1)

Combining (6.2) and (7.1) yields the quantitative inequality

    [f(x_minus)^(1/4)+f(x_plus)^(1/4)]/2
       -f(x_mid)^(1/4)
      >3/5000000>0.                              (7.2)

Equation (4.1) identifies x_mid as the midpoint of x_minus and x_plus.
Concavity would require the left side of (7.2) to be nonpositive.
Consequently f^(1/4) is not concave on the positive orthant.

8. Negation of the original universal assertion.

Paragraphs 1-3 establish every coefficient, positivity, exponent, and
global diagonal-concavity hypothesis of the n=p=4 instance. Paragraphs
4-7 establish failure of its full-concavity conclusion at admissible
interior points. Hence that instance is false, and the original assertion
quantified over every 1<=p<=n is false.

The source separately discusses a real-rootedness sufficient condition.
The counterexample does not assume that condition. In particular, the
normalized coefficient sequence fails a Newton log-concavity inequality:

    a_2^2=9<10=a_1a_3.

The full statement refuted here is the diagonal-concavity conjecture,
not a statement with a real-rootedness hypothesis added.
